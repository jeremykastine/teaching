# MATH 101 Unit-Test Specifications

**Owner:** Jeremy Kastine  
**Updated:** October 12, 2026  
**Status:** Standing generation specifications for the four unit tests and their practice/retake versions.  
**Textbook:** OpenStax *College Algebra 2e*.  
**Format:** Five main questions, 20 points each, 100 points total; designed for completion in 30 minutes.

## 1. Scope and instruction hierarchy

Use this file to generate an individual unit test, parallel practice/retake versions, or a requested batch. These are construction requirements, not a list from which to select five unrelated exercises.

1. A later instructor instruction controls.
2. The applicable question-family requirements below control mathematical content, subpart weights, and workload.
3. The common unit-test rules in this file control the five-question structure and timing.
4. [MATH 101 assessment specifications](final-exam-specifications.md) control presentation, calculator policy, identifiers, verification, and packaging wherever this file does not specify otherwise. Its ten-question/10-point architecture and final-only variant probabilities apply to cumulative assessments, not these unit tests.
5. Use [TSM assessment specifications](../tsm/specifications/TSM_Test_Specs.md) as a fallback for an otherwise unspecified preference, particularly fresh parallel versions, controlled variation, manageable arithmetic, and keeping multipart question units together. Do not import TSM-specific institutional reporting, final ordering, or conflicting older presentation rules.

**Assessment boundary:** All MATH 101 tests assess strictly algebraic content, including algebraic graphs and their interpretation. Word problems, contextual applications, modeling from descriptions or data, regression, financial scenarios, and optimization applications belong in classroom activities/labs and are not tested. This applies to application exercises embedded in core sections as well as optional application sections 2.3, 4.2, 4.3, 6.7, and 6.8. A prompt such as "find the minimum value of this quadratic" is algebraic and allowed; constructing a model or interpreting a physical quantity is not.

The four units retain the [scheduled boundaries](course-schedule.md). Prerequisite algebra may support later questions but must not become an extra unrelated question. Five questions sample the stated priorities; they do not certify exhaustive assessment of every textbook subsection on every form.

| Unit | Title | Core sections | Scheduled test point |

| 2 | Functions and Their Graphs | 3.1–3.7; 4.1 | Week 6 |

## 2. Common structure, scoring, and time controls

- Exactly **five numbered main question units**, each worth **20 points**. Lettered parts stay inside their main question. Each version totals **100 points**.
- Include each of the unit's five families exactly once. Family IDs below are internal identifiers, not fixed printed question numbers.
- Randomize the five main questions independently per version. Keep ordered solution stages and all subparts together. Do not reveal family labels, intended methods, or exceptional outcomes unless a method is expressly required below.
- Use the stated subpart weights. A single-part question has one 20-point response area. Award instructional partial credit for valid work; these tests have no supplied institutional binary-reporting requirement.
- Default to free response, with the explicitly specified multiple-choice recognition tasks. Each multiple-choice part must have exactly one defensible answer; use four plausible options unless the task is the four-way graph matching.
- Basic **four-function calculator only**; exact answers. No numerical logarithm approximations, statistical tools, graphing calculators, CAS, or advanced calculator keys.
- Use small integer coefficients, simple fractions, and clean radicals. Do not rely on a square-root or exponent key; extracting small perfect squares and leaving simplified irrational radicals are mathematical tasks.
- **Thirty minutes is the design limit for student work, not the instructor's solution time.** Target approximately 24–25 minutes for the questions, leaving 5–6 minutes for reading directions, recording answers, and checking. The per-family estimates below include reading and recording each answer.
- The unit's time budgets are workload ceilings, not permission to add parts. A five-question test must not hide several full tests inside multipart questions. Do not add bonus questions, extra independent drills, or exhaustive feature inventories.
- Use at most three top-level response parts per family, except the four short matches in U4-1. Related feature lists may share a part; each listed response must still have an explicit integer scoring allocation.
- Balance difficulty at the whole-test level. If an allowed variant increases work, simplify values elsewhere without removing required constructs. If the full form cannot reasonably fit 30 minutes, regenerate overloaded items within the blueprint rather than extending the time, omitting a family, or quietly changing scoring.
- Timing estimates are authoring estimates, not observed student data. After classroom use, adjust coefficient size, reading burden, and approved variants if needed; retain the five families and 100-point structure unless the instructor changes them.

### Parallel versions

Change numerical data, expressions, or graphs, not merely order or names. Preserve family, part weights, intended methods, domain complications, reading burden, and approximate solution length. For a specifically matched practice/actual or original/retake set, keep the same subtype in corresponding families with fresh values. Across independent batches, distribute allowed variants as evenly as practical, without an obvious answer pattern. Do not rotate into unlisted skills.

### Presentation and output

Follow the approved [algebra test format](../docs/algebra-test-format.md): combine questions in the left two-thirds of each printable page and aligned answer spaces in the right one-third. Students show all work on separate paper and record exact final answers in the right column. Omit a course-section field; keep timestamp IDs in America/New_York with batch suffixes.

Aim for one or two student pages. The concise instructor key uses one column per test page, preserving exact vertical response geometry; one-, two-, or three-page tests have one-, two-, or three-column keys respectively. Longer tests may require additional key pages. There is no separate student answer sheet and no requirement for equal student/key page counts.

Use open response areas with no answer baselines, solid rules between main questions, and light dotted/dashed rules only between parts. Put part points at the bottom of the response area; express each 20-point question's total through its part weights rather than repeating it in the heading. Generate both views from shared key-first geometry and omit answers entirely from student pages.

Default package: combined student test -> completely blank padding page only if the test length is odd -> concise instructor key. The key starts on an odd-numbered page. Render and inspect all pages, verify vertical matches and duplex padding, and keep supplied interpretation graphs with questions and blank student grids in the answer column. Include worked solutions or audits only when requested.

## 3. Unit 1 — Algebraic Expressions, Equations, and Inequalities

**Priority:** Reliable manipulation and equation solving that later function work assumes. Scientific-notation context, extensive standalone complex arithmetic, and routine signed-number drills are not separate main questions.

| ID | Required family | Response parts and points | Student time |
|---|---|---|---|
| U1-1 | Exponents and radicals | (a) Exponent-law simplification 10; (b) radical/rational-exponent simplification 10 | 4 min |
| U1-2 | Polynomial operations and factoring | (a) Expand/simplify 10; (b) factor completely 10 | 4 min |
| U1-3 | Rational-expression operation | Simplified expression 14; original excluded values 6, in one related task | 5 min |
| U1-4 | Equation solving | (a) Quadratic equation 10; (b) linear, rational, or one-radical equation 10 | 7 min |
| U1-5 | Inequality solving | Solution derivation 12; interval notation 8, in one related task | 4 min |
| **Total** | **Five families; 100 points** | | **24 min + 6 min allowance** |

### U1-1 — Exponents and radicals (1.1–1.3)

(a) Use one monomial quotient/product with two or three exponent-law decisions, including a negative exponent. Require a simplified answer with positive exponents. At most two variables, integer exponents of modest size, and coefficients simplifying to an integer or simple fraction. State variables are nonzero.

(b) Use one short numeric radical or rational-exponent expression requiring a perfect-square/cube extraction or rewrite followed by simplification. Do not combine nested radicals, long rationalization, and exponent arithmetic. If variables appear under an even radical, state positivity when needed or require the mathematically correct absolute value. Do not silently equate sqrt(x²) with x for unrestricted real x.

### U1-2 — Polynomial operations and factoring (1.4–1.5)

(a) Multiply two linear binomials and combine like terms, or distribute one monomial over a three-term polynomial and combine with at most one additional like term. Require actual distribution; avoid a special-product shortcut in the binomial form.

(b) Factor a degree-2 polynomial completely. Use a GCF followed by a monic trinomial, a primitive nonmonic trinomial with a small factor-pair search, or a difference of squares with a GCF. At most two factoring stages, no long grouping/cube-factor exercise. Do not use identical factors or coefficients in (a) and (b).

### U1-3 — Rational expressions (1.6)

One operation only: simplify a quotient, multiply/divide two rational expressions, or add/subtract two with related denominators. Require at least one polynomial factorization and preserve restrictions from the original expression.

- Factor only through degree 2; at most two components need factoring.
- Multiplication/division: at most two polynomial factor cancellations. For division, the original divisor must also be defined and nonzero; include its zero restrictions.
- Addition/subtraction: a manageable LCD with at most three distinct linear factors; final reduction may require at most one obvious common factor.
- Do not stack a long LCD calculation and a difficult final nonmonic factorization.
- The key lists all original exclusions, even those hidden by cancellation.

### U1-4 — Equations (2.2, 2.4–2.6)

(a) One quadratic with no lengthy preliminary expansion. Rotate factoring, square-root form, and the quadratic formula across independent forms. The formula subtype has a manageable nonsquare discriminant; real irrational roots are the default. A negative discriminant is allowed in an independent form to assess the limited complex-number scope, with a small simplified imaginary result. Do not replace this with a standalone complex-number arithmetic drill.

(b) Choose one short equation: a linear equation with numerical fractions and at most one distribution; a rational equation becoming linear after at most one denominator factorization; or a one-radical equation becoming a small factorable quadratic. For rational/radical variants require original restrictions/checks and rejection of invalid candidates. No two-radical equation, extra quadratic substitution, or long rational equation. Do not combine the upper-end quadratic-formula subtype in (a) with the upper-end radical subtype in (b); keep the combined seven-minute ceiling.

Checking is included in the 10 points for the applicable equation, not an extra task outside the family.

### U1-5 — Inequalities (2.7)

Use one compound linear inequality or one absolute-value inequality. Require a union or intersection of intervals and correct endpoint inclusion. At most one distribution or one isolation step before the main inequality work. Include sign reversal when naturally required across independent forms; do not make every version exceptional. No contextual tolerance problem and no extra equation to solve. A number-line sketch may replace the interval-notation response only if requested.

**Supporting coverage:** Coordinate and intercept ideas from 2.1 recur directly in Units 2–4; do not add a sixth coordinate-geometry question here. Standalone distance/midpoint computations, every equation subtype, and every factoring pattern need not appear on each short form.

## 4. Unit 2 — Functions and Their Graphs

**Priority:** Function language and structure, with the final's composition and transformation tasks explicitly represented.

| ID | Required family | Response parts and points | Student time |
|---|---|---|---|
| U2-1 | Evaluate a function; domain and range | (a) Value 6; (b) domain 7; (c) range 7 | 4 min |
| U2-2 | Read graph behavior and average rate | (a) Average rate 6; (b) monotonic intervals 8; (c) specified extrema 6 | 5 min |
| U2-3 | Composition and decomposition | (a) Recognize inside/outside functions 8; (b) form/evaluate a composition 12 | 5 min |
| U2-4 | Transformations from graphs | One multiple-choice transformation recognition 20 | 4 min |
| U2-5 | Linear function and its inverse | (a) Line formula 10; (b) inverse formula 6 and inverse domain/range 4 | 6 min |
| **Total** | **Five families; 100 points** | | **24 min + 6 min allowance** |

### U2-1 — Function notation, domain, range (3.1–3.2, 3.6)

Give one simple function and one input. Use a transformed absolute-value function, a transformed square root, or a reciprocal of a shifted linear expression. At most two transformations and values supporting easy exact evaluation. Domain and range must be derivable from a basic function and transformations without calculus or solving a general rational inequality. State any domain restriction in the prompt. Absolute-value forms are the default in the first independent batch so 3.6 receives direct assessment.

### U2-2 — Rates and graph behavior (3.3)

Give an exact, clearly labeled graph on a bounded interval, with at most four line segments and sufficient coordinates to compute one average rate. Include at least one increasing and one decreasing interval; a short constant segment is allowed.

(a) Compute average rate between two specified inputs.
(b) State increasing/decreasing intervals (4 points each); omit an additional constant-interval response unless it replaces, rather than adds to, one requested category.
(c) Identify either local extrema or absolute extrema, explicitly stated, with at most two requested points (3 points each). Make open/closed endpoints clear; avoid an unattained bound described as an attained extremum. No new drawing or long table construction.

### U2-3 — Composition (3.4)

(a) Match the final's recognition style: give h(x) and four proposed pairs f,g; identify the pair with h = f∘g. Use plausible reversed-order/misplaced-constant distractors and only one valid pair.

(b) With a separate simple pair of functions, form one specified composition (8 points) and evaluate it at one clean input (4 points). An affine function composed with a quadratic is preferred; at most one binomial expansion. No pair of complicated rational compositions, two full opposite-order expansions, or long composition-domain analysis. Domain understanding is already assessed in U2-1.

### U2-4 — Transformations (3.5)

Use the final's approved form: original y=f(x) and transformed y=g(x) on the same coordinate plane, no formula for the original, and four expressions for g in terms of f. Use an asymmetric original graph with clearly mapped points.

Allow exactly one transformation family per question:
- horizontal/vertical translations, which may be combined;
- vertical scaling and/or x-axis reflection;
- horizontal scaling and/or y-axis reflection.

Do not combine scaling/reflection families with one another or with translations. Use clean scale factors. Verify that the graph distinguishes all choices; do not add an independent graph-sketch task.

### U2-5 — Linear functions and inverses (3.7, 4.1)

(a) Give two integer-coordinate points with distinct x-values and a manageable nonzero slope. Require the affine function formula, including slope and intercept. Neither point should simply provide the entire formula by being a trivial intercept pair.

(b) Find the inverse of that same function and state its domain and range (2 points each). Use domain R by default; a bounded-domain variant is allowed only if mapping endpoints keeps the full question within six minutes. Do not use a horizontal line or call the reciprocal an inverse. The key checks both composition directions internally; a full two-direction written verification is not an added student part.

## 5. Unit 3 — Polynomial, Rational, and Radical Functions

**Priority:** The final's polynomial construction, complete zeros, rational behavior, and radical analysis. Keep quadratic features brief so these four strands fit the 30-minute test.

| ID | Required family | Response parts and points | Student time |
|---|---|---|---|
| U3-1 | Quadratic function features | (a) Vertex/extreme value 10; (b) zeros 10 | 3 min |
| U3-2 | Construct a polynomial from a graph | Factored polynomial 16; end behavior 4 | 4 min |
| U3-3 | Find all zeros of a cubic | (a) RRT candidates/test 5; (b) long division 7; (c) quadratic-formula zeros 8 | 7 min |
| U3-4 | Rational-function behavior | (a) Domain 4; (b) discontinuity/asymptote features 10; (c) intercepts 6 | 5 min |
| U3-5 | Radical function analysis | (a) Domain/range 8; (b) intercepts 6; (c) end behavior 6 | 5 min |
| **Total** | **Five families; 100 points** | | **24 min + 6 min allowance** |

### U3-1 — Quadratic features (5.1)

Give vertex form a(x-h)²+k with small integers and a nonzero. Ask for vertex (6 points) and minimum/maximum value with its type (4 points), then the zeros (10 points). Choose zeros obtainable directly by square roots, preferably integers. This is algebraic function analysis, not an optimization application. Do not add completing the square, a graph sketch, range, and intercepts to this question.

### U3-2 — Polynomial construction and behavior (5.2–5.3)

Use the final's graph-to-polynomial task, restricted to degree 3 or 4 for unit timing. All real zeros and their multiplicities must be unambiguous; provide a labeled nonzero-value point to determine the leading constant. All factors are real linear factors, with at least one repeated zero, and a is an integer or simple fraction.

Require the factored polynomial (zeros/multiplicities 10 points; correct leading constant 6) and both directions of end behavior (2 points each). Do not require expanding the resulting polynomial or estimating extrema numerically. Degree and leading sign support the end-behavior response; do not add them as unrelated extra exercises.

### U3-3 — Polynomial zeros with required methods (5.4–5.5)

Retain the final's method sequence but use **degree 3 only**: one rational real zero and two real irrational zeros.

- Primitive integer coefficients; at most eight distinct Rational Root Theorem candidates after deduplication.
- Ask for all candidates (3 points), but supply a subset of at most two to test; identifying the rational zero earns 2 points. Never require testing the whole candidate list.
- Require **one polynomial long division** by the confirmed linear factor (7 points), with a small integer-coefficient quadratic quotient.
- Require the **quadratic formula** for the remaining zeros (8 points); discriminant positive and nonsquare, with easy simplification.
- Do not accept synthetic division as a substitute when long division is expressly required.
- No second division, missing rational roots, complex final roots, long factor search, or additional polynomial-construction part.
- Anchor workload: (x-1)(x²+2x-1) expands to x³+x²-3x+1; RRT candidates ±1, and remaining zeros -1±sqrt(2). This is an authoring model, not a question to reuse unchanged.

The full method sequence, including recording all three zeros, must fit approximately seven minutes.

### U3-4 — Rational behavior (5.6)

Select one of the final's two approved families, with explicit unit workload limits.

**Form A (default):** Fully factored degree-at-most-2 numerator and denominator; one common linear factor creates a hole, and one remaining denominator factor creates a vertical asymptote. Choose a simple horizontal asymptote and clean intercepts. Score domain 4; hole coordinate 4, vertical asymptote 3, horizontal asymptote 3; x- and y-intercepts 3 each. At most one hole and one vertical asymptote.

**Form B:** Unfactored quadratic numerator over a linear denominator, no cancellation/hole, and clean numerator roots. Score domain 4; polynomial division/slant asymptote 7 and vertical asymptote 3; x- and y-intercepts 3 each. No difficult factoring or extra horizontal-asymptote task. Pair this variant with particularly simple U3-1/U3-2 values so the full test stays within its timing budget.

State nonexistent intercepts explicitly when appropriate. Original restrictions survive cancellation. Do not ask for a full rational graph sketch, a sign chart, or a second rational function.

### U3-5 — Radical analysis (5.7)

Give f(x)=a sqrt(x-h)+k, with small a≠0 and values chosen for simple exact intercepts. Domain and range receive 4 points each; x- and y-intercepts 3 each; the unbounded-direction behavior 4 and behavior at the finite domain endpoint 2. An intercept may fail to exist, but the answer must follow from the formula.

Do not describe a limit through inputs outside the domain. No numerical plotting table or extra radical equation. Inverse-function understanding from 3.7 is reinforced by an optional replacement for part (b): find the inverse formula (4 points) and its domain (2 points), retaining the same 6-point weight and five-minute workload. For this replacement, explicitly ask for the inverse and use a simple nonzero a; preserve the original range restriction in the inverse domain. Use the same variant across a matched set, and default to intercepts to align with the final.

**Coverage boundary:** Nonreal zeros, Descartes' Rule, IVT, and other chapter terminology remain instructional material; do not silently add standalone questions for them. The five families deliberately prioritize the final's selected constructs. This does not remove those sections from instruction.

## 6. Unit 4 — Exponential and Logarithmic Functions; Systems

**Priority:** The final's exponential graph matching, logarithmic analysis/properties, and equation solving, plus the scheduled systems material. Keep systems and inequalities within one bounded family.

| ID | Required family | Response parts and points | Student time |
|---|---|---|---|
| U4-1 | Exponential graph matching | Four matches, 5 points each | 3 min |
| U4-2 | Transformed logarithmic function | (a) Domain/range 8; (b) asymptote/intercept 6; (c) end behavior 6 | 4 min |
| U4-3 | Logarithmic properties | Two short exact evaluations, 10 points each | 4 min |
| U4-4 | Exponential/logarithmic equations | (a) Exponential equation 8; (b) logarithmic equation 12 | 6 min |
| U4-5 | Linear/nonlinear systems and inequality membership | (a) Linear system 6; (b) nonlinear system 10; (c) related region-membership check 4 | 7 min |
| **Total** | **Five families; 100 points** | | **24 min + 6 min allowance** |

### U4-1 — Exponential graphs (6.1–6.2)

Exactly the final's graph-matching style: four functions a b^x and four graphs on a single plane, no shifts, four 5-point matches. Use positive a and valid positive bases b≠1, growth and decay, and at least one distinction requiring intercept or relative-rate reasoning beyond simply growth versus decay.

All curves have the same solid-line style and labels A–D at both far edges. Choose a legible window and separate labels; no tangency/crossing ambiguity. No extra computations or contextual growth models.

### U4-2 — Logarithmic analysis (6.3–6.4)

One transformed log function with an affine argument, base positive and unequal to 1, and clean values. Ask for domain/range (4 each), vertical asymptote/x-intercept (3 each), and both relevant end behaviors (3 each). Make the x-intercept a familiar exact power of the base. No numerical log approximations, quadratic-argument domain problem, or graph sketch.

### U4-3 — Properties (6.5)

Two independent short exact evaluations. Each genuinely uses one or two product, quotient, power, or change-of-base properties; individual logs should not already be immediately evaluable. At least one part combines terms before evaluation. Prefer common-log decimal patterns combining to powers of 10; exact change-of-base values are allowed.

Avoid expansion into many terms, arbitrary ln values needing a scientific calculator, and apparent identities with incompatible domains. State positive-variable assumptions if variables are used. Do not add an equation-solving part to this family.

### U4-4 — Equations (6.6)

(a) Different-looking bases rewriting to a common base, then a linear equation in x; 8 points. Use a level comparable to 8^(x-1)=4^(x+1), without logs or long exponent expansion.

(b) Exactly one log property before conversion to exponential form; 12 points including domain checks. Choose product→linear, quotient→linear, or product→small factorable quadratic with one rejected candidate. Match the final's approved subtypes, but keep the quadratic variant short and use simpler system values in U4-5 when it appears.

Every form contains both equation categories; do not import the final's 25% radical/exponential/logarithmic/system lottery into a unit test. Radical equations belong in Unit 1; systems have their own Unit 4 family. No decimal approximation.

### U4-5 — Systems and nonlinear-inequality membership (7.1, 7.3)

(a) A short 2×2 linear system with a unique integer ordered pair. Arrange one equation for substitution or permit elimination without large multipliers. Six points; no lengthy preliminary rearrangement.

(b) A line-plus-parabola or line-plus-circle system, with two intersections and manageable substitution leading to a factorable quadratic. Ten points; use nonconstant line equations, simple integer coordinates, and a circle in a simple form. Balance the two nonlinear subtypes across independent batches. No quartic, two general conics, or irrational intersection arithmetic.

(c) Reuse the two boundaries from (b) as a system of inequalities and give one specified point away from the boundaries. Ask whether it is in the common solution region and require its substitution comparisons. Four points: correct comparisons 2 and membership conclusion 2. Include one strict or inclusive symbol naturally; do not ask students to sketch/shade the entire region or solve another system. The given point should not be one of the already found intersections.

The three parts are intentionally unequal in depth. The complete family must stay within seven minutes; a six-point linear system must not have the workload of a full separate unit-test problem.

## 7. Internal verification before generating a finished test

Perform these checks for every version; expose an audit only when requested.

1. Exactly five families from the requested unit, one each; each main question 20 points, all integer subweights explicit and sum to 100.
2. Correct sections and algebra-only scope. No word problems, modeling/application interpretation, regression, or lab scenario slips into a question.
3. Independently solve every problem. Verify original domains, canceled restrictions, divisor-zero exclusions, extraneous candidates, complex-root notation where allowed, and exact simplifications.
4. Check the family-specific methods, degree limits, factorization burden, number of transformations, and subtype restrictions. Do not silently relax a constraint to use a convenient example.
5. Estimate student time for the whole form from reading, mathematical decisions, written work, and transferring answers. Verify the 24–25-minute question target plus remaining allowance within 30 minutes. Check all multipart workloads, especially U1-4, U3-3/U3-4, and U4-5. Instructor speed alone is not evidence.
6. For graph items, verify the underlying formulas/coordinates, plotted zeros/multiplicities, labeled points, transformed coordinates, scales, and unique answer choices. Never use a graph image that merely suggests the required data.
7. For matched forms, preserve constructs, subtypes, scoring, method requirements, supports, reading demand, and approximate difficulty; vary numerical content genuinely.
8. Check randomization keeps related parts together and response areas/key columns follow printed order. Use timestamp identifiers consistently; no Version A/B.
9. For actual PDFs, render every page and inspect legibility, wrapping, graph labels, and page breaks. Confirm combined questions/answers with no deliberate work areas, one key column per student page and exact vertical matches, integer part points only in response areas, correct separators, no answer baselines, no hidden answers, and odd-page section starts.
10. Deliver only the requested tests/versions and requested format. Do not automatically add finals, homework, worked-solution documents, or audit reports.
