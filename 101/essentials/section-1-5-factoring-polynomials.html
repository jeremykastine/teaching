Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. What should you look for first when factoring?
  2. What reverses factoring?
  3. State the difference-of-squares identity.
  4. For x2+bx+c, what must the two constant factors do?
  5. When is a polynomial completely factored over the integers?
Check short-answer key
  1. A greatest common factor.
  2. Multiplication or distribution.
  3. a2−b2=(a−b)(a+b).
  4. Multiply to c and add to b.
  5. Its remaining nonconstant factors cannot be factored further with integer coefficients.

Example problems

Example 1

Factor 3x3−12x.

Show solution
  1. Remove the GCF: 3x(x2−4).
  2. Factor the difference of squares: 3x(x−2)(x+2).

Example 2

Factor 6x2+x−2.

Show solution
  1. ac = −12; 4 and −3 have sum 1.
  2. Split the middle term: 6x2+4x−3x−2.
  3. Group as 2x(3x+2)−(3x+2).
  4. Result: (2x − 1)(3x + 2).
