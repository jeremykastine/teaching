Answer the questions from memory on paper. Then check the answer key. Attempt each example before opening its solution.

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. How is the imaginary unit i defined?
  2. For a positive real a, what is −a?
  3. What is standard complex form?
  4. In a + bi, what are the real and imaginary parts?
  5. How does a principal square root differ from solving a squared equation?
Check short-answer key
  1. i2=−1.
  2. ia, the principal square root.
  3. a + bi with real a and b.
  4. The real part is a; the imaginary part is b.
  5. A principal root gives one value; the equation can have both positive and negative roots.

Example problems

Example 1

Write −20 in standard form and identify its parts.

Show solution
  1. −20=i4·5=2i5.
  2. Its real part is 0 and its imaginary part is 25.

Example 2

Evaluate −49, then solve x2=−49.

Show solution
  1. The principal root is 7i.
  2. The equation has both solutions x = 7i and x = −7i.
  3. The ± belongs to the equation's solution set.
