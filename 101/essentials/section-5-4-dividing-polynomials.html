Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. What identity relates dividend, divisor, quotient, and remainder?
  2. How must the remainder's degree compare with the divisor's?
  3. Why insert zero coefficients for missing powers?
  4. What divisor form is used for standard synthetic division?
  5. What does zero remainder tell you?
Check short-answer key
  1. Dividend = divisor × quotient + remainder.
  2. It must be smaller, unless the remainder is zero.
  3. To keep powers correctly aligned during division.
  4. x − c.
  5. The divisor is a factor of the dividend.

Example problems

Example 1

Divide x2+3x+2 by x + 1.

Show solution
  1. The first quotient term is x; subtract x(x + 1) to leave 2x + 2.
  2. The next term is 2; subtract 2(x + 1) to leave 0.
  3. Quotient: x + 2; remainder: 0.

Example 2

Divide x2+1 by x − 1.

Show solution
  1. Include the missing 0x term.
  2. Subtract x(x − 1), leaving x + 1; then subtract 1(x − 1), leaving 2.
  3. Quotient x + 1 and remainder 2; (x2+1)/(x−1)=x+1+2/(x−1), x ≠ 1.
