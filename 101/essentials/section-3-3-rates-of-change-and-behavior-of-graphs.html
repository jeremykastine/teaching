Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. When is a function increasing on an interval?
  2. When is it decreasing?
  3. What is a local minimum?
  4. How is an absolute minimum different?
  5. Does an open endpoint attain a maximum or minimum?
Check short-answer key
  1. Its y-values rise as x increases through that interval.
  2. Its y-values fall as x increases.
  3. An attained height no greater than nearby heights.
  4. It is the smallest attained height on the entire graph.
  5. No; its excluded point cannot be an attained extremum.

Example problems

Example 1

Find intervals of increase and decrease, interior local extrema, and absolute extrema, when they exist.

Show solution
  1. Moving left to right, the graph decreases to (−2, −1), increases to (0, 2), decreases to (2, −2), then increases to (3, 1).
  2. Decreasing intervals: (−4, −2) and (0, 2). Increasing intervals: (−2, 0) and (2, 3).
  3. Local minima are (−2, −1) and (2, −2); the interior local maximum is (0, 2). The absolute minimum is −2 at x = 2; the absolute maximum is 3 at x = −4.

Example 2

Find intervals of increase and decrease, interior local extrema, and absolute extrema, when they exist.

Show solution
  1. The graph rises from the filled point (−2, −1) toward the open point (2, 3).
  2. It is increasing on (−2, 2).
  3. The absolute minimum is −1 at x = −2. There is no absolute maximum because the upper endpoint is excluded and no point has the greatest height.
