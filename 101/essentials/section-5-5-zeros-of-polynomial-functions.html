Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. State the Factor Theorem.
  2. State the Remainder Theorem.
  3. What rational-zero candidates are possible for an integer-coefficient polynomial?
  4. Is every rational-zero candidate an actual zero?
  5. How does multiplicity affect zero counting?
Check short-answer key
  1. x − c is a factor of f(x) exactly when f(c) = 0.
  2. Division by x − c has remainder f(c).
  3. Ratios ±p/q, where p divides the constant and q divides the leading coefficient.
  4. No; it must be tested.
  5. Repeated roots count according to their multiplicity.

Example problems

Example 1

Find the real zeros of x3−2x2−x+2.

Show solution
  1. Group: x2(x−2)−1(x−2)=(x−2)(x2−1).
  2. Factor completely: (x − 2)(x − 1)(x + 1).
  3. The zeros are 2, 1, and −1.

Example 2

List rational candidates for 2x2−3x+1, then find its zeros.

Show solution
  1. Candidates: ±1 and ±1/2.
  2. Testing gives f(1) = 0; factoring gives (2x − 1)(x − 1).
  3. The actual zeros are 1/2 and 1.
