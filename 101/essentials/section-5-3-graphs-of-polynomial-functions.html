5.3: Graphs of Polynomial Functions · Essentials

Answer the questions from memory on paper. Then check the answer key. Attempt each example before opening its solution.

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. What is a zero of a polynomial function?
  2. How does odd multiplicity affect a zero's graph?
  3. How does even multiplicity affect it?
  4. What does the Intermediate Value Theorem guarantee for opposite-sign endpoint values?
  5. Can a polynomial graph have a break or corner?
Check short-answer key
  1. An input where its output equals zero.
  2. The graph crosses the x-axis.
  3. The graph touches the x-axis without changing sign there.
  4. At least one zero between them for a continuous function.
  5. No; polynomial graphs are continuous and smooth.

Example problems

Example 1

Analyze y=(x+2)2(x−1).

Show solution
  1. Zeros are −2 of multiplicity 2 and 1 of multiplicity 1.
  2. The graph touches at −2 and crosses at 1.
  3. The positive cubic leading term gives left end down, right end up; the y-intercept is −4.

Example 2

A polynomial has f(1) = −3 and f(2) = 4. What can be concluded?

Show solution
  1. A polynomial is continuous on [1, 2].
  2. Because 0 lies between −3 and 4, at least one c in (1, 2) has f(c) = 0.
  3. The information does not guarantee exactly one zero.
