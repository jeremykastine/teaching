Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. What determines a rational function's domain?
  2. What does a canceled denominator factor typically produce?
  3. What produces a vertical asymptote?
  4. For equal numerator and denominator degrees, what is the horizontal asymptote?
  5. May a graph cross a horizontal asymptote?
Check short-answer key
  1. Its original denominator must be nonzero.
  2. A hole at the excluded input, if the simplified denominator is nonzero there.
  3. A denominator zero remaining after common factors are canceled.
  4. The ratio of leading coefficients.
  5. Yes.

Example problems

Example 1

Analyze y=(x2−1)/(x−1).

Show solution
  1. Require x ≠ 1.
  2. Cancel x − 1 to get y = x + 1 on that domain.
  3. The graph is that line with a hole at (1, 2), and has no vertical asymptote.

Example 2

Find domain and asymptotes of y=(2x+1)/(x−3).

Show solution
  1. Domain: all real x except 3.
  2. The uncanceled denominator zero gives vertical asymptote x = 3.
  3. Equal degrees give horizontal asymptote y = 2.
