2.4: Complex Numbers

The imaginary unit and standard form

The imaginary unit satisfies i² = −1. For a positive real number a, √(−a) = i√a. A complex number has the form a + bi; a is its real part and b is its imaginary part. This course limits Section 2.4 to these ideas.

Example 1 · Identify the parts

Write √(−9) and 5 + √(−16) in standard form and identify their real and imaginary parts.

√(−9) = 3i = 0 + 3i: real part 0, imaginary part 3. The second number is 5 + 4i: real part 5, imaginary part 4.

Try It 1 · Standard form

Write √(−24) in standard form. Identify its real and imaginary parts.

√(−24) = i√(4·6) = 2i√6 = 0 + (2√6)i; real part 0 and imaginary part 2√6.

Additional practice · Nonperfect squares

Simplify √(−12), −√(−18), and 2 + √(−32).

√(−12) = i√(4·3) = 2i√3. Next, −√(−18) = −3i√2. Finally, 2 + √(−32) = 2 + 4i√2.

Additional practice · A root versus an equation

Evaluate √(−36). Then solve x² = −36. Explain why the answers differ.

The principal square root is √(−36) = 6i. The equation has two solutions, x = ±6i, because both (6i)² and (−6i)² equal −36.
