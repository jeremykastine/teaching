Excel Competency Checks

  • Check 1 · Chapter 2

    Match Count, Mean, Median, and Mode

    Create 50–100 observations of your own to match generated summary statistics.

  • Check 2 · Chapter 2

    Match Mean, Range, and Spread

    Create ten observations to match a generated mean, range, and standard deviation or variance, showing the spread calculation step by step.

  • Check 3 · Chapters 1–2

    Build a frequency table and bar chart

    Count categories, calculate relative frequencies, and connect a chart to the results.

  • Check 4 · Chapter 2

    Create data with specified statistical properties

    Adjust observations to match varying combinations of mean, median, extremes, and standard deviation.

  • Check 5 · Chapter 2

    Calculate sample standard deviation two ways

    Show deviations and squared deviations, then verify against the built-in function.

  • Check 6 · Chapter 3

    Build a two-way table and compare probabilities

    Summarize paired categorical data and choose the correct probability denominators.

  • Check 7 · Chapters 3–4

    Simulate random trials and the law of large numbers

    Generate 1,000 trials and compare results from 10, 100, and 1,000 observations.

  • Check 8 · Chapter 6

    Compare probabilities in a normal model

    Link three probability calculations to shared inputs and verify that they sum to one.

  • Check 9 · Chapters 7–8

    Compare sample sizes and confidence intervals

    Fill a comparison table for standard errors, margins of error, and interval endpoints.

How to prepare

  • Open the page for the check and practice its complete workflow in Excel until you can build it efficiently from a fresh worksheet with new data. Relevant Google Sheets differences appear in parentheses.
  • A prepared demonstration should take about five minutes. The detailed directions are for learning in advance; practice itself may take longer.
  • Every check has a Regenerate problem button that changes the data, parameters, or requirements within the same skill. Check 1 randomly alternates between one and two modes. Check 2 randomly chooses a population or sample standard deviation or variance. Checks 3–9 also offer a practice variation selector. Complete one generated task and its listed explanation or follow-up per attempt.
  • For a live check, open the skill page, regenerate a fresh problem in front of the instructor, and use that version for the entire attempt. On Checks 3–9, Live check view hides practice answers and worked examples. Demonstrate the work in Excel or Sheets; these pages do not submit or grade a workbook.
  • Copy data directly from the generated task when provided. On Checks 3–9, use Copy version link to save or share the exact requirements and data. Refreshing that link keeps the same task; press Regenerate for a new one. Random spreadsheet trial results in Check 7 will still vary on recalculation.
  • For Checks 1–2, follow the help link for setup and reasoning. For Checks 3–9, expand the worked directions to learn the method. Their fixed example is separate from the generated assignment. After practice, use the self-check to review your results. No practice-answer reveal is needed during the live demonstration.
  • Use formulas linked to the data and inputs. Be able to show the formulas, fill them across or down, explain fixed versus changing cell references, and explain the result. A page of typed final answers does not demonstrate these skills.
  • Use clear labels and readable number formats. Keep full precision in calculations. These examples assume English function names and comma argument separators; regional settings may use semicolons.

Course alignment: sampling and descriptive statistics, probability and expected value, normal models, sampling distributions, and confidence intervals. The random-trial experiment illustrates the law of large numbers.
