Excel Competency Checks

  • Check 1 · Chapter 2

    Match Count, Mean, Median, and Mode

    Create 20–30 observations of your own to match generated summary statistics.

  • Check 2 · Chapter 2

    Match Mean, Range, and Spread

    Create ten observations to match a generated mean, range, and standard deviation or variance, showing the spread calculation step by step.

  • Check 3 · Chapter 4

    Compare Risk and Reward

    Use one live calculation table for two probability distributions, record both results, and orally compare expected net gain and short-term variability.

  • Check 4 · Chapter 4

    Compare Binomial Theory and Simulation

    Build a probability triangle and compare its final row with the results of 100 simulated experiments.

How to prepare

  • Open the page for the check and practice its complete workflow in Excel until you can build it efficiently from a fresh worksheet with new data. Relevant Google Sheets differences appear in parentheses.
  • A prepared demonstration should take about five minutes. The detailed directions are for learning in advance; practice itself may take longer.
  • Every check has a Regenerate problem button that changes the data, parameters, or requirements within the same skill. Check 1 randomly alternates between one and two modes. Check 2 randomly chooses a population or sample standard deviation or variance. Check 4 supplies a probability of success and a number of trials for a 100-experiment simulation. Use one generated task throughout your attempt.
  • For a live check, open the skill page, regenerate a fresh problem in front of the instructor, and use that version for the entire attempt. Demonstrate the work in Excel or Sheets; these pages do not submit or grade a workbook.
  • Follow the instructions link on each check page to learn the worksheet setup and practice the formulas.
  • Use labeled formulas linked to the data and inputs. Be able to fill formulas across or down and show how the results update. For Check 3, record both games’ results in a separate comparison area. For Check 4, keep both charts linked to the probability and simulation tables.
  • Use clear labels and readable number formats. Keep full precision in calculations. These examples assume English function names and comma argument separators; regional settings may use semicolons.

Course alignment includes descriptive statistics, expected value and standard deviation, and the binomial distribution.
