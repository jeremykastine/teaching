      Learn the workflow and see a worked example

      What to learn

      Build a normal-probability model with separate input cells. Calculate the probabilities below, between, and above two cutoffs, then change an input and interpret the updated results.

      Practice example

      1. Put the labels Mean, Standard deviation, Lower cutoff, and Upper cutoff in A2:A5. Put 100, 15, 85, and 115 in B2:B5. These describe a normally distributed measurement; the standard deviation must be positive and the lower cutoff must be below the upper cutoff.
      2. In D2:D7 label the outputs Cumulative at lower cutoff, Cumulative at upper cutoff, Below lower cutoff, Between cutoffs, Above upper cutoff, and Total probability.
      3. In E2 enter =NORM.DIST(B4,$B$2,$B$3,TRUE); in E3 enter =NORM.DIST(B5,$B$2,$B$3,TRUE). TRUE requests cumulative probability, the area to the left of the cutoff.
      4. In E4 enter =E2, in E5 enter =E3-E2, and in E6 enter =1-E3. In E7 enter =SUM(E4:E6). The three disjoint regions cover the entire distribution.
      5. Format E4:E7 as percentages. Expect approximately 15.87%, 68.27%, 15.87%, and 100%. Keep full precision in the formulas.
      6. Increase B3 from 15 to 30, leaving both cutoffs fixed. The middle probability drops to about 38.29%, and each outer probability increases to about 30.85%. Explain why a wider distribution puts less probability inside this fixed interval.

      (Google Sheets: use =NORMDIST(B4,$B$2,$B$3,TRUE) and the corresponding upper-cutoff formula. The remaining formulas are unchanged.)

      During your check

      Build the linked calculations for supplied parameters and cutoffs, verify the probability total, and demonstrate a requested input change. Briefly explain the affected region or regions.

      Variations to prepare for

      • Different means, positive standard deviations, or asymmetric cutoffs.
      • Change the mean while holding the cutoffs fixed, or move one cutoff while keeping the distribution fixed.
      • Report the probability outside the interval by adding the two tails, or the expected count inside the interval among a supplied number of independent observations using count × probability.

      Be ready to explain

      Why the middle region requires subtraction and the upper tail requires a complement. FALSE returns a density hnine, which is not the requested area. For a continuous normal variable, including or excluding a single endpoint does not change the probability.
