      Learn the workflow and see a worked example

      What to learn

      Show the calculation of a sample standard deviation in visible spreadsheet steps. Use the same data with Excel’s built-in function and verify that the two methods agree.

      Practice example

      Put the heading Observation in A1 and the values 2, 4, 4, 6, 6, 8 in A2:A7. Put Deviation in B1 and Squared deviation in C1.

      1. Build a summary in E2:F8. In E2:E8 put the labels Mean, Count, Squared-deviation sum, Sample variance, Sample standard deviation, Built-in standard deviation, and Difference.
      2. In F2 enter =AVERAGE(A2:A7); in F3 enter =COUNT(A2:A7).
      3. In B2 enter =A2-$F$2. In C2 enter =B2^2. Fill both down through row 7. Check that the mean reference stays F2 while each observation reference changes.
      4. In F4 enter =SUM(C2:C7). In F5 enter =F4/(F3-1). In F6 enter =SQRT(F5).
      5. In F7 enter =STDEV.S(A2:A7). In F8 enter =ABS(F6-F7). Both standard deviations should be approximately 2.0976; the difference should be zero or extremely small because of computer rounding.
      6. Change the last observation from 8 to 28. Watch the mean, all deviations, variance, and both standard-deviation results update. The two methods must still agree.

      Keep full precision in formulas and use number formatting to shorten the display. Squaring the deviations prevents positive and negative deviations from canceling. For a sample, divide the squared-deviation sum by n − 1.

      (Google Sheets: STDEV.S works; STDEV also calculates the sample standard deviation. The other formulas are the same.)

      During your check

      From a supplied list of about six observations, construct both methods, show their agreement, and demonstrate one requested data change. Fill formulas down rather than typing every row separately.

      Variations to prepare for

      • A different list or number of observations: update all affected ranges and use a count formula.
      • Add the same constant to every observation and compare the mean and standard deviation before and after.
      • If explicitly asked for a population standard deviation, divide by n and compare with STDEV.P instead. Do not mix sample and population methods.

      Be ready to explain

      Why the mean reference is fixed, why changing one observation affects every deviation, and why the standard deviation uses the original measurement units.
