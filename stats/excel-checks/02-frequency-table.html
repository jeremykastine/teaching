      Learn the workflow and see a worked example

      What to learn

      Build a summary that stays connected to a raw list of categorical observations. Use formulas for the counts and proportions, and make a bar or column chart from the summary.

      Practice example

      1. In D1:F1 enter Category, Frequency, and Relative frequency. Put Bus, Car, and Walk in D2:D4.
      2. In E2 enter =COUNTIF($A$2:$A$16,D2), then fill down through E4. The dollar signs keep the raw-data range fixed; the category reference changes as you copy.
      3. In E5 calculate the total with =SUM(E2:E4). In F2 enter =E2/$E$5 and fill through F4. Format F2:F4 as percentages. In F5 use =SUM(F2:F4).
      4. Check the results: Bus 5, Car 6, Walk 4; total frequency 15 and total relative frequency 100%. Keep the underlying values unrounded.
      5. Select D1:E4 and insert a bar or column chart. Give it a descriptive title. The categories should identify the bars; the total row must not become an extra category.
      6. Change one Car response to Walk. Show the two affected frequencies, relative frequencies, and chart updating while the total stays 15.

      (Google Sheets: the formulas are the same. Use Insert → Chart, then choose a bar or column chart in the chart editor and check the category labels and data range.)

      During your check

      Using a supplied short list with three or four categories, create the frequency and relative-frequency table, verify its totals, make the chart, and demonstrate one requested data change.

      Variations to prepare for

      • Different category names, counts, or list length; adjust every raw-data reference.
      • A chart of relative frequency instead of frequency.
      • One additional category: add a summary row and extend totals and the chart range.

      Be ready to explain

      What a relative frequency measures and why its denominator is the total number of observations. If the frequencies do not sum to the data count, check for an omitted category or an inconsistent spelling.
