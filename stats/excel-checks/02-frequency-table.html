Compare Risk and Reward

You may choose between two games at a fundraiser. Each play costs the entry fee and gives exactly one of the three prizes below. The percentage beside each prize is its probability. Assume the probabilities stay the same on every play and plays are independent.

Build one Excel calculation table to compare the expected net gain and standard deviation of the two games. Net gain means prize minus entry fee; receiving no prize means losing the entry fee.

Requirements

  1. Start with a new Excel workbook. Enter Game A’s entry fee, three prizes, and probabilities. Calculate each net gain with a formula and verify that the probabilities sum to 1 (100%).
  2. Build a labeled table showing net gain, probability, net gain × probability, deviation from the expected net gain, squared deviation, and probability × squared deviation. Use SUM to calculate the expected net gain and variance, then SQRT for standard deviation. Keep every calculation linked to the inputs.
  3. Create a separate comparison area with columns for Game A and Game B and rows for expected net gain and standard deviation. Manually type Game A’s two results as fixed numbers, rounded to cents. Do not link those recorded results to the calculation table.
  4. With your instructor watching, replace only the input data with Game B’s fee, prizes, and probabilities. Reuse the same table without rebuilding it or editing its formulas. Show the net gains, expected value, and standard deviation updating automatically while the recorded Game A results stay unchanged.
  5. Manually record Game B’s expected net gain and standard deviation beside Game A’s. Keep full precision in formulas; round only the recorded comparison results to cents.
  6. Orally explain what each expected value means, which game has the better long-run average net gain, and which has more variability for a single play. Use the standard deviations, loss probabilities, and possible losses and gains to explain your short-term choice. You may choose neither game if you explain why.

Show the weighted calculation step by step. Do not use AVERAGE, STDEV.P, or STDEV.S on the three possible gains: the outcomes have different probabilities. Do not divide the weighted squared-deviation sum by 3 or by 2.
