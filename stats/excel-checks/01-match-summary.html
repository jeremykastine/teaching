      Learn the workflow and see a worked example

      What to learn

      Construct a dataset from its summary statistics. The generator creates a hidden dataset with 50–100 observations between 0 and 1,000, but provides only its count, mean, median, and mode or modes. About half of random challenges have one mode; the other half have exactly two. At least one measure of center is substantially different from the others. Your numbers may differ from the original hidden numbers.

      Set up a blank Excel workbook

      1. Generate one assignment and keep its version link. Open a new, blank Excel workbook and put Observation in A1. Enter your own numbers in A2 downward, one number per cell. Use exactly the target count and keep every value between 0 and 1,000 inclusive. Decimals are allowed.
      2. Put the labels Count, Mean, Median, and Mode(s) in C2:C5. In D2:D5 enter =COUNT(A:A), =AVERAGE(A:A), =MEDIAN(A:A), and =MODE.MULT(A:A). Keep column A reserved for your observations; the text header is ignored.
      3. Leave D6 empty. Enter MODE.MULT once in D5: in current Excel, two modes spill into D5:D6 automatically. A blocked destination produces #SPILL!; clear the obstruction. A dataset with no repeated value gives #N/A, so create the required repetitions. In older Excel without dynamic arrays, select D5:D6, enter the formula with Ctrl+Shift+Enter, and disregard an unused #N/A slot for a one-mode result.
      4. Adjust your observations while watching the formulas update. The count and median must match exactly. MODE.MULT must return exactly the listed mode or both listed modes, in either order, with no additional modes. For two modes, both must occur equally often and more often than any other value.
      5. Bring the mean as close as possible to the target. Your instructor judges whether it is close enough; there is no fixed numerical tolerance. Show enough decimal places to inspect the error. Keep the count, median, and mode(s) exact throughout your final adjustments.

      Ways to reason about your adjustments

      • The required total is count × target mean. Changing the total by a small amount changes the mean by that amount divided by the count.
      • For an odd count, control the middle value after sorting. For an even count, control the average of the two middle values.
      • Give the required mode or modes the highest frequency. Avoid accidentally creating other equally frequent values.
      • To refine the mean, adjust values away from the middle positions and required repetitions. Use progressively smaller decimal changes, keep values in bounds, and recheck every summary after each adjustment.

      Formatting an incorrect value to look correct does not satisfy an exact target. Use formulas connected to the observations, rather than typing the target answers into the summary cells. The generated mean is an exact whole-number target, so you can keep refining your actual result without ambiguity from a rounded target.

      During your check

      Show your completed column A and all four formula-based summaries to your instructor. Match count, median, and the complete set of modes exactly, and mean approximately to the instructor’s satisfaction. Explain how you could narrow the mean error arbitrarily far while preserving the other requirements. You do not need to reproduce the hidden dataset or perform a second target change.

      Practice variations

      Start with Random variation for approximately equal chances of one or two modes. Use the selector to practice either kind deliberately. Regenerate for different counts and target values. The original hidden numbers are never shown, including in the practice self-check.
