      Learn the workflow and see a worked example

      What to learn

      Build linked confidence-interval calculations for several sample sizes. Separate fixed inputs from calculations, use cell references correctly, and explain how the margin of error changes.

      Practice example: known population standard deviation

      Assume independent random observations from a normal population with known population standard deviation. Use a sample mean of 50 and population standard deviation of 10. The supplied critical value 1.96 gives an approximately 95% confidence interval. Hold the mean and population standard deviation fixed to isolate the effect of sample size.

      1. Put Sample mean, Population standard deviation, and Critical value in A2:A4; enter 50, 10, and 1.96 in B2:B4.
      2. In D1:I1 enter Sample size, Standard error, Margin of error, Lower endpoint, Upper endpoint, and Interval width. Put 25, 100, and 400 in D2:D4.
      3. In E2 enter =$B$3/SQRT(D2). In F2 enter =$B$4*E2. In G2 enter =$B$2-F2; in H2 enter =$B$2+F2; in I2 enter =H2-G2.
      4. Fill E2:I2 down through row 4. Verify the input references stay fixed while the sample-size and intermediate-result references move with the row.
      5. Check that standard errors are 2, 1, and 0.5; margins of error are 3.92, 1.96, and 0.98; and the interval endpoints are 46.08–53.92, 48.04–51.96, and 49.02–50.98.
      6. Change the supplied critical value from 1.96 to 2.576, representing approximately 99% confidence. Show the margins and widths increasing while the standard errors stay fixed.

      (Google Sheets: all formulas and cell-reference rules are the same.)

      During your check

      Build the three-row comparison using supplied inputs and a supplied critical value, fill formulas efficiently, and demonstrate one requested change. You do not need to calculate the critical value yourself.

      Variations to prepare for

      • Different positive sample sizes, means, population standard deviations, or supplied critical values.
      • Find a sample size that halves the margin of error: multiply the original sample size by four when the population standard deviation and confidence level stay fixed. Verify with the table.
      • Change the sample mean alone and explain why both endpoints shift by the same amount while interval widths stay unchanged.

      Be ready to explain

      The difference between population standard deviation and standard error; why quadrupling sample size halves the standard error; and why higher confidence requires a wider interval. The confidence level describes the long-run success rate of the interval procedure, rather than the percentage of individual observations in this interval.

      This check uses known population standard deviation and a z critical value. If the population standard deviation is unknown, a procedure using sample standard deviation and a t critical value is a different model; do not substitute it into these directions without an explicit instruction.
