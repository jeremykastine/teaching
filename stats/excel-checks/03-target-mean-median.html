      Learn the workflow and see a worked example

      What to learn

      Use a spreadsheet to construct a dataset with specified properties. Enter formulas once, then adjust the observations while watching your summary update. The generated assignment specifies which statistics must match; the others may vary.

      Practice example

      1. Use A2:A6 for exactly five observations. Put labels Count, Sum, Mean, and Median in D2:D5. In E2:E5 enter =COUNT(A2:A6), =SUM(A2:A6), =AVERAGE(A2:A6), and =MEDIAN(A2:A6).
      2. Enter five nonnegative whole numbers with mean 10 and median 8. Keep the count at 5. Before experimenting, calculate the required total: count × target mean. Keep the values sorted while you reason about which observation must be in the middle.
      3. Use the summary to check your choices. One possible dataset is 4, 6, 8, 12, 20. Many other solutions work.
      4. Now change only one observation so the mean becomes 12 while the median remains 8. With five observations, increasing the mean by 2 requires increasing the total by 10. For the example above, changing 20 to 30 works.
      5. Finally, experiment with changing the median while holding the total, and therefore the mean, fixed. Offset an increase in one observation with an equal decrease in another; check the middle position after each change.

      Use the underlying results to check your work. Formatting 9.96 to display as 10 does not make its actual value equal to 10. Avoid changing the formulas to display the requested answer.

      (Google Sheets: these formulas and cell-reference rules are the same.)

      How to approach the generated variations

      • Mean and median: aim for a total of count × target mean (with whole numbers, use an attainable integer total within the stated mean tolerance). Sort the values and control the middle observation (or the average of the middle pair). Increasing one maximum raises the mean without changing the median.
      • Mean and sample standard deviation: use AVERAGE and STDEV.S. Start with pairs equally far above and below the target mean; for an odd count, add the mean itself. Move pairs farther apart to increase spread, or closer together to decrease it. Decimals are allowed. Adding a constant to every observation shifts the mean and leaves standard deviation unchanged.
      • Minimum, maximum, and sample standard deviation: keep one observation at each required endpoint. Start the remaining values near the midpoint, and move interior pairs toward the endpoints to increase spread. Adjust and recheck until the standard deviation is within the stated tolerance.
      • Median with extremes: fix both endpoints, then place the middle observation or middle pair at the requested median.
      • Mean with extremes: fix both endpoints, subtract their sum from the required total, then distribute the remaining total among the interior observations without exceeding the endpoints.

      Use COUNT, AVERAGE, MEDIAN, MIN, MAX, and STDEV.S for the live summary. (Google Sheets supports the same functions.) Exact-match requirements and tolerances appear in the generated task. Keep all underlying precision; number formatting alone does not satisfy a target.

      During your check

      Meet all the generated requirements at once, using the specified count and allowed values. Demonstrate the listed follow-up change and explain which statistics it preserves. The targets are generated from attainable datasets; many different answers may work.

      Variations to prepare for

      • Use the selector to practice all five target combinations above. Only the listed targets are required for an attempt.
      • The count can be 5, 6, 7, 8, or 10. For an even count, the median is the average of the two middle values after sorting. Update all summary ranges.
      • Some tasks require whole numbers; the standard-deviation constructions allow decimals. Read the allowed-values row and the follow-up before starting.

      Be ready to explain

      How the required total helps you control the mean, why changing an extreme observation can leave the median unchanged, and why the number of observations must remain fixed.
