Statistics

1.1: Definitions of Statistics, Probability, and Key Terms · Essentials

Answer the questions from memory on paper. Then check the answer key. Attempt each example before opening its solution.

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. What is a population?
  2. What is a sample?
  3. How does a parameter differ from a statistic?
  4. What is descriptive statistics?
  5. What is inferential statistics?
Check short-answer key
  1. The entire group about which a study seeks information.
  2. The subset of the population actually observed.
  3. A parameter describes a population; a statistic describes a sample.
  4. Organizing and summarizing observed data.
  5. Using sample evidence to draw conclusions about a population with uncertainty.

Example problems

Example 1

A university has 4,000 students. A survey of 100 finds mean study time 8 hours per week. Identify population, sample, and statistic.

Show solution
  1. The population is all 4,000 students; the sample is the 100 surveyed.
  2. The sample mean of 8 hours is a statistic.
  3. The mean for all 4,000 students would be the population parameter.

Example 2

A report displays a class's exam average, then predicts the university average from that class. Identify the two types of statistics.

Show solution
  1. Summarizing the observed class is descriptive.
  2. Generalizing to the university is inferential.
  3. Whether that inference is justified depends on how representative the class is and on the sampling design.
