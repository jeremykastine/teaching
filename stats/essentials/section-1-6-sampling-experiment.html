Statistics

1.6: Sampling Experiment · Essentials

Guided lab

Answer the questions from memory on paper. Then check the answer key. Attempt each example before opening its solution.

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. Why do two random samples usually give different summaries?
  2. What is a sampling frame?
  3. What can happen if the frame omits part of the population?
  4. How can a simple random sample be selected reproducibly?
  5. Does one unusual sample prove random sampling failed?
Check short-answer key
  1. Sampling variability.
  2. The list or mechanism from which a sample is selected.
  3. Undercoverage bias.
  4. Label population members, use a random selection method, and record the procedure or seed.
  5. No; random samples can differ by chance.

Example problems

Example 1

Select a simple random sample of 5 from a class roster of 30. Describe the procedure.

Show solution
  1. Label students 1 through 30.
  2. Use a random-number tool to choose 5 distinct labels without replacement.
  3. Record the method and selected labels; each possible five-student subset should have equal probability.

Example 2

Two samples estimate mean commute time as 18 and 22 minutes. Explain the difference.

Show solution
  1. Different selected students can yield different means even under the same sound method.
  2. This is sampling variability.
  3. Without more information, neither mean can be declared the exact population mean or proof of bias.
