5.1: Continuous Probability Functions · Essentials

Answer the questions from memory on paper. Then check the answer key. Attempt each example before opening its solution.

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. How are continuous probabilities represented by a density curve?
  2. What is the total area under a density curve?
  3. Can a density be negative?
  4. What is P(X = c) for a continuous random variable?
  5. Do including or excluding interval endpoints change continuous probabilities?
Check short-answer key
  1. As areas under the curve over intervals.
  2. 1.
  3. No.
  4. 0 for any single value c.
  5. No; individual endpoints have probability zero.

Example problems

Example 1

A density equals 0.25 on [0, 4] and zero elsewhere. Verify it and find P(1 < X < 3).

Show solution
  1. The density is nonnegative and its total area is 4(0.25) = 1.
  2. The requested interval has width 2.
  3. Its area and probability are 2(0.25) = 0.5.

Example 2

For a continuous X, suppose P(X ≤ 7) = 0.8. Find P(X > 7) and P(X = 7).

Show solution
  1. The complement probability is 1 − 0.8 = 0.2.
  2. The probability of the individual point 7 is zero.
  3. Therefore P(X ≥ 7) is also 0.2.
