Statistics

1.4: Experimental Design and Ethics · Essentials

Answer the questions from memory on paper. Then check the answer key. Attempt each example before opening its solution.

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. How does an experiment differ from an observational study?
  2. What is a confounding variable?
  3. What is random assignment for?
  4. What is blinding?
  5. How do random sampling and random assignment serve different purposes?
Check short-answer key
  1. An experiment assigns treatments; an observational study records without assigning them.
  2. A variable whose effect cannot be separated from another explanatory effect in the study.
  3. To balance potential confounders across treatment groups in expectation.
  4. Concealing treatment assignments from participants or evaluators to reduce bias.
  5. Sampling supports population generalization; assignment supports causal comparisons.

Example problems

Example 1

Students choose whether to attend tutoring; attendees score higher. Does this establish tutoring caused the difference?

Show solution
  1. This is observational because students chose attendance.
  2. Motivation or preparation could affect both attendance and scores.
  3. The observed association alone does not establish causation.

Example 2

Design an experiment comparing two study methods.

Show solution
  1. Randomly assign consenting students to the two methods.
  2. Keep study time and the assessment comparable; blind graders to group.
  3. Compare outcomes, recording attrition and recognizing the limits of the recruited population.
