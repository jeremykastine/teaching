6.2: Using the Normal Distribution · Essentials

Answer the questions from memory on paper. Then check the answer key. Attempt each example before opening its solution.

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. How is a normal interval probability calculated after standardizing?
  2. How do you find a value from a known z-score?
  3. What is a percentile cutoff?
  4. What must be specified to use a normal model for an individual measurement?
  5. How do you find an upper-tail probability?
Check short-answer key
  1. Subtract the lower cumulative area from the upper cumulative area.
  2. x = μ + zσ.
  3. A value with the specified cumulative proportion at or below it.
  4. A justified normal distribution with its mean and SD.
  5. Subtract the cumulative probability from 1.

Example problems

Example 1

Let X be normal with mean 50 and SD 10. Find P(40 < X < 60).

Show solution
  1. Standardize bounds to −1 and 1.
  2. Standard normal cumulative areas are approximately 0.1587 and 0.8413.
  3. Probability ≈ 0.6826.

Example 2

Find the 95th percentile of a normal population with mean 70 and SD 8.

Show solution
  1. The standard normal 95th-percentile z is approximately 1.645.
  2. x = 70 + 1.645(8) = 83.16.
  3. Approximately 95% of model values lie at or below 83.16.
