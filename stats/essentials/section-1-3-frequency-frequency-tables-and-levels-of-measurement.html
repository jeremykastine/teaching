Statistics

1.3: Frequency, Frequency Tables, and Levels of Measurement · Essentials

Answer the questions from memory on paper. Then check the answer key. Attempt each example before opening its solution.

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. What is frequency?
  2. What is relative frequency?
  3. What is cumulative frequency?
  4. What distinguishes nominal from ordinal data?
  5. What distinguishes interval from ratio measurements?
Check short-answer key
  1. The count of observations in a category or class.
  2. Frequency divided by total sample size.
  3. The running total through an ordered category or class.
  4. Ordinal categories have meaningful order; nominal categories do not.
  5. Ratio measurements have a meaningful zero and meaningful ratios; interval measurements do not.

Example problems

Example 1

Ratings low, medium, high have counts 2, 5, 3. Find relative and cumulative frequencies.

Show solution
  1. Total n = 10; relative frequencies are 0.2, 0.5, 0.3.
  2. Cumulative counts in that order are 2, 7, 10.
  3. The relative frequencies sum to 1.

Example 2

Classify blood type, class rank, Celsius temperature, and mass by level of measurement.

Show solution
  1. Blood type is nominal; rank is ordinal.
  2. Celsius is interval because zero does not mean absence of temperature.
  3. Mass is ratio because zero and ratios are meaningful.
