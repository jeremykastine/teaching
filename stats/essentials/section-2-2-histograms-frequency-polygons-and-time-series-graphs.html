2.2: Histograms, Frequency Polygons, and Time Series Graphs · Essentials

Answer the questions from memory on paper. Then check the answer key. Attempt each example before opening its solution.

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. What type of data is a histogram designed for?
  2. What must be true of histogram classes?
  3. What is a frequency polygon?
  4. What is a time-series graph?
  5. Why distinguish frequency from relative frequency on an axis?
Check short-answer key
  1. Quantitative data grouped into numerical intervals.
  2. They should not overlap and should account for the observations.
  3. Class-midpoint frequencies plotted and connected by line segments.
  4. Measurements plotted in time order.
  5. One shows counts; the other shows proportions or percentages.

Example problems

Example 1

Group 1, 2, 2, 4, 5, 7 into classes [0, 3), [3, 6), [6, 9).

Show solution
  1. The first class contains 1, 2, 2: frequency 3.
  2. The second contains 4, 5: frequency 2; the third contains 7: frequency 1.
  3. Equal-width histogram bars have heights 3, 2, 1; midpoint-frequency pairs are (1.5, 3), (4.5, 2), (7.5, 1).

Example 2

Values on Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday are 10, 14, 11. Describe their time-series graph.

Show solution
  1. Plot the values against days in chronological order.
  2. Connect consecutive days to show a rise of 4 followed by a fall of 3.
  3. Reordering the days by value would destroy the time pattern.
