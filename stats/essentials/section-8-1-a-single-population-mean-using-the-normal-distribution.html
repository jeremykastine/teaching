8.1: A Single Population Mean using the Normal Distribution · Essentials

Answer the questions from memory on paper. Then check the answer key. Attempt each example before opening its solution.

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. When is a z interval for a population mean appropriate?
  2. What is the z-interval formula?
  3. What is a margin of error?
  4. What does 95% confidence describe?
  5. How does increasing confidence affect width with other factors fixed?
Check short-answer key
  1. With known population SD σ and a sampling design and normality/large-sample conditions supporting the normal reference model.
  2. x¯±z*σ/n.
  3. The amount added to and subtracted from the point estimate.
  4. In repeated sampling, about 95% of intervals from the procedure contain the fixed population parameter under the assumptions.
  5. It makes the interval wider.

Example problems

Example 1

For x̄ = 50, known σ = 10, n = 100, find a 95% z interval, assuming conditions hold.

Show solution
  1. SE = 10/√100 = 1; use z* ≈ 1.96.
  2. Margin = 1.96.
  3. Interval: (48.04, 51.96), estimating the population mean.

Example 2

Plan a 95% mean interval with margin at most 2 and known σ = 10. Find n.

Show solution
  1. Use n≥(z*σ/E)2.
  2. (1.96 × 10/2)² = 96.04.
  3. Round upward to n = 97, subject to the study design assumptions.
