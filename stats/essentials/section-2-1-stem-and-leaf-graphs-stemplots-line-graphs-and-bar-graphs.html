Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. What does a stem-and-leaf plot preserve?
  2. Why must a stemplot have a key?
  3. What does a bar graph compare?
  4. Why are gaps commonly used between bars in a categorical bar graph?
  5. What should every graph label clearly?
Check short-answer key
  1. Individual data values, along with distribution shape.
  2. The key explains each stem and leaf's place value and units.
  3. Frequencies or values for categories.
  4. To distinguish separate categories.
  5. Variables, units, and any scale or encoding needed to interpret it.

Example problems

Example 1

Make a stemplot for 12, 15, 15, 21, 24.

Show solution
  1. Use tens as stems and ones as leaves.
  2. Stem 1 has leaves 2, 5, 5; stem 2 has leaves 1, 4.
  3. Key: 1 | 2 means 12. Repeated leaves preserve repeated observations.

Example 2

A class has 8 biology majors, 5 business majors, and 7 other majors. Describe a bar graph.

Show solution
  1. Put the three categories on the horizontal axis and student counts on the vertical axis.
  2. Draw separate bars of heights 8, 5, and 7 on a scale starting at zero.
  3. The total count is 20; biology is the largest category.
