Statistics

2.5: Measures of the Center of the Data · Essentials

Answer the questions from memory on paper. Then check the answer key. Attempt each example before opening its solution.

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. How is the arithmetic mean calculated?
  2. How is the median found for an even number of observations?
  3. What is a mode?
  4. Which center is more resistant to extreme values?
  5. How is a weighted mean calculated?
Check short-answer key
  1. Sum the observations and divide by their count.
  2. Sort the values and average the two middle values.
  3. A most frequent data value; there may be more than one.
  4. The median.
  5. Sum value × weight and divide by the sum of weights.

Example problems

Example 1

Find mean, median, and mode for 2, 4, 4, 6, 14.

Show solution
  1. The sum is 30, so the mean is 30/5 = 6.
  2. The middle sorted value is 4, so median = 4.
  3. The most frequent value is 4, so mode = 4.

Example 2

Scores 70 and 90 have weights 1 and 3. Find the weighted mean.

Show solution
  1. Multiply and sum: 70(1) + 90(3) = 340.
  2. Total weight = 4.
  3. Weighted mean = 340/4 = 85.
