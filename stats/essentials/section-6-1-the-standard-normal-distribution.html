6.1: The Standard Normal Distribution · Essentials

Answer the questions from memory on paper. Then check the answer key. Attempt each example before opening its solution.

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. What are the mean and SD of the standard normal distribution?
  2. How do you standardize a normal value x?
  3. What does a negative z-score mean?
  4. What area lies on each side of the standard normal mean?
  5. What does a z-score measure?
Check short-answer key
  1. Mean 0 and SD 1.
  2. z=(x−μ)/σ.
  3. The value lies below the mean.
  4. 0.5.
  5. Distance from the mean in standard-deviation units.

Example problems

Example 1

A normal variable has mean 100 and SD 15. Standardize x = 130.

Show solution
  1. z = (130 − 100)/15 = 2.
  2. The value is two SD above the mean.
  3. For a normal variable, the area below z = 2 is approximately 0.9772.

Example 2

For standard normal Z, use P(Z ≤ 1) ≈ 0.8413 to find P(Z > 1) and P(−1 < Z < 1).

Show solution
  1. The upper tail is 1 − 0.8413 = 0.1587.
  2. Symmetry gives P(Z ≤ −1) ≈ 0.1587.
  3. Middle area ≈ 0.8413 − 0.1587 = 0.6826.
