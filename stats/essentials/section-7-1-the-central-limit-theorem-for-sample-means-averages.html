Statistics

7.1: The Central Limit Theorem for Sample Means (Averages) · Essentials

Answer the questions from memory on paper. Then check the answer key. Attempt each example before opening its solution.

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. What is a sampling distribution of the sample mean?
  2. What is its mean for independent identically distributed observations with finite mean?
  3. What is its standard error when the population SD is σ?
  4. What does the Central Limit Theorem say?
  5. Does n = 30 guarantee a good normal approximation for every population?
Check short-answer key
  1. The distribution of means from repeated samples of the same size under the sampling model.
  2. The population mean μ.
  3. σ/n.
  4. With suitable independent observations and finite variance, standardized sample means approach a normal distribution as sample size grows.
  5. No; adequacy depends on skewness, outliers, tails, and sampling conditions.

Example problems

Example 1

A population has mean 80 and SD 12. For independent samples of size 36, find mean and SE of the sample mean.

Show solution
  1. Sampling-distribution mean = 80.
  2. SE = 12/√36 = 2.
  3. Its shape is exactly normal for a normal population, or approximately normal if CLT conditions and sample size make the approximation adequate.

Example 2

From a normal population with mean 50 and SD 10, take n = 25 independent observations. Find P(sample mean > 54).

Show solution
  1. The sample mean is normal with SE = 10/5 = 2.
  2. z = (54 − 50)/2 = 2.
  3. Upper-tail probability ≈ 0.0228.
