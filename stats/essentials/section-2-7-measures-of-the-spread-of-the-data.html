2.7: Measures of the Spread of the Data · Essentials

Answer the questions from memory on paper. Then check the answer key. Attempt each example before opening its solution.

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. What is the range?
  2. How is population variance calculated?
  3. How is sample variance calculated?
  4. How is standard deviation related to variance?
  5. What does the empirical rule say for an approximately normal distribution?
Check short-answer key
  1. Maximum minus minimum.
  2. Average the squared deviations from the population mean, dividing by N.
  3. Sum squared deviations from the sample mean and divide by n − 1.
  4. It is the nonnegative square root of variance.
  5. About 68%, 95%, and 99.7% lie within 1, 2, and 3 standard deviations of the mean.

Example problems

Example 1

For the sample 2, 4, 6, find range, variance, and standard deviation.

Show solution
  1. Mean = 4; deviations are −2, 0, 2 and squared deviations sum to 8.
  2. Range = 6 − 2 = 4; sample variance = 8/(3 − 1) = 4.
  3. Sample standard deviation = √4 = 2. If these were the entire population, variance would instead be 8/3.

Example 2

An approximately normal population has mean 70 and SD 5. Estimate the percentage between 60 and 80.

Show solution
  1. Both bounds are 10 units, or 2 SD, from the mean.
  2. The empirical rule gives approximately 95% within this interval.
