2.3: Measures of the Location of the Data · Essentials

Answer the questions from memory on paper. Then check the answer key. Attempt each example before opening its solution.

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. What does the median divide ordered data into?
  2. What do Q₁ and Q₃ represent?
  3. What is the interquartile range?
  4. Why sort data before locating quartiles?
  5. Can quartile algorithms give slightly different answers?
Check short-answer key
  1. A lower half and an upper half, with the middle value or middle pair determining the median.
  2. The first and third quartiles, near the 25th and 75th percentiles.
  3. IQR = Q₃ − Q₁.
  4. Quartiles describe positions in the ordered distribution.
  5. Yes, especially for small datasets; state and consistently use the chosen convention.

Example problems

Example 1

Find the median and quartiles of 1, 3, 5, 7, 9, 11, 13, 15 using medians of halves.

Show solution
  1. The median is (7 + 9)/2 = 8.
  2. The lower half is 1, 3, 5, 7, giving Q₁ = 4.
  3. The upper half is 9, 11, 13, 15, giving Q₃ = 12; IQR = 8.

Example 2

Interpret a 90th-percentile score of 82.

Show solution
  1. The cutoff is near the point at or below which 90% of scores lie, subject to the percentile convention and ties.
  2. It is a position in the distribution.
  3. It does not mean the student answered 90% of questions correctly.
