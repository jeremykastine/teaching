1.2: Data, Sampling, and Variation in Data and Sampling · Essentials

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. How do qualitative and quantitative data differ?
  2. How do discrete and continuous quantitative data differ?
  3. What is a simple random sample of size n?
  4. How do stratified and cluster sampling differ?
  5. Does a larger sample automatically remove selection bias?
Check short-answer key
  1. Qualitative data give categories; quantitative data give numerical counts or measurements.
  2. Discrete values are countable; continuous measurements can vary across an interval.
  3. Every possible subset of n population members has the same selection probability.
  4. Stratified sampling samples within every stratum; cluster sampling randomly selects clusters and studies their members.
  5. No.

Example problems

Example 1

Classify eye color, number of courses, and height.

Show solution
  1. Eye color is qualitative.
  2. Number of courses is quantitative discrete.
  3. Height is quantitative continuous, even if recorded to a limited precision.

Example 2

A college samples 10 students at random from every major. Name the design and compare it with selecting three entire majors.

Show solution
  1. Sampling within every major is stratified sampling.
  2. Randomly selecting three majors and surveying all their students is cluster sampling.
  3. The designs use groups differently; neither guarantees freedom from nonresponse bias.
