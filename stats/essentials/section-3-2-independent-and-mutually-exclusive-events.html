Statistics

3.2: Independent and Mutually Exclusive Events · Essentials

Answer the questions from memory on paper. Then check the answer key. Attempt each example before opening its solution.

Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. What does independence mean?
  2. What does mutually exclusive mean?
  3. What product rule holds for independent A and B?
  4. Can two mutually exclusive events with positive probabilities be independent?
  5. Does sampling without replacement usually preserve independence?
Check short-answer key
  1. Knowing one event occurred does not change the other's probability.
  2. The events cannot occur together.
  3. P(A and B) = P(A)P(B).
  4. No; occurrence of one rules out the other.
  5. No; the first selection changes the remaining population.

Example problems

Example 1

A fair coin is tossed twice. Are first-toss heads and second-toss heads independent?

Show solution
  1. Each has probability 1/2; both together have probability 1/4.
  2. Since 1/4 = (1/2)(1/2), they are independent.
  3. They are not mutually exclusive because HH is possible.

Example 2

A fair die is rolled. Are rolling 1 and rolling 2 independent?

Show solution
  1. The events cannot occur together, so they are mutually exclusive.
  2. Their joint probability is 0, while the product is 1/36.
  3. They are not independent.
