3.5: Tree and Venn Diagrams

Double-click or double-tap the blank space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Follow along with Section 3.5 in the textbook. Examples and Try It questions appear in textbook order.

Tree diagrams

A tree diagram records an experiment in stages. Each branch gives a count or a probability. Multiply along a path to find its joint probability; add the probabilities of distinct paths when either path satisfies the event. Branch probabilities leaving the same node sum to 1.

Example 3.24 · Two draws with replacement

An urn contains three red balls and eight blue balls. Draw twice, replacing the first ball before the second draw. Label the balls R₁–R₃ and B₁–B₈. There are 11 × 11 = 121 equally likely ordered pairs of individual balls.

In a frequency tree, the first branches have counts 8 B and 3 R; each second stage again has 8 B and 3 R. The four path counts are 64 BB, 24 BR, 24 RB, and 9 RR.

  1. List the 24 individual-ball outcomes for BR.
  2. Find P(RR).
  3. Find P(RB OR BR).
  4. Find P(R on first AND B on second).
  5. Find P(R on second | B on first). Explain the restricted sample space.
  6. Find P(BB).
  7. Find P(B on second | R on first).
  1. B₁R₁, B₁R₂, B₁R₃; B₂R₁, B₂R₂, B₂R₃; B₃R₁, B₃R₂, B₃R₃; B₄R₁, B₄R₂, B₄R₃; B₅R₁, B₅R₂, B₅R₃; B₆R₁, B₆R₂, B₆R₃; B₇R₁, B₇R₂, B₇R₃; B₈R₁, B₈R₂, B₈R₃.
  2. (3/11)(3/11) = 9/121.
  3. (3/11)(8/11) + (8/11)(3/11) = 48/121.
  4. P(RB) = (3/11)(8/11) = 24/121.
  5. 24/(64 + 24) = 24/88 = 3/11. Restrict to the 88 ordered pairs whose first ball is blue.
  6. (8/11)(8/11) = 64/121.
  7. 24/(24 + 9) = 24/33 = 8/11. Replacement restores the original composition.

Try It 3.24 · Cards with replacement

A standard deck has 12 face cards (F) and 40 other cards (N). Draw twice with replacement. Draw a frequency tree and find P(FF).

There are 52² = 2704 equally likely ordered card pairs. P(FF) = (12/52)(12/52) = 144/2704 = 9/169 ≈ 0.0533.

Drawing without replacement

Without replacement means the first selection stays out. The next denominator decreases by 1, and the numerator depends on what was removed. The second-stage labels are conditional probabilities, such as P(R on second | B on first).

Example 3.25 · The same urn, without replacement

Use the urn of three red and eight blue balls. This time, do not replace the first ball.

  1. Find P(RR).
  2. Find P(RB OR BR). Write both products.
  3. Find P(R on second | B on first).
  4. Find P(R on first AND B on second).
  5. Find P(BB).
  6. Find P(B on second | R on first).
  1. (3/11)(2/10) = 6/110 = 3/55.
  2. (3/11)(8/10) + (8/11)(3/10) = 48/110 = 24/55.
  3. 3/10. A blue ball was removed, leaving all three red balls among ten balls.
  4. P(RB) = (3/11)(8/10) = 24/110.
  5. (8/11)(7/10) = 56/110 = 28/55.
  6. 8/10 = 4/5. After a red is removed, eight of the remaining ten are blue.

Check: (56 + 24 + 24 + 6)/110 = 1. Notice the difference between a two-draw joint probability and a second-draw conditional probability.

Try It 3.25 · Cards without replacement

Draw two cards without replacement from a standard deck of 12 face cards (F) and 40 non-face cards (N). Draw and label a probability tree.

  1. Find P(FN OR NF).
  2. Find P(N on second | F on first).
  3. Find P(at most one face card).
  4. Find P(at least one face card).
  1. (12/52)(40/51) + (40/52)(12/51) = 960/2652 = 80/221 ≈ 0.3620.
  2. 40/51. The first card is already known to be a face card.
  3. Zero or one: NN, FN, NF. Probability = (1560 + 480 + 480)/2652 = 2520/2652 = 210/221 ≈ 0.9502. Equivalently, 1 − P(FF).
  4. One or two: FN, NF, FF. Probability = (480 + 480 + 132)/2652 = 1092/2652 = 91/221 ≈ 0.4118. Equivalently, 1 − P(NN).

Example 3.26 · Selecting two kittens

A shelter has four tabby kittens (T) and five black kittens (B). Two kittens are selected randomly, without replacement. Draw a tree before calculating.

  1. Find the probability of selecting two tabbies.
  2. Find the probability of selecting one of each color.
  3. Find P(tabby second | black first).
  4. Find the probability of selecting two of the same color.
  1. (4/9)(3/8) = 12/72 = 1/6.
  2. P(TB) + P(BT) = (4/9)(5/8) + (5/9)(4/8) = 40/72 = 5/9. Both orders count.
  3. 4/8 = 1/2.
  4. P(TT) + P(BB) = 12/72 + 20/72 = 32/72 = 4/9.

Try It 3.26 · One of each color

A box contains four red balls and three yellow balls. Draw two without replacement. What is the probability of drawing one of each color?

There are two acceptable paths: RY and YR. P(RY OR YR) = (4/7)(3/6) + (3/7)(4/6) = 24/42 = 4/7 ≈ 0.5714.

Venn diagrams

A Venn diagram places events inside a rectangle representing the sample space S. Overlap means AND; the whole area in either event means OR. Outside an event is its complement. Each outcome belongs in exactly one region. A region containing several listed outcomes represents that many possible outcomes, not just one.

Example 3.27 · Equally likely numbered outcomes

Let S = {1, 2, …, 12}, all equally likely. Let A = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6} and B = {6, 7, 8, 9}. Draw a Venn diagram and identify A AND B and A OR B.

A AND B = {6}. A OR B = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9}. Thus P(A AND B) = 1/12 and P(A OR B) = 9/12. The outcome 6 appears only once in the union.

Try It 3.27 · Color outcomes

The equally likely outcomes are black, white, red, orange, yellow, green, blue, and purple. Let C = {green, blue, purple} and P = {red, yellow, blue}. Draw a Venn diagram and identify the intersection and union.

C AND P = {blue}. C OR P = {green, blue, purple, red, yellow}. The probabilities are 1/8 and 5/8, respectively.

Example 3.28 · Two fair coins

Flip two fair coins. Let A = tails on the first coin and B = tails on the second coin. List S, A, B, A AND B, and A OR B. Draw the Venn diagram. Which outcome is in neither event?

S = {HH, HT, TH, TT}; A = {TH, TT}; B = {HT, TT}. A AND B = {TT}; A OR B = {TH, TT, HT}. HH is in neither event.

Each outcome has probability 1/4, so the intersection has probability 1/4 and the union has probability 3/4.

Try It 3.28 · Prime and odd die rolls

Roll a fair six-sided die. Let A = prime result and B = odd result. Draw a Venn diagram, identifying both the overlap and the union.

A = {2, 3, 5}; B = {1, 3, 5}. A AND B = {3, 5}; A OR B = {1, 2, 3, 5}.

P(A AND B) = 2/6 = 1/3. P(A OR B) = 4/6 = 2/3.

Venn diagrams with percentages and probabilities

Start by putting the intersection in the overlap. Subtract it from each whole-event total to obtain the “only” regions. Subtract the union from 1 to obtain neither. For a conditional probability, divide the overlap by the probability of the given event.

Example 3.29 · Clubs and part-time work

At a college, 40% of students belong to a club (C), 50% work part time (PT), and 5% do both. Draw a Venn diagram.

  1. Find P(C).
  2. Find P(PT).
  3. Find P(C AND PT).
  4. Find P(C | PT).
  5. Find P(C OR PT).
  1. P(C) = 0.40 = 0.35 + 0.05.
  2. P(PT) = 0.50 = 0.45 + 0.05.
  3. P(C AND PT) = 0.05.
  4. P(C | PT) = 0.05/0.50 = 0.10. Among part-time workers, 10% belong to a club.
  5. P(C OR PT) = 0.40 + 0.50 − 0.05 = 0.85.

Neither = 1 − 0.85 = 0.15. The 40% and 50% are whole-circle totals, not the “only” regions.

Try It 3.29 · Workers and households

At a factory, 50% of workers have a second job (W), 25% have a spouse who also works (S), and 5% satisfy both descriptions. Draw a Venn diagram.

W only = 50% − 5% = 45%. S only = 25% − 5% = 20%. Union = 70%; neither = 30%. The overlap is 5%.

Example 3.30 · Two blood characteristics

For this textbook probability exercise, use the supplied proportions for the population being modeled: P(O) = 0.51 for type O blood, P(R) = 0.075 for Rh-negative, and P(O AND R) = 0.04. The 0.075 is the textbook’s midpoint of 0.05 and 0.10. These are exercise assumptions. Draw a Venn diagram.

  1. State P(O).
  2. State P(R).
  3. State P(O AND R).
  4. Find P(O OR R).
  5. Describe the overlap in a complete sentence.
  6. Describe the region outside both events in a complete sentence.
  1. 0.51.
  2. 0.075.
  3. 0.04.
  4. 0.51 + 0.075 − 0.04 = 0.545.
  5. The overlap represents individuals who have both type O blood and an Rh-negative factor.
  6. Outside both represents individuals who have neither type O blood nor an Rh-negative factor. Its probability is 1 − 0.545 = 0.455.

Try It 3.30 · Bookstore purchases

A bookstore customer has probability 0.6 of buying a novel (N), probability 0.4 of buying nonfiction (F), and probability 0.2 of buying both.

  1. Draw a Venn diagram.
  2. Find P(N OR F).
  3. Describe the overlap in a complete sentence.
  4. Add an oval for the event “buys only compact discs.” Where should it go?
  1. N only = 0.6 − 0.2 = 0.4. F only = 0.4 − 0.2 = 0.2. Both = 0.2; neither = 0.2.
  2. P(N OR F) = 0.6 + 0.4 − 0.2 = 0.8.
  3. The overlap represents customers who buy both a novel and a nonfiction book.
  4. Place the compact-discs-only oval inside the sample-space rectangle but completely outside both book circles. Its probability is not specified; it need not fill the entire neither region.
