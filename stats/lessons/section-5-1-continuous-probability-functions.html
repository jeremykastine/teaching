5.1: Continuous Probability Functions

Double-click or double-tap a blank solution area to show or hide the answer. With a keyboard, focus the area and press Enter or Space.

Follow the textbook’s sequence below. Historical datasets and supplied models are classroom exercises. Try It answers may be concise; use the preceding worked example as a method guide.

We begin by defining a continuous probability density function. We use the function notation f(x). Intermediate algebra may have been your first formal introduction to functions. In the study of probability, the functions we study are special. We define the function f(x) so that the area between it and the x-axis is equal to a probability. Since the maximum probability is one, the maximum area is also one. For continuous probability distributions, PROBABILITY = AREA.

Example 5.1

Let f(x) = 1/20 for 0 ≤ x ≤ 20 and zero elsewhere. Sketch the density and verify its total area. Find P(0<X<2), P(4<X<15), P(X=15), and P(2.3<X<12.7). Explain left-tail and right-tail probabilities.

The horizontal density has height 1/(20−0), zero outside [0,20]. The shaded rectangle runs from 2.3 to 12.7.

Total area = 20(1/20) = 1. Requested probabilities are 2/20 = 0.10; 11/20 = 0.55; 0 (a single point has zero width); and 10.4/20 = 0.52. F(x) = P(X≤x) is area to the left; P(X>x) = 1−F(x).

Try It 5.1

Consider the function f(x) = 1 8 for 0 ≤ x ≤ 8. Draw the graph of f(x) and find P(2.5 < x < 7.5).

The horizontal density has height 1/(8−0), zero outside [0,8]. The shaded rectangle runs from 2.5 to 7.5.

Area = (7.5−2.5)(1/8) = 5/8 = 0.625.
