1. Simple random sample

Choose 15 restaurants. Describe the procedure and record the selected names.

Number all N restaurants in a fixed order, then select 15 distinct labels uniformly at random. Every set of 15 must have the same selection probability. Do not choose familiar restaurants or retain repeated labels as if they were new restaurants. Your selected names depend on the random draw.

2. Systematic sample

Choose a systematic sample of 15 restaurants. Explain how you handle a frame size not divisible by 15.

For N divisible by 15, let k=N/15, choose a uniform random starting integer from 1 through k, and take every kth name. For general N, use fractional interval k=N/15: choose u uniformly in (0,k] and take positions ceil(u+jk), j=0,…,14. The spacing is fixed apart from rounding, all 15 are distinct, and no wrapping is required. Check whether the ordering has a pattern related to the variable of interest.

3. Stratify by city

Select about 25% of the restaurants within each city, rounding each city’s count to the nearest whole number. Record the number selected per city.

Cities are strata. Count Nᵢ in each city, allocate nᵢ = round(0.25Nᵢ), then choose nᵢ distinct restaurants randomly within every city. The rounded totals should be checked against the actual frame; do not force a stated grand total if the frame counts disagree. Every city contributes to the sample.

4. Stratify by entree price

Repeat the 25% stratified procedure, now using the price categories rather than cities.

The price bands are now strata. Pool all restaurants in each band across cities, count each band, round its quarter, and sample randomly within each band. This gives representation across price categories. Use consistent boundaries so every restaurant belongs to one stratum.

5. Cluster by city

Choose two cities at random and include their restaurants. Contrast this with stratifying by city.

Choose two distinct cities uniformly, then include every restaurant in those cities. The number of restaurants depends on the chosen cities. Stratification samples within every city; clustering selects only some cities and takes all units in those selected clusters. Cities differ in size, so a simple unweighted restaurant average from this design may need careful interpretation.
