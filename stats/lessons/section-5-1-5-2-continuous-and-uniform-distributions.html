Build an area graph

Mark 0, 2, 5, 10, 20, and 60 on a number line. Draw a rectangle over each time interval so its area equals the reported proportion of patients. Start with 5 to 10 minutes. What height gives that rectangle an area of 30%? How does the 10-to-20 rectangle compare?

Area = width × height, so height = proportion ÷ width. For 5 to 10 minutes, 0.30 ÷ 5 = 0.06 per minute. The 10-to-20 rectangle has the same area and twice the width, so its height is half as large: 0.30 ÷ 10 = 0.03 per minute.

We use this step-shaped graph as a probability density function, written f(t). A density is nonnegative and has total area 1. Its height gives a rate per unit of time. For example, a height of 2 per minute across half a minute gives area 1.

Use whole rectangles and pieces

Suppose the probability is spread evenly within each bin. Find the modeled probability of waiting (a) from 2 to 5 minutes, (b) from 2 to 6 minutes, and (c) from 4 to 12 minutes. Which answer follows directly from the original report?

  1. 12%. The full 2-to-5 rectangle has area 12%. The report gives this share directly.
  2. 18%. Add 12% for the full 2-to-5 bin and 6% for the first minute of the 5-to-10 bin.
  3. 40%. From 4 to 5 gives 1 × 4% = 4%. From 5 to 10 gives 30%. From 10 to 12 gives 2 × 3% = 6%.

The report fixes each whole-bin area. Within a bin, the flat density makes the probability proportional to the width of the part you shade.

How many patients are called back by minute 6?

Find the probability of being called back by minute 6 and of still waiting after minute 6. How could you use two cumulative probabilities to find the probability of a wait between 2 and 6 minutes?

The cumulative distribution function (CDF) F(t) = P(T ≤ t) gives the total area up through time t. It rises from 0 to 1 as the cutoff moves across the graph.

F(6) = 8% + 12% + 6% = 26%. The area to the right is 1 − F(6) = 74%. Subtract the two left areas to get the area in between: F(6) − F(2) = 26% − 8% = 18%.

What is the probability of a wait of exactly 7 minutes?

How wide is the part of the graph that represents this event? Would including the endpoints of the interval from 2 to 6 change its modeled probability?

The single point 7 has zero width, so P(T = 7) = 0 under this continuous model. Adding the endpoints 2 and 6 adds zero area. For example, P(2 < T < 6) and P(2 ≤ T ≤ 6) both equal 18%.

Go from minutes to half-minutes or seconds

What columns would you use in Excel to build the graph with finer data? If two of the 100 patients fall between 7 and 7.5 minutes, what height should that rectangle have? What times would a stopwatch rounded to the nearest minute report as 7?

Use one row per bin with its lower edge, upper edge, width, count, relative frequency, and density. Use the actual bin edges on the time axis. For the half-minute bin, 2% ÷ 0.5 = 4% per minute. For a one-second bin on an axis measured in minutes, divide its probability by 1/60 minute. Add full bin areas and use the flat-within-bin assumption for any partial bins.

A reading rounded to 7 minutes represents 6.5 to under 7.5 minutes using the usual half-up convention. That whole interval can have positive probability. Finer bins need finer readings, and with a small sample many narrow bins may be empty.

The uniform distribution

Return to the original model. Focus on patients whose waits fall from 5 to 10 minutes and use the flat assumption within that bin. In a uniform distribution, intervals of the same length within the range have the same probability.

Make the 5-to-10 group the whole population under discussion

Write the distribution using U(a, b) and draw the density. What height makes the area of this single rectangle equal to 1? What fraction of this group waited more than 8 minutes?

In this subgroup the model is U(5, 10). Its density is 1 ÷ (10 − 5) = 0.20 per minute between 5 and 10 and zero outside. For a uniform model from a to b, the height is 1/(b − a).

Waiting more than 8 minutes takes up the last 2 minutes of the 5-minute range, so the probability is 2/5 = 40%. This is conditional on being in the 5-to-10 group. On the original graph, the 8-to-10 area is 12% and the 5-to-10 area is 30%. The conditional calculation is 12% ÷ 30% = 40%.

For an interval that extends beyond the uniform range, count only the overlap. For example, 9 to 12 overlaps the range for one minute and has probability 1/5 in this subgroup.

Describe the center and spread

For the U(5, 10) model, find the mean and standard deviation. What happens to each if every wait increases by two minutes?

The mean is the midpoint, μ = (a + b)/2 = 7.5 minutes. The standard deviation is σ = (b − a)/√12 = 5/√12 ≈ 1.44 minutes. These are model values. A sample drawn from the model can have a different mean and standard deviation.

Adding two minutes changes the range to 7 through 12 and moves the mean to 9.5 minutes. The width stays 5 minutes, so the standard deviation stays the same. A wider range would increase the standard deviation.

Work backward from an area

For the U(5, 10) model, find the 90th percentile. Then find the interval containing the middle 50% of modeled waits.

The 90th percentile leaves 90% to its left and 10% to its right. A width of 0.90 × 5 = 4.5 minutes gives that left area. Start at minute 5 to get 9.5 minutes.

The middle 50% is between the 25th and 75th percentiles. Start at 5 and move 0.25 × 5 = 1.25 minutes for the lower endpoint and 0.75 × 5 = 3.75 minutes for the upper endpoint. The interval runs from 6.25 to 8.75 minutes.
