Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. What does it mean for a to divide b?
  2. How can multiplication check a division?
  3. How do you test divisibility by 3?
  4. How do you test divisibility by 2, 5, and 10?
  5. Dividing by 3 and then by 4 equals dividing by what?
Check short-answer key
  1. b is a whole-number multiple of a, with a nonzero.
  2. Multiply the quotient by the divisor; include any remainder.
  3. Add the digits; the sum must be divisible by 3.
  4. The last digit is even; 0 or 5; and 0, respectively.
  5. By 12, the product of the divisors.

Example problems

Example 1

Compute 144 divided by 12 using successive division.

Show solution
  1. Divide by 3: 144 ÷ 3 = 48.
  2. Divide by 4: 48 ÷ 4 = 12.
  3. Check: 12 × 12 = 144.

Example 2

Is 234 divisible by 6? Explain.

Show solution
  1. A number divisible by both 2 and 3 is divisible by 6.
  2. 234 ends in an even digit, and 2 + 3 + 4 = 9 is divisible by 3.
  3. Therefore 234 is divisible by 6; 234 ÷ 6 = 39.
