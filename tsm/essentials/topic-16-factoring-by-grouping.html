Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. What is factoring by grouping?
  2. What must match after the first factoring step?
  3. Why might you factor a negative from a group?
  4. Can terms be reordered before grouping?
  5. How do you check the final product?
Check short-answer key
  1. Factoring groups of terms separately to create a common factor.
  2. A common binomial or other polynomial factor.
  3. To make its remaining factor match the other group's factor.
  4. Yes, provided every term keeps its sign.
  5. Multiply it out and compare with the original.

Example problems

Example 1

Factor x3+3x2+2x+6.

Show solution
  1. Group: x2(x+3)+2(x+3).
  2. Factor the shared binomial: (x+3)(x2+2).

Example 2

Factor 3x3−6x2−2x+4.

Show solution
  1. Group: 3x2(x−2)−2(x−2).
  2. Factor (x − 2): (x−2)(3x2−2).
