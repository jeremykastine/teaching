Essential facts, definitions, and procedures

  1. What should you do before multiplying rational expressions?
  2. How do you divide rational expressions?
  3. What restrictions arise from a divisor?
  4. May you cancel a term in an unfactored sum?
  5. What numerator remains after all its factors cancel?
Check short-answer key
  1. Factor all numerators and denominators.
  2. Multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor.
  3. It must be defined and nonzero, in addition to all original denominator restrictions.
  4. No; only common factors may be canceled.
  5. 1.

Example problems

Example 1

Simplify x2−9x2−x−6·x−2x+3.

Show solution
  1. Factor: (x − 3)(x + 3) over (x − 3)(x + 2), times (x − 2)/(x + 3).
  2. Original restrictions are x ≠ 3, −2, −3.
  3. Cancel common factors to get (x − 2)/(x + 2), keeping all restrictions.

Example 2

Simplify 2xx2−4÷4x2x+2.

Show solution
  1. Original denominators exclude ±2; the divisor is zero at x = 0, so exclude 0 too.
  2. Multiply 2x(x−2)(x+2)·x+24x2.
  3. Cancel to get 12x(x−2), with x ≠ −2, 0, 2.
