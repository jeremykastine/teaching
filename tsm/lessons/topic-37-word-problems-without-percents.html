Topic 37: Word Problems Without Percents

Assigned to: TSM 098 and TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic translates non-percent word problems into linear equations or systems and interprets the resulting values in context.

Key definitions

Variable: a symbol representing an unknown quantity. Constraint: a relationship the unknowns must satisfy. Model: an equation or system representing a situation. Solution in context: a numerical solution that also makes sense for the stated situation.

Building an equation from words

Read for quantities, units, totals, differences, and rates. Define the variable before writing an equation. After solving, answer the question in words and check that the result is reasonable in the original situation.

Keep units beside important numbers, use one variable for one clearly defined quantity, and avoid rounding until the final step. A negative count or an impossible time usually signals an incorrect translation.

Common relationships

Consecutive even integers differ by \(2\). If the smaller integer is \(n\), the next is \(n + 2\). Translate any condition using those expressions before solving.

Mixture problems usually need one equation for total amount and another for total value or concentration. Ticket problems use total tickets and total revenue. Motion problems use \(d = rt\); objects traveling toward each other cover a combined distance, while objects moving in the same direction often involve a difference of distances.

Thirty coins are quarters and half-dollars with a total value of $10.50. How many of each coin are there?

Let \(q\) be the number of quarters and \(h\) the number of half-dollars. Both counts must be nonnegative integers. The count and value give two conditions:

\[\begin{cases}q+h=30\\0.25q+0.50h=10.50\end{cases}\]

Solve the count equation for q and substitute into the value equation, keeping both conditions.

\[\begin{cases}q=30-h\\0.25(30-h)+0.50h=10.50\end{cases}\]

\[\begin{cases}q=30-h\\7.50+0.25h=10.50\end{cases}\]

Subtract 7.50 and divide by 0.25 in the second equation, then find q.

\[\begin{cases}q=18\\h=12\end{cases}\]

There are 18 quarters and 12 half-dollars. Both totals check (\(18+12=30\) and \(0.25(18)+0.50(12)=10.50\)).

Two players have 246 hits in total. One has 14 more hits than the other. Find each player’s number of hits.

Two players have a total of \(246\) hits. One has \(14\) more hits than the other. Let \(s\) be the smaller number of hits. Then the larger number is \(s + 14\).

\[s + (s + 14) = 246\]

\[2s + 14 = 246\]

\[s = 116.\]

The other player has \(130\) hits. The check is \(116 + 130 = 246\) and \(130 - 116 = 14\).

Two concert groups visit 178 cities in total, counting each group’s visits separately. One visits 96 more cities than the other. How many does each visit?

Let n be the smaller count; the larger is n+96.

\[n+(n+96)=178\]

\[2n=82\quad\Rightarrow\quad n=41\]

The groups visit 41 and 137 cities. Check: \(41+137=178\) and \(137-41=96\).
