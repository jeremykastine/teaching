Topic 16: Factoring by Grouping

Assigned to: TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic factors four-term polynomials by pairing terms, factoring each pair, and then factoring the common binomial.

Key definitions

Factoring by grouping: a method that creates matching binomial factors from groups of terms. Common binomial factor: an identical two-term factor appearing in each grouped product.

Factoring by grouping

Treat four signed terms as two useful pairs. Factor a GCF from each pair; open parentheses as part of that factoring step rather than enclosing the unfactored pairs first. When the two resulting parenthetical expressions match, factor out that common expression. A negative GCF or a rearrangement of signed terms may be needed.

Factor by grouping.

\[x^{3} + 9x^{2} + 3x + 27\]

Use the first two terms as one pair and the last two terms as the second pair. Do not rewrite those pairs inside parentheses. Instead, determine the GCF of each pair and write each GCF first. The new parentheses are opened only as part of that factoring step.

The GCF of the first group is \(x^{2}\). The GCF of the second group is \(3\).

\[x^{2}(x + 9) + 3(x + 9)\]

The important result is that both terms now contain exactly the same factor, \(x + 9\). Think of the expression above as two terms: one term is \(x^{2}\) times \(x + 9\), and the other is \(3\) times \(x + 9\). The entire quantity \(x + 9\) is their common factor.

\[(x + 9)\left( x^{2} + 3 \right)\]

Factoring by grouping therefore uses the GCF idea twice. First, factor a GCF from each small group. Then factor the matching parenthetical expression from the two resulting terms. Distributing the final product would recreate all four terms in the original expression.

Factor by grouping.

\[18x^{3} - 45x^{2} + 14x - 35\]

Use the first two terms as one pair and the last two terms as the second pair. Again, do not enclose the unfactored pairs in parentheses. The signs inside each new set of parentheses are determined only after we decide which common factor, including its sign, is being removed.

The first group has GCF \(9x^{2}\), and the second group has GCF \(7\). Keep the negative signs with the terms they belong to.

\[9x^{2}(2x - 5) + 7(2x - 5)\]

Both new terms contain the factor \(2x - 5\).

\[(2x - 5)\left( 9x^{2} + 7 \right)\]

The expressions inside the two sets of parentheses must match exactly before they can be factored out as one common factor.

Factor by grouping.

\[x^{3} + 4x^{2} + 7x + 28\]

Factor the GCF from each pair.

\[x^{2}(x + 4) + 7(x + 4)\]

The repeated factor is \(x + 4\).

\[(x + 4)\left( x^{2} + 7 \right)\]

Factor by grouping.

\[14x^{3} - 35x^{2} + 6x - 15\]

The first pair has GCF \(7x^{2}\), and the second pair has GCF \(3\).

\[7x^{2}(2x - 5) + 3(2x - 5)\]

Factor out the matching parenthetical expression.

\[(2x - 5)\left( 7x^{2} + 3 \right)\]

Factor by grouping.

\[8z^3+24z^2-5z-15\]

The first two terms share a factor of \(8z^2\). Factor that out first.

\[8z^2(z+3)-5z-15\]

Factor negative 5 from the last two terms so that the expressions in parentheses match.

\[8z^2(z+3)-5(z+3)\]

Now factor out the common binomial.

\[(z+3)(8z^2-5)\]

Factoring out positive 5 instead would leave \(-z-3\). Choosing negative 5 produces the matching factor directly.

Factor by grouping. Rearrange the terms if needed.

\[4+9x^3-18x^2-2x\]

Rearrange the signed terms so that the cubic and quadratic terms are together, followed by the linear and constant terms. Keep each sign attached to its term.

\[9x^3-18x^2-2x+4\]

Factor \(9x^2\) from the first pair and negative 2 from the second pair.

\[9x^2(x-2)-2(x-2)\]

Factor out the matching binomial.

\[(x-2)(9x^2-2)\]

More challenging coefficients

The next polynomials have leading coefficients and constant terms with many possible factors. Begin by checking for a GCF of the entire polynomial. Then use the GCF of each pair to produce matching binomial factors. Keep signs attached when rearranging terms, and check whether any resulting factor can be factored further.

Factor completely by grouping.

\[36x^3+81x^2+40x+90\]

The leading coefficient 36 has factor pairs \(1\cdot36,\ 2\cdot18,\ 3\cdot12,\ 4\cdot9,\ 6\cdot6\). The constant 90 has factor pairs \(1\cdot90,\ 2\cdot45,\ 3\cdot30,\ 5\cdot18,\ 6\cdot15,\ 9\cdot10\). Grouping uses the other coefficients to identify useful factors instead of guessing among all these combinations.

There is no GCF greater than 1 for all four terms. The first pair has GCF \(9x^2\), and the second has GCF 10.

\[9x^2(4x+9)+10(4x+9)\]

Factor out the matching binomial.

\[(4x+9)(9x^2+10)\]

The quadratic has no GCF and is a sum of positive terms, so it does not factor further over the integers. Check by multiplying.

\[(4x+9)(9x^2+10)=36x^3+81x^2+40x+90\]

Factor completely by grouping.

\[60x^3-144x^2-35x+84\]

Both 60 and 84 have six positive factor pairs, but the four terms have no common factor greater than 1. For the first pair, factor out \(12x^2\). For the second pair, factor out negative 7 so that the binomials match.

\[12x^2(5x-12)-7(5x-12)\]

Removing negative 7 changes the signs inside its parentheses: \(-35x\div(-7)=5x\) and \(84\div(-7)=-12\).

\[(5x-12)(12x^2-7)\]

The quadratic cannot factor further over the integers: its possible roots would require \(x^2=7/12\), which has no rational square root. Check the product.

\[(5x-12)(12x^2-7)=60x^3-144x^2-35x+84\]

Factor completely. Begin with a GCF, then use grouping.

\[72x^3+90x^2+96x+120\]

The leading coefficient 72 has six positive factor pairs, and the constant 120 has eight. First simplify the coefficients by factoring the GCF 6 from the entire polynomial.

\[6(12x^3+15x^2+16x+20)\]

Inside the parentheses, factor \(3x^2\) from the first pair and 4 from the second pair.

\[6\left[3x^2(4x+5)+4(4x+5)\right]\]

Factor out the common binomial and retain the outside factor 6.

\[6(4x+5)(3x^2+4)\]

The quadratic does not factor further over the integers. Check by distributing the binomials and then the outside factor.

\[6(4x+5)(3x^2+4)=6(12x^3+15x^2+16x+20)=72x^3+90x^2+96x+120\]

Factor completely by grouping. Rearrange the terms if needed.

\[144+36x^3-81x^2-64x\]

The cubic coefficient 36 and constant 144 have five and eight positive factor pairs, respectively. There is no GCF greater than 1 for the whole polynomial. Rearrange the signed terms into descending powers.

\[36x^3-81x^2-64x+144\]

Factor \(9x^2\) from the first pair and negative 16 from the second pair.

\[9x^2(4x-9)-16(4x-9)\]

Remove the matching binomial.

\[(4x-9)(9x^2-16)\]

The second factor is a difference of squares: \(9x^2=(3x)^2\) and \(16=4^2\). Continue factoring.

\[(4x-9)(3x-4)(3x+4)\]

Check by multiplying the last two factors first, then distributing the remaining binomial.

\[(4x-9)(3x-4)(3x+4)=(4x-9)(9x^2-16)=36x^3-81x^2-64x+144\]
