Topic 6: Simplifying Square Roots

Assigned to: TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic rewrites square roots in simplest radical form by separating perfect-square factors from the remaining radicand.

Key definitions

Simplest radical form: no perfect-square factor greater than 1 remains under the radical. Perfect-square factor: a factor such as 4, 9, 16, or 25 that has an integer square root.

Simplify the square root.

\[\sqrt{75}\]

The largest perfect-square factor of \(75\) is \(25\).

\[\sqrt{75} = \sqrt{25 \cdot 3}\]

Now evaluate the perfect-square part.

\[\sqrt{75} = \sqrt{25}\sqrt{3} = 5\sqrt{3}.\]

The expression \(5\sqrt{3}\) is simplified because \(3\) has no perfect-square factor greater than \(1\).

When a square root does not evaluate to a whole number

If a radicand is not a perfect square, look for its largest perfect-square factor. Use the product property

\[\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a}\sqrt{b}\]

to separate that factor. The goal is to leave a squarefree number under the radical.

Simplify the square root.

\[\sqrt{90}\]

The largest perfect-square factor of \(90\) is \(9\).

\[\sqrt{90} = \sqrt{9 \cdot 10} = 3\sqrt{10}.\]

Choose the largest perfect-square factor when possible. This avoids creating several small steps that later have to be combined.

Negative radicands

First separate the negative sign as a factor of \(- 1\), and then simplify the positive radical.

\[\sqrt{- 45} = \sqrt{- 1}\sqrt{9 \cdot 5} = 3i\sqrt{5}.\]

Do not write \(\sqrt{- 45} = - \sqrt{45}\). A negative sign outside a radical and a negative radicand have different meanings.

Simplify \(\sqrt{12}\), \(\sqrt{50}\), \(\sqrt{98}\), and \(\sqrt{- 80}\). In each problem, identify the perfect-square factor before simplifying.
