Topic 19: Multiplying and Dividing Fractions

Assigned to: TSM 098 and TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Rearranging multiplication and division

Read an ungrouped chain of multiplication and division from left to right to identify the factors and divisors. Then regroup to make the arithmetic easier, keeping each factor and divisor in its original role. A division can be applied to any one factor of the product. When you divide by a parenthesized expression, treat the whole expression as one divisor.

Using division signs

Evaluate by pairing each divisor with a factor it divides evenly.

\[8\times15\div5\times6\div4\]

Apply the division by 4 to the factor 8 and the division by 5 to the factor 15.

\[(8\div4)\times(15\div5)\times6=2\times3\times6=36\]

Evaluate by combining the divisions by 4 and 3. Which factor can you divide by their product?

\[9\div4\times48\div3\times5\]

Dividing by 4 and by 3 has the same effect as dividing by \(4\times3=12\). Apply that division to 48.

\[9\times\bigl(48\div(4\times3)\bigr)\times5=9\times4\times5=180\]

Evaluate by rewriting the division by the parenthesized quotient. Look for two divisors you can combine.

\[3\times4\div5\times6\div7\div(2\div70)\]

Dividing by \(2\div70\) reverses the effect of multiplying by 2 and dividing by 70. Rewrite it as multiplying by 70 and dividing by 2.

\[3\times4\div5\times6\div7\times70\div2\]

Combine the divisions by 5 and 7 into division by 35 and apply it to 70. Apply the division by 2 to the factor 4.

\[3\times(4\div2)\times6\times(70\div35)=3\times2\times6\times2=72\]

Using fraction bars

A fraction bar represents division and groups the expressions above and below it. In a product of fractions, a denominator factor can divide a numerator factor from any of the fractions.

Evaluate by pairing each denominator with a numerator it divides evenly.

\[\frac{14}{9}\times\frac{27}{4}\times\frac{8}{7}\]

Pair 14 with 7, 27 with 9, and 8 with 4.

\[\frac{14}{7}\times\frac{27}{9}\times\frac{8}{4}=2\times3\times2=12\]

Evaluate by combining the two denominator factors and dividing one numerator factor by their product.

\[\frac{11}{4}\times\frac{60}{5}\times3\]

The denominators 4 and 5 give a combined divisor of 20. Apply it to 60.

\[11\times\frac{60}{4\times5}\times3=11\times3\times3=99\]

Evaluate. Use the long fraction bar to identify the entire divisor.

\[\frac{\frac{5}{6}\times\frac{8}{7}}{\frac{4}{42}}\]

The long bar divides the entire product by \(\frac{4}{42}\). Rewrite that division as multiplication by \(\frac{42}{4}\).

\[\frac{5}{6}\times\frac{8}{7}\times\frac{42}{4}\]

Combine 6 and 7 and use their product to divide 42. Use the remaining divisor, 4, to divide 8.

\[5\times\frac{8}{4}\times\frac{42}{6\times7}=5\times2\times1=10\]

Overview

This topic multiplies and divides numerical fractions, with emphasis on using reciprocals and simplifying factors before multiplication when useful.

Key definitions

Reciprocal: a fraction formed by interchanging numerator and denominator. Cross-cancellation: simplifying common factors from different numerator and denominator positions before multiplying. Complex fraction: a fraction containing a fraction in its numerator or denominator.

Multiplying fractions

Multiply numerator factors together and denominator factors together. Before multiplying, reduce any numerator factor with any denominator factor by their common factor. A nonzero factor divided by itself becomes 1. Early reduction keeps the arithmetic small. If every numerator factor reduces to 1, keep a 1 above the fraction bar. A denominator of 1 may be omitted, but a numerator of 1 must remain.

Multiply and simplify.

\(\frac{7}{6} \cdot \frac{1}{7}\)

The seven in a numerator and the seven in a denominator have a common factor of seven. Divide both sevens by seven. Each becomes one.

\(\frac{1}{6} \cdot \frac{1}{1}\)

Now multiply the remaining numerator factors and denominator factors.

\(\frac{1}{6}\)

Multiply and simplify.

\(\frac{12}{7} \cdot \frac{9}{8}\)

Twelve and eight have a common factor of four. Divide twelve by four to get three, and divide eight by four to get two.

\(\frac{3}{7} \cdot \frac{9}{2}\)

No numerator factor shares another factor with a denominator factor. Multiply across.

\(\frac{27}{14}\)

The numerator and denominator have no common factor greater than one, so the product is already in lowest terms.

Multiply and give the answer in lowest terms.

\(\frac{2}{3} \cdot \frac{9}{10} \cdot \frac{5}{8}\)

Begin with one clear reduction. Two and ten share a factor of two. Replace the two with one and the ten with five.

\(\frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{9}{5} \cdot \frac{5}{8}\)

Nine and three share a factor of three. Divide both by three.

\(\frac{1}{1} \cdot \frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{5}{8}\)

The five in a numerator and the five in a denominator now reduce completely. Each becomes one.

\(\frac{1}{1} \cdot \frac{3}{1} \cdot \frac{1}{8}\)

Only one nontrivial numerator factor and one nontrivial denominator factor remain.

\(\frac{3}{8}\)

The order of the reductions could be different. What matters is that every displayed product has the same value as the original product and that each changed factor is replaced by the correct quotient.

Multiply and give the answer in lowest terms.

\(\frac{39}{25} \cdot \frac{10}{63} \cdot \frac{27}{26}\)

Thirty-nine and twenty-six share a factor of thirteen. Divide both by thirteen.

\(\frac{3}{25} \cdot \frac{10}{63} \cdot \frac{27}{2}\)

Ten and twenty-five share a factor of five. Divide both by five.

\(\frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{2}{63} \cdot \frac{27}{2}\)

The remaining two in a numerator and the two in a denominator reduce completely.

\(\frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{1}{63} \cdot \frac{27}{1}\)

Twenty-seven and sixty-three share a factor of nine. Divide twenty-seven by nine to get three, and divide sixty-three by nine to get seven.

\(\frac{3}{5} \cdot \frac{1}{7} \cdot \frac{3}{1}\)

Multiply the factors that remain.

\(\frac{9}{35}\)

Although the original product looked complicated, the arithmetic at the end is small because the common factors were removed before multiplication.

Multiply and write the answer in lowest terms.

\[30\cdot\frac{2}{5}\]

A whole number can be written as a fraction with denominator one. This allows the same multiplication and reduction method to be used.

\(\frac{30}{1} \cdot \frac{2}{5}\)

Thirty and five share a factor of five. Divide thirty by five to get six, and divide five by five to get one.

\(\frac{6}{1} \cdot \frac{2}{1}\)

\[6\cdot2=12.\]

Multiply and give the answer in lowest terms.

\[\frac{6}{35}\cdot\frac{7}{18}\]

The numerators begin with no factors equal to 1. Reduce six and eighteen by six, then seven and thirty-five by seven.

\[\frac{1}{5}\cdot\frac{1}{3}=\frac{1\cdot1}{5\cdot3}=\frac{1}{15}\]

Every numerator factor has become 1. Their product is 1, so it stays above the fraction bar.

Dividing fractions

To divide by a fraction, leave the first number as it is, change division to multiplication, and replace the divisor with its reciprocal. The reciprocal is formed by switching the numerator and denominator of the fraction being divided by. After that rewrite, the problem is an ordinary fraction multiplication problem.

Divide and simplify.

\(\frac{9}{8} \div \frac{18}{5}\)

The divisor is eighteen fifths, so its reciprocal is five eighteenths.

\(\frac{9}{8} \cdot \frac{5}{18}\)

Nine and eighteen share a factor of nine. Divide both by nine.

\(\frac{1}{8} \cdot \frac{5}{2}\)

Multiply across.

\(\frac{5}{16}\)

Divide and simplify.

\(\frac{7}{2} \div \frac{1}{2}\)

Replace one half with its reciprocal, two over one, and change division to multiplication.

\(\frac{7}{2} \cdot \frac{2}{1}\)

The two in the numerator and the two in the denominator reduce completely.

\(\frac{7}{1} \cdot \frac{1}{1}\)

The result is a whole number.

\(7\)

Divide and write the answer in lowest terms.

\[28\div\frac{4}{5}\]

Write the whole number with denominator one.

\[\frac{28}{1}\div\frac{4}{5}\]

Change division to multiplication and replace four fifths with five fourths.

\(\frac{28}{1} \cdot \frac{5}{4}\)

Twenty-eight and four share a factor of four. Divide both by four.

\(\frac{7}{1} \cdot \frac{5}{1}\)

Multiply the remaining factors.

\(35\)

Divide and write the answer in lowest terms.

\[\frac{5}{3}\div5\]

A whole-number divisor may also be written with denominator one.

\(\frac{5}{3} \div 5\)

Replace the divisor with its reciprocal and change division to multiplication.

\(\frac{5}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{5}\)

The two factors of five reduce completely.

\(\frac{1}{3} \cdot \frac{1}{1}\)

Multiply the remaining factors.

\(\frac{1}{3}\)

Divide and give the answer in lowest terms.

\[\frac{14}{15}\div\frac{28}{5}\]

Keep the first fraction and multiply by the reciprocal of the second.

\[\frac{14}{15}\cdot\frac{5}{28}\]

Reduce fourteen and twenty-eight by fourteen, then five and fifteen by five.

\[\frac{1}{3}\cdot\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{6}\]

The numerator is 1 after cancellation; it does not disappear.

Complex fractions

A long fraction bar also represents division. When an entire fraction appears below that bar, first rewrite the complex fraction as a division problem.

Simplify the complex fraction.

\[\frac{12}{\frac{4}{5}}\]

\(\frac{12}{\frac{4}{5}}\)

The numerator is twelve, and the denominator is four fifths. The expression therefore means twelve divided by four fifths.

\(\frac{12}{1} \div \frac{4}{5}\)

Change division to multiplication and use the reciprocal of four fifths.

\(\frac{12}{1} \cdot \frac{5}{4}\)

Twelve and four share a factor of four. Divide both by four.

\(\frac{3}{1} \cdot \frac{5}{1}\)

\[3\cdot5=15.\]

Simplify the complex fraction.

\[\frac{\frac{9}{14}}{\frac{27}{7}}\]

The long fraction bar means division. Rewrite, then take the reciprocal of the divisor.

\[\frac{9}{14}\div\frac{27}{7}=\frac{9}{14}\cdot\frac{7}{27}\]

Reduce nine and twenty-seven by nine, and seven and fourteen by seven.

\[\frac{1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{3}=\frac{1}{6}\]
