Topic 13: Greatest Common Divisor (GCD)

Assigned to: TSM 098 and TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Find greatest common divisors of whole numbers and greatest common factors of terms with variables. For every problem, use both methods: listing factors and prime factorization.

Key definitions

Common factor or common divisor: a factor shared by every given number or algebraic term. Greatest common factor (GCF), also called greatest common divisor (GCD): the common factor that includes all shared numerical and variable factors, with the smallest exponent shared by every term. For whole numbers, this is the largest positive common factor.

GCF and GCD mean the same thing

In this course, greatest common factor (GCF) and greatest common divisor (GCD) mean the same thing. We use the terms interchangeably for both whole numbers and algebraic terms, including terms with variables. Either name asks for the common factor containing all the numerical and variable factors shared by every given number or term. Use both methods: listing factors and prime factorization.

Find the GCD of 12, 20. Use both methods: listing factors and prime factorization.

Listing factors: The factors of 12 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. The factors of 20 are 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20. The common factors are 1, 2, 4, so the GCD is 4.

Prime factorization: \(12=2^{2}\cdot3\); \(20=2^{2}\cdot5\). Keep only factors shared by every number, using the smallest shared exponent. The GCD is \(4\).

Find the GCD of 90 and 126. Use both methods: listing factors and prime factorization.

Listing factors: The factors of 90 are 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 9, 10, 15, 18, 30, 45, 90. The factors of 126 are 1, 2, 3, 6, 7, 9, 14, 18, 21, 42, 63, 126. The common factors are 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, and 18, so the GCD is 18.

Prime factorization: \(90=2\cdot3^2\cdot5\) and \(126=2\cdot3^2\cdot7\). Thus \(GCD(90,126)=2\cdot3^2=18\).

Find the GCD of 50, 60, 70. Use both methods: listing factors and prime factorization.

Listing factors: The factors of 50 are 1, 2, 5, 10, 25, 50. The factors of 60 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30, 60. The factors of 70 are 1, 2, 5, 7, 10, 14, 35, 70. The common factors are 1, 2, 5, 10, so the GCD is 10.

Prime factorization: \(50=2\cdot5^{2}\); \(60=2^{2}\cdot3\cdot5\); \(70=2\cdot5\cdot7\). Keep only factors shared by every number, using the smallest shared exponent. The GCD is \(10\).

Find the GCD of 6, 10, 15. Use both methods: listing factors and prime factorization.

Listing factors: The factors of 6 are 1, 2, 3, 6. The factors of 10 are 1, 2, 5, 10. The factors of 15 are 1, 3, 5, 15. The common factors are 1, so the GCD is 1.

Prime factorization: \(6=2\cdot3\); \(10=2\cdot5\); \(15=3\cdot5\). Keep only factors shared by every number, using the smallest shared exponent. The GCD is \(1\).

Find the GCD of 120, 94. Use both methods: listing factors and prime factorization.

Listing factors: The factors of 120 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8, 10, 12, 15, 20, 24, 30, 40, 60, 120. The factors of 94 are 1, 2, 47, 94. The common factors are 1, 2, so the GCD is 2.

Prime factorization: \(120=2^{3}\cdot3\cdot5\); \(94=2\cdot47\). Keep only factors shared by every number, using the smallest shared exponent. The GCD is \(2\).

Finding the GCF

Find the numerical GCF of the coefficients. Include a variable only if it occurs in every term, using the smallest exponent shared by the terms.

Find the GCF of \(20x, 45\). Use both methods: listing factors and prime factorization.

Listing factors: The factors of 20 are 1, 2, 4, 5, 10, 20. The factors of 45 are 1, 3, 5, 9, 15, 45. The greatest common numerical factor is 5. No variable factor is shared because one term has no x. The GCF is \(5\).

Prime factorization: \(20x=2^{2}\cdot5\cdotx\); \(45=3^{2}\cdot5\). Compare prime factors of the coefficients and repeated variable factors; keep only shared factors with the smallest shared exponent. The GCF is \(5\).

Find the GCF of \(110x^{3}, 70x^{4}, 60x^{7}\). Use both methods: listing factors and prime factorization.

Listing factors: The factors of 110 are 1, 2, 5, 10, 11, 22, 55, 110. The factors of 70 are 1, 2, 5, 7, 10, 14, 35, 70. The factors of 60 are 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 10, 12, 15, 20, 30, 60. The greatest common numerical factor is 10. The common variable factors are \(1, x, x^{2}, x^{3}\). The GCF is \(10x^{3}\).

Prime factorization: \(110x^{3}=2\cdot5\cdot11\cdotx^{3}\); \(70x^{4}=2\cdot5\cdot7\cdotx^{4}\); \(60x^{7}=2^{2}\cdot3\cdot5\cdotx^{7}\). Compare prime factors of the coefficients and repeated variable factors; keep only shared factors with the smallest shared exponent. The GCF is \(10x^{3}\).

Find the GCF of \(10m^{5}, 40m^{9}\). Use both methods: listing factors and prime factorization.

Listing factors: The factors of 10 are 1, 2, 5, 10. The factors of 40 are 1, 2, 4, 5, 8, 10, 20, 40. The greatest common numerical factor is 10. The common variable factors are \(1, m, m^{2}, m^{3}, m^{4}, m^{5}\). The GCF is \(10m^{5}\).

Prime factorization: \(10m^{5}=2\cdot5\cdotm^{5}\); \(40m^{9}=2^{3}\cdot5\cdotm^{9}\). Compare prime factors of the coefficients and repeated variable factors; keep only shared factors with the smallest shared exponent. The GCF is \(10m^{5}\).
