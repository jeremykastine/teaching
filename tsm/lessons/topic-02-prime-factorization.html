Topic 2: Prime Factorization

Assigned to: TSM 098 and TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic breaks composite numbers into prime factors so later work with fractions, common factors, and common multiples can be organized efficiently.

Key definitions

Prime number: a whole number greater than 1 with exactly two positive factors. Composite number: a whole number greater than 1 with more than two positive factors. Prime factorization: a product of prime numbers equal to the original number.

Find the prime factorization of 100.

Begin with any convenient factor pair. Twenty and five work well.

\[100\ = \ 20\ \cdot \ 5\]

Five is already prime, but twenty is composite. Break twenty into two and ten.

\[100\ = \ 2\ \cdot \ 10\ \cdot \ 5\]

Ten is still composite, so break it into two and five.

\[100\ = \ 2\ \cdot \ 2\ \cdot \ 5\ \cdot \ 5\]

Every factor is now prime. This is the prime factorization without exponents. Repeated prime factors may be compressed with exponents.

\[100\ = \ 2^{2}\ \cdot \ 5^{2}\]

This topic is built from three related ideas: factors, prime factorizations, and common factors or multiples. The words sound similar, but they ask different questions. A factor divides into a number. A multiple is produced by multiplying a number by a whole number. Prime factorization takes a number apart into its prime building blocks.

A prime number has exactly two positive factors: one and itself. A composite number can be broken into smaller whole-number factors. The number one is neither prime nor composite, so one is never included as a prime factor.

A factor tree is one way to organize the work. Break a composite number into any convenient pair of factors, and then keep breaking every composite factor. Stop only when every factor at the ends of the branches is prime. Different trees may begin differently, but they lead to the same collection of prime factors.

Find the prime factorization of 78.

Seventy-eight is even, so two is an easy first factor.

\[78\ = \ 2\ \cdot \ 39\]

Thirty-nine is composite because it is three times thirteen. Both three and thirteen are prime.

\[78\ = \ 2\ \cdot \ 3\ \cdot \ 13\]

No prime factor repeats, so exponent notation does not shorten this factorization.

Find the prime factorization of 125.

A number ending in five is divisible by five. Split 125 into five and twenty-five.

\[125\ = \ 5\ \cdot \ 25\]

Twenty-five is five times five.

\[125\ = \ 5\ \cdot \ 5\ \cdot \ 5\]

There are three copies of the prime factor five.

\[125\ = \ 5^{3}\]

Find the prime factorization of 90.

Ninety is even, so begin by taking out a factor of two.

\[90\ = \ 2\ \cdot \ 45\]

Forty-five is five times nine.

\[90\ = \ 2\ \cdot \ 5\ \cdot \ 9\]

Nine is three times three.

\[90\ = \ 2\ \cdot \ 5\ \cdot \ 3\ \cdot \ 3\]

The order of factors does not affect the product. Prime factorizations are usually written from the smallest prime to the largest.

\[90\ = \ 2\ \cdot \ 3\ \cdot \ 3\ \cdot \ 5\]

The repeated threes may be written with an exponent.

\[90\ = \ 2\ \cdot \ 3^{2}\ \cdot \ 5\]

When the original number is already prime

Eleven cannot be broken into smaller whole-number factors other than one and eleven. Since one is not a prime factor, the prime factorization of eleven is simply eleven itself.

\[11\ = \ 11\]

Fifteen is composite, so it does break into smaller prime factors.

\[15\ = \ 3\ \cdot \ 5\]
