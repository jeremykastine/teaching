Topic 17: Factoring by the Sum-Product Method with Leading Coefficients Greater Than 1

Assigned to: TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic factors trinomials whose leading coefficient is greater than 1 by splitting the middle term and using grouping.

Key definitions

Sum-product method: find two numbers whose sum is the middle coefficient and whose product is the product of the leading coefficient and constant. Split the middle term: replace one middle term with two terms having the required sum.

Factoring with a larger leading coefficient

First remove any GCF. For a remaining trinomial \(ax^2+bx+c\), where x is the variable and a, b, and c are fixed integer coefficients, find two integers with sum \(b\) and product \(ac\). Use them to split the middle term, then factor by grouping. They are not automatically the constants in the final binomial factors. Check that every factor is complete.

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[3b^{2}\ - \ 15b\ + \ 18\]

Every coefficient is divisible by 3, so factor out 3 first:

\[3b^{2}\ - \ 15b\ + \ 18\]

\[= \ 3(b^{2}\ - \ 5b\ + \ 6)\]

Inside the parentheses, the leading coefficient is now 1. We need two integers whose sum is -5 and whose product is 6. The numbers -3 and -2 work (\(( - 3)\ + \ ( - 2)\ = \ - 5\); \(( - 3)( - 2)\ = \ 6\)).

Because the trinomial inside the parentheses has leading coefficient 1, we may use the Topic 15 shortcut and place -3 and -2 directly into the factors:

\[3b^{2}\ - \ 15b\ + \ 18\ = \ 3(b\ - \ 3)(b\ - \ 2)\]

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[6c^{2}\ + \ 35c\ - \ 6\]

There is no common factor other than 1. Multiply the leading coefficient by the constant term (\(6( - 6)\ = \ - 36\)).

We need two integers whose sum is 35 and whose product is -36. Because the product is negative, the signs must be different. The numbers 36 and -1 work (\(36\ + \ ( - 1)\ = \ 35\); \(36( - 1)\ = \ - 36\)).

Use those numbers to split 35c into 36c - c. Then factor the four terms by grouping:

\[6c^{2}\ + \ 35c\ - \ 6\]

\[= \ 6c^{2}\ + \ 36c\ - \ c\ - \ 6\]

\[= \ 6c(c\ + \ 6)\ - \ 1(c\ + \ 6)\]

\[= \ (c\ + \ 6)(6c\ - \ 1)\]

The pair 36 and -1 split the middle term. The actual binomial factors are created only after grouping, which is why we cannot simply write c + 36 and c - 1 as the answer.

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[15b^{2}\ - \ 19b\ + \ 6\]

The product of the leading coefficient and the constant is 90. We need two integers with a sum of -19 and a product of positive 90. Both numbers must therefore be negative. The pair -10 and -9 works (\(( - 10)\ + \ ( - 9)\ = \ - 19\); \(( - 10)( - 9)\ = \ 90\)).

Split the middle term and group:

\[15b^{2}\ - \ 19b\ + \ 6\]

\[= \ 15b^{2}\ - \ 10b\ - \ 9b\ + \ 6\]

\[= \ 5b(3b\ - \ 2)\ - \ 3(3b\ - \ 2)\]

\[= \ (3b\ - \ 2)(5b\ - \ 3)\]

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[8s^{2}\ - \ 30s\ + \ 18\]

The coefficients share a greatest common factor of 2. Factoring it out first gives:

\[8s^{2}\ - \ 30s\ + \ 18\]

\[= \ 2(4s^{2}\ - \ 15s\ + \ 9)\]

For the trinomial inside the parentheses, multiply 4 by 9. We need two integers with a sum of -15 and a product of 36. The numbers -12 and -3 work (\(( - 12)\ + \ ( - 3)\ = \ - 15\); \(( - 12)( - 3)\ = \ 36\)).

Split the middle term and factor by grouping, keeping the original factor of 2 outside:

\[2(4s^{2}\ - \ 15s\ + \ 9)\]

\[= \ 2(4s^{2}\ - \ 12s\ - \ 3s\ + \ 9)\]

\[= \ 2\lbrack 4s(s\ - \ 3)\ - \ 3(s\ - \ 3)\rbrack\]

\[= \ 2(s\ - \ 3)(4s\ - \ 3)\]

It is possible to begin without removing the GCF. That route produces (s - 3)(8s - 6), but the second binomial still contains a factor of 2. We would then have to continue and rewrite it as 2(s - 3)(4s - 3). Factoring out the GCF first makes the work easier and helps ensure that the final answer is completely factored.

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[-7x^2-50x-7\]

First factor out negative 1 to make the leading coefficient positive.

\[-(7x^2+50x+7)\]

The product of the leading coefficient and constant is 49. The numbers 49 and 1 have product 49 and sum 50. Use them to split the middle term.

\[-(7x^2+49x+x+7)\]

Factor each pair, keeping the initial negative sign outside.

\[-\bigl[7x(x+7)+1(x+7)\bigr]\]

\[-(x+7)(7x+1)\]

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[49r^{2}\ + \ 27r\ + \ 4\]

There is no common factor. Multiply the leading coefficient by the constant (\(49(4)=196\)).

We would need two integers with a sum of 27 and a product of 196. No integer pair satisfies both conditions. Although 49r squared and 4 are both perfect squares, the middle coefficient is 27 rather than the 28 that would appear in a perfect-square trinomial. Therefore the trinomial does not factor over the integers, and we leave it as written.

\[49r^{2}\ + \ 27r\ + \ 4\]

More challenging factor choices

These trinomials have more possible factors in both the leading coefficient and the constant term. After removing any GCF, use the sum-product method to organize the search: find a signed factor pair of the product of the leading coefficient and constant whose sum is the middle coefficient. Then split the middle term and group.

Factor completely over the integers using the sum-product method and grouping.

\[36x^2+121x+90\]

There is no GCF greater than 1. The leading coefficient 36 has five positive factor pairs, and the constant 90 has six. Rather than trying every combination of binomial factors, find two integers with product \(36(90)=3240\) and sum 121.

Both numbers must be positive. Some factor pairs are 30 and 108 (sum 138), 36 and 90 (sum 126), and 40 and 81 (sum 121). The last pair works because \(40+81=121\) and \(40(81)=3240\).

Split the middle term, then factor the GCF from each pair.

\[36x^2+81x+40x+90\]

\[9x(4x+9)+10(4x+9)\]

Factor out the matching binomial.

\[(4x+9)(9x+10)\]

The numbers 81 and 40 split the middle term; they are not the constants in the final binomials. Check by multiplying and combining the middle terms.

\[(4x+9)(9x+10)=36x^2+40x+81x+90=36x^2+121x+90\]

Factor completely over the integers using the sum-product method and grouping.

\[60x^2-109x-84\]

There is no GCF greater than 1. Both 60 and 84 have six positive factor pairs. We need two integers with product \(60(-84)=-5040\) and sum -109.

The signs must be opposite, with the larger size negative. Factor pairs of 5040 include 30 and 168 (difference 138), 35 and 144 (difference 109), and 36 and 140 (difference 104). Choose -144 and 35: \(-144+35=-109\) and \((-144)(35)=-5040\).

Split the middle term.

\[60x^2-144x+35x-84\]

Factor \(12x\) from the first pair and 7 from the second pair.

\[12x(5x-12)+7(5x-12)\]

\[(5x-12)(12x+7)\]

Check the signs and the middle coefficient by multiplying.

\[(5x-12)(12x+7)=60x^2+35x-144x-84=60x^2-109x-84\]

Factor completely over the integers using the sum-product method and grouping.

\[72x^2-199x+120\]

There is no GCF greater than 1. The leading coefficient 72 has six positive factor pairs, and 120 has eight. The required product is \(72(120)=8640\), and the required sum is -199.

Both numbers must be negative. Among positive factor pairs of 8640, 60 and 144 sum to 204, 64 and 135 sum to 199, and 72 and 120 sum to 192. Thus choose -135 and -64: \(-135+(-64)=-199\) and \((-135)(-64)=8640\).

Split the middle term, then remove \(9x\) from the first pair and negative 8 from the second pair.

\[72x^2-135x-64x+120\]

\[9x(8x-15)-8(8x-15)\]

Factoring out negative 8 creates the same binomial as in the first group.

\[(8x-15)(9x-8)\]

Check by multiplying.

\[(8x-15)(9x-8)=72x^2-64x-135x+120=72x^2-199x+120\]

Factor completely over the integers. Begin with a GCF, then use the sum-product method and grouping.

\[96x^2+188x+90\]

First remove the GCF 2 from the entire trinomial.

\[2(48x^2+94x+45)\]

The remaining leading coefficient 48 has five positive factor pairs, and the constant 45 has three. For this smaller trinomial, the required product is \(48(45)=2160\), and the required sum is 94. Both numbers are positive.

Factor pairs of 2160 include 30 and 72 (sum 102), 36 and 60 (sum 96), and 40 and 54 (sum 94). Choose 54 and 40, since \(54+40=94\) and \(54(40)=2160\).

Split the middle term inside the parentheses, keeping the outside factor 2.

\[2(48x^2+54x+40x+45)\]

\[2\left[6x(8x+9)+5(8x+9)\right]\]

\[2(8x+9)(6x+5)\]

Both binomials are fully factored. Check the product, including the outside factor.

\[2(8x+9)(6x+5)=2(48x^2+94x+45)=96x^2+188x+90\]
