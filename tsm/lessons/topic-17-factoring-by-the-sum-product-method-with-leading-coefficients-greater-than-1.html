Topic 17: Factoring by the Sum-Product Method with Leading Coefficients Greater Than 1

Assigned to: TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic factors trinomials whose leading coefficient is greater than 1 by splitting the middle term and using grouping.

Key definitions

Sum-product method: find two numbers whose sum is the middle coefficient and whose product is the product of the leading coefficient and constant. Split the middle term: replace one middle term with two terms having the required sum.

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[3b^{2}\ - \ 15b\ + \ 18\]

Every coefficient is divisible by 3, so factor out 3 first:

\[3b^{2}\ - \ 15b\ + \ 18\]

\[= \ 3(b^{2}\ - \ 5b\ + \ 6)\]

Inside the parentheses, the leading coefficient is now 1. We need two integers whose sum is -5 and whose product is 6. The numbers -3 and -2 work:

\[( - 3)\ + \ ( - 2)\ = \ - 5\]

\[( - 3)( - 2)\ = \ 6\]

Because the trinomial inside the parentheses has leading coefficient 1, we may use the B9 shortcut and place -3 and -2 directly into the factors:

\[3b^{2}\ - \ 15b\ + \ 18\ = \ 3(b\ - \ 3)(b\ - \ 2)\]

Why this topic requires an additional step

In Topic B9, the coefficient of the squared term was 1. Once we found two integers with the correct sum and product, we could place those numbers directly into two binomial factors. That shortcut does not work in exactly the same way when the leading coefficient is greater than 1, because the first terms of the two factors must now multiply to something other than x squared.

For a trinomial in the form

\[ax^{2}\ + \ bx\ + \ c\]

we will still search for two integers whose sum is the middle coefficient b. However, their product must now be the product of the leading coefficient and the constant term:

\[m\ + \ n\ = \ b\]

\[mn\ = \ ac\]

Those two integers are used to split the middle term. Once the trinomial has four terms, we factor by grouping.

\[ax^{2}\ + \ bx\ + \ c\]

\[= \ ax^{2}\ + \ mx\ + \ nx\ + \ c\]

Always check for a greatest common factor first

Before using the new method, look for a factor shared by every term. Removing the greatest common factor makes the remaining trinomial smaller and may reduce it to the easier B9 form.

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[6c^{2}\ + \ 35c\ - \ 6\]

There is no common factor other than 1. Multiply the leading coefficient by the constant term:

\[6( - 6)\ = \ - 36\]

We need two integers whose sum is 35 and whose product is -36. Because the product is negative, the signs must be different. The numbers 36 and -1 work:

\[36\ + \ ( - 1)\ = \ 35\]

\[36( - 1)\ = \ - 36\]

Use those numbers to split 35c into 36c - c. Then factor the four terms by grouping:

\[6c^{2}\ + \ 35c\ - \ 6\]

\[= \ 6c^{2}\ + \ 36c\ - \ c\ - \ 6\]

\[= \ 6c(c\ + \ 6)\ - \ 1(c\ + \ 6)\]

\[= \ (c\ + \ 6)(6c\ - \ 1)\]

The pair 36 and -1 split the middle term. The actual binomial factors are created only after grouping, which is why we cannot simply write c + 36 and c - 1 as the answer.

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[15b^{2}\ - \ 19b\ + \ 6\]

The product of the leading coefficient and the constant is 90. We need two integers with a sum of -19 and a product of positive 90. Both numbers must therefore be negative. The pair -10 and -9 works:

\[( - 10)\ + \ ( - 9)\ = \ - 19\]

\[( - 10)( - 9)\ = \ 90\]

Split the middle term and group:

\[15b^{2}\ - \ 19b\ + \ 6\]

\[= \ 15b^{2}\ - \ 10b\ - \ 9b\ + \ 6\]

\[= \ 5b(3b\ - \ 2)\ - \ 3(3b\ - \ 2)\]

\[= \ (3b\ - \ 2)(5b\ - \ 3)\]

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[8s^{2}\ - \ 30s\ + \ 18\]

The coefficients share a greatest common factor of 2. Factoring it out first gives:

\[8s^{2}\ - \ 30s\ + \ 18\]

\[= \ 2(4s^{2}\ - \ 15s\ + \ 9)\]

For the trinomial inside the parentheses, multiply 4 by 9. We need two integers with a sum of -15 and a product of 36. The numbers -12 and -3 work:

\[( - 12)\ + \ ( - 3)\ = \ - 15\]

\[( - 12)( - 3)\ = \ 36\]

Split the middle term and factor by grouping, keeping the original factor of 2 outside:

\[2(4s^{2}\ - \ 15s\ + \ 9)\]

\[= \ 2(4s^{2}\ - \ 12s\ - \ 3s\ + \ 9)\]

\[= \ 2\lbrack 4s(s\ - \ 3)\ - \ 3(s\ - \ 3)\rbrack\]

\[= \ 2(s\ - \ 3)(4s\ - \ 3)\]

It is possible to begin without removing the GCF. That route produces (s - 3)(8s - 6), but the second binomial still contains a factor of 2. We would then have to continue and rewrite it as 2(s - 3)(4s - 3). Factoring out the GCF first makes the work easier and helps ensure that the final answer is completely factored.

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[- 7a^{2}\ - \ 50ab\ - \ 7b^{2}\]

If the squared term has a negative coefficient, it is usually easier to factor out -1 before doing anything else. This changes all the signs inside the parentheses and gives us a positive leading coefficient.

First factor out -1:

\[- 7a^{2}\ - \ 50ab\ - \ 7b^{2}\]

\[= \ - (7a^{2}\ + \ 50ab\ + \ 7b^{2})\]

Inside the parentheses, the product of the first and last coefficients is 49. The numbers 49 and 1 have a sum of 50, so they split the middle term:

\[- (7a^{2}\ + \ 49ab\ + \ ab\ + \ 7b^{2})\]

\[= \ - \lbrack 7a(a\ + \ 7b)\ + \ b(a\ + \ 7b)\rbrack\]

\[= \ - (a\ + \ 7b)(7a\ + \ b)\]

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[49r^{2}\ + \ 27r\ + \ 4\]

There is no common factor. The product of the leading coefficient and the constant is:

\[49(4)\ = \ 196\]

We would need two integers with a sum of 27 and a product of 196. No integer pair satisfies both conditions. Although 49r squared and 4 are both perfect squares, the middle coefficient is 27 rather than the 28 that would appear in a perfect-square trinomial. Therefore the trinomial does not factor over the integers, and we leave it as written.

\[49r^{2}\ + \ 27r\ + \ 4\]

  1. Arrange the trinomial in descending order.

  2. Factor out the GCF. If the leading coefficient is negative, include -1 in that factor.

  3. Multiply the leading coefficient by the constant term.

  4. Find two integers whose sum is b and whose product is ac.

  5. Use those integers to split the middle term.

  6. Factor the four resulting terms by grouping.

  7. Confirm that every factor is complete. If no integer pair works, the trinomial does not factor over the integers.
