Topic 27: Solving Quadratic Equations by All Three Methods

Assigned to: TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic solves quadratic equations by factoring, taking square roots, or using the quadratic formula, choosing the method that fits the equation.

Key definitions

Quadratic equation: an equation that can be written as ax squared + bx + c = 0 with a not equal to 0. Zero-product property: if a product is 0, at least one factor is 0. Discriminant: b squared minus 4ac. Quadratic formula: the formula that gives the solutions from a, b, and c.

Choosing a method

A quadratic equation can be solved by factoring, by taking square roots, or by using the quadratic formula. First write the equation in a useful form. Factoring requires one side to equal zero. The square-root method works best when a squared expression can be isolated. The quadratic formula works for every equation in standard form \(ax^{2} + bx + c = 0\), where \(a \neq 0\).

Solve by factoring.

\[x^2-5x+6=0\]

\[(x - 2)(x - 3) = 0.\]

By the zero-product property, at least one factor must equal zero.

\[x - 2 = 0\quad\text{or}\quad x - 3 = 0\]

\[x = 2\quad\text{or}\quad x = 3.\]

Solve by taking square roots. Give exact answers.

\[(x-4)^2=75\]

\[x - 4 = \pm \sqrt{75} = \pm 5\sqrt{3}\]

\[x = 4 \pm 5\sqrt{3}.\]

Both signs are needed because either value of \(x-4\) has square 75.

Solve \(x^2=-64\).

Take both square roots and use the imaginary unit.

\[x=\pm\sqrt{-64}=\pm8i\]

The quadratic formula

For \(ax^2+bx+c=0\) with \(a\ne0\), the quadratic formula gives \(x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\).

The discriminant

For integer coefficients, the discriminant \(b^2-4ac\) predicts the solutions: positive gives two real solutions (rational for a perfect square, irrational otherwise); zero gives one repeated real solution; negative gives two nonreal complex solutions. Use parentheses when substituting negative coefficients.

Solve using the quadratic formula. Give exact answers.

\[2x^2+6x+3=0\]

In standard form, \(a=2\), \(b=6\), and \(c=3\).

\[x = \frac{- 6 \pm \sqrt{6^{2} - 4(2)(3)}}{2(2)} = \frac{- 6 \pm \sqrt{12}}{4} = \frac{- 3 \pm \sqrt{3}}{2}.\]

Use parentheses around substituted negative coefficients.

Solve \(x^2+x-12=0\) by factoring and check.

\[(x+4)(x-3)=0\]

\[x=-4\quad\text{or}\quad x=3\]

Checks: \(16-4-12=0\) and \(9+3-12=0\).

Solve \(3(x+1)^2=48\) by square roots and check.

\[(x+1)^2=16\]

\[x+1=\pm4\]

\[x=3\quad\text{or}\quad x=-5\]

Both values make \((x+1)^2=16\), so the original left side is 48.

Solve \(5x^2+2x-3=0\) using the quadratic formula and check.

Use \(a=5,b=2,c=-3\).

\[x=\frac{-2\pm\sqrt{2^2-4(5)(-3)}}{10}=\frac{-2\pm8}{10}\]

\[x=\frac35\quad\text{or}\quad x=-1\]

Checks: \(5(3/5)^2+2(3/5)-3=0\) and \(5-2-3=0\).
