Topic 23: Adding and Subtracting Rational Expressions

Assigned to: TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic adds and subtracts rational expressions by factoring denominators, building the least common denominator, combining numerators, and simplifying.

Key definitions

Least common denominator (LCD): the least expression containing every denominator factor with sufficient multiplicity. Rational expression: a quotient of polynomials. Restriction: an input that makes an original denominator 0.

Common denominators and restrictions

Record restrictions from the original denominators. Factor the denominators to find an LCD, then multiply each numerator and denominator by any missing factors. Combine the numerators over the LCD. For subtraction, subtract the entire second numerator. Finally factor and simplify the result, keeping the original restrictions.

When the denominators already match, keep that denominator and combine the numerators directly.

Add, simplify, and state all restrictions.

\[\frac{2x+1}{(x-2)(x+3)}+\frac{x-7}{(x-2)(x+3)}\]

The common denominator gives the restrictions \(x\ne-3,2\). Add the numerators, then factor the result and cancel the common factor \(x-2\).

\[\frac{(2x+1)+(x-7)}{(x-2)(x+3)}=\frac{3x-6}{(x-2)(x+3)}=\frac{3(x-2)}{(x-2)(x+3)}=\frac{3}{x+3}\]

Both original restrictions still apply.

Subtract, simplify, and state all restrictions.

\[\frac{3x}{(x-2)(x+3)}-\frac{x-4}{(x-2)(x+3)}\]

The common denominator gives the restrictions \(x\ne-3,2\). Put the second numerator in parentheses and distribute the negative sign to both terms.

\[\frac{3x-(x-4)}{(x-2)(x+3)}=\frac{3x-x+4}{(x-2)(x+3)}=\frac{2(x+2)}{(x-2)(x+3)}\]

The numerator factor \(x+2\) looks like \(x-2\), but their constant terms have different signs. There is no common factor to cancel.

Add, simplify, and state all restrictions.

\[\frac{2}{3x}+\frac{5}{4x}\]

To add \(2/(3x)\) and \(5/(4x)\), the least common denominator is \(12x\).

\[\frac{2}{3x} + \frac{5}{4x} = \frac{8}{12x} + \frac{15}{12x} = \frac{23}{12x},\quad\quad x \neq 0.\]

Add, simplify, and state all restrictions: \(\frac7k+\frac5k\).

The common denominator is k, which must be nonzero. Add the numerators.

\[\frac7k+\frac5k=\frac{12}k,\qquad k\ne0\]

Subtract, simplify, and state all restrictions.

\[\frac{x}{x + 2} - \frac{14}{x^{2} - 4}.\]

Factor the difference of squares: \(x^{2} - 4 = (x - 2)(x + 2)\). The LCD is \((x - 2)(x + 2)\), and the original restrictions are \(x \neq - 2,2\).

\[\frac{x(x - 2)}{(x + 2)(x - 2)} - \frac{14}{(x - 2)(x + 2)} = \frac{x^{2} - 2x - 14}{(x - 2)(x + 2)}.\]

The numerator does not factor over the integers, so no final cancellation is possible.

Subtract, simplify, and state all restrictions: \(\frac3{x-1}-\frac{x+5}{x^2-1}\).

\[\frac{3}{x - 1} - \frac{x + 5}{x^{2} - 1} = \frac{3(x + 1) - (x + 5)}{(x - 1)(x + 1)} = \frac{2x - 2}{(x - 1)(x + 1)} = \frac{2}{x + 1},\]

with \(x \neq - 1,1\). The final cancellation is valid only after the numerator has been combined and factored.

Simplify and state all restrictions: \(\frac5{2a}+\frac3{6a}\).

The original denominators require \(a\ne0\). Use the factored LCD, then combine numerators and check for common factors.

\[\frac{15+3}{6a}=\frac3a\]

The final numerator and denominator have no further common factor.

Simplify and state all restrictions: \(\frac4{x-3}-\frac1{x+3}\).

The original denominators require \(x\ne-3,3\). Use the factored LCD, then combine numerators and check for common factors.

\[\frac{4(x+3)-(x-3)}{(x-3)(x+3)}=\frac{3x+15}{(x-3)(x+3)}\]

The final numerator and denominator have no further common factor.

Simplify and state all restrictions: \(\frac2{m+4}+\frac{m-2}{m^2-16}\).

The original denominators require \(m\ne-4,4\). Use the factored LCD, then combine numerators and check for common factors.

\[\frac{2(m-4)+(m-2)}{(m-4)(m+4)}=\frac{3m-10}{(m-4)(m+4)}\]

The final numerator and denominator have no further common factor.

More practice factoring denominators

Add, simplify, and state all restrictions.

\[\frac{1}{2x^2-6x}+\frac{1}{4x-12}\]

Factor out the GCF in each denominator.

\[2x^2-6x=2x(x-3),\qquad 4x-12=4(x-3)\]

The LCD is \(4x(x-3)\), and the original restrictions are \(x\ne0,3\). Multiply the first fraction by \(2/2\) and the second by \(x/x\).

\[\frac{2+x}{4x(x-3)}=\frac{x+2}{4x(x-3)}\]

There is no common factor to cancel.

Add, simplify, and state all restrictions.

\[\frac{1}{x^2+5x+6}+\frac{1}{x^2+7x+12}\]

Use the sum-product method to factor each trinomial.

\[x^2+5x+6=(x+2)(x+3),\qquad x^2+7x+12=(x+3)(x+4)\]

The LCD is \((x+2)(x+3)(x+4)\), and the original restrictions are \(x\ne-4,-3,-2\).

\[\frac{(x+4)+(x+2)}{(x+2)(x+3)(x+4)}=\frac{2(x+3)}{(x+2)(x+3)(x+4)}=\frac{2}{(x+2)(x+4)}\]

Cancel the common factor \(x+3\) and keep all three original restrictions.

Subtract, simplify, and state all restrictions.

\[\frac{2}{2x^2+5x+2}-\frac{x-1}{x^2-4}\]

Split the middle term of the first denominator as \(4x+x\) and factor by grouping. The second denominator is a difference of squares.

\[2x^2+5x+2=(2x+1)(x+2),\qquad x^2-4=(x-2)(x+2)\]

The LCD is \((2x+1)(x+2)(x-2)\), and the original restrictions are \(x\ne-2,-\frac12,2\).

\[\frac{2(x-2)-(x-1)(2x+1)}{(2x+1)(x+2)(x-2)}=\frac{-2x^2+3x-3}{(2x+1)(x+2)(x-2)}\]

The numerator is nonzero at each of the three excluded values, so it shares no factor with the denominator.

Add, simplify, and state all restrictions.

\[\frac{1}{x^2-6x+9}+\frac{2}{x^2-3x}\]

The first denominator is a perfect-square trinomial. Factor out \(x\) from the second.

\[x^2-6x+9=(x-3)^2,\qquad x^2-3x=x(x-3)\]

The LCD is \(x(x-3)^2\) because the first denominator needs two copies of \(x-3\). The original restrictions are \(x\ne0,3\).

\[\frac{x+2(x-3)}{x(x-3)^2}=\frac{3(x-2)}{x(x-3)^2}\]

There is no common factor to cancel.

Subtract, simplify, and state all restrictions.

\[\frac{2}{x^2-1}-\frac{6}{x^3+2x^2-x-2}\]

Group the four terms of the second denominator, then factor the difference of squares that appears in both denominators.

\[x^3+2x^2-x-2=x^2(x+2)-(x+2)=(x+2)(x^2-1)=(x+2)(x-1)(x+1)\]

The LCD is \((x+2)(x-1)(x+1)\), and the original restrictions are \(x\ne-2,-1,1\).

\[\frac{2(x+2)-6}{(x+2)(x-1)(x+1)}=\frac{2(x-1)}{(x+2)(x-1)(x+1)}=\frac{2}{(x+2)(x+1)}\]

Cancel the common factor \(x-1\) and keep all three original restrictions.
