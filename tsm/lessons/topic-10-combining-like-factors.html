TSM Lecture Notes

Topic 10: Combining Like Factors

Assigned to: TSM 098 and TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic simplifies products and quotients of powers by counting repeated factors and applying exponent rules.

Key definitions

Factor: a quantity being multiplied. Product rule: add exponents when multiplying powers with the same base. Quotient rule: subtract exponents when dividing powers with the same base. Power rule: multiply exponents when raising a power to another power. Negative exponent: indicates a reciprocal factor.

Multiply and simplify.

\[4^{5}\ \cdot \ 4^{6}\]

The exponent five means that the first power contains five factors of 4. The exponent six means that the second power contains six more factors of 4. Written as shortened factor strings, the two groups sit next to each other because the groups are being multiplied.

\[\overset{5}{\overbrace{4 \cdot 4 \cdot 4 \cdot 4 \cdot 4}} \cdot \overset{6}{\overbrace{4 \cdot 4 \cdot 4 \cdot 4 \cdot 4 \cdot 4}}\]

Putting the two strings together gives one product containing eleven factors of 4. Eleven repeated factors of 4 are written with an exponent of eleven.

\[4^{11}\]

This is the product rule for exponents: when powers with the same base are multiplied, add the exponents. The reason the exponents add is that the two strings of repeated factors have been joined into one longer string.

\[a^{m}\ \cdot \ a^{n}\ = \ a^{m + n}\]

In the previous topic, combining like terms meant counting copies that were being added. In this topic, the expressions are products. We will count copies that are being multiplied. Those repeated copies are factors, and exponent notation gives us a short way to record how many copies of the same factor appear.

Addition and multiplication do different things

The operation sign tells us what kind of counting to do. When like terms are added, the coefficients tell how many copies of the entire term we have. The exponent does not change.

\[5x^{9}\ + \ 3x^{9}\]

Five copies of x to the ninth plus three more copies make eight copies of x to the ninth.

\[8x^{9}\]

When powers are multiplied, the factors inside the powers join together. The exponents count those factors, so the exponents are added. Do not use the product rule across an addition sign.

Simplify.

\[z^{3}\ \cdot \ z^{7}\ \cdot \ z^{4}\]

The first power contributes three factors of z, the second contributes seven, and the third contributes four. Altogether there are fourteen factors of z.

\[3\ + \ 7\ + \ 4\ = \ 14\]

\[z^{14}\]

Use the product rule to simplify.

\[( - 2x^{2})( - 7x^{7})\]

There are two kinds of factors to handle. First multiply the numerical factors. A negative times a negative is positive, and two times seven is fourteen. Then join the two strings of x-factors: two copies of x and seven more copies make nine copies.

\[( - 2)( - 7)\ \cdot \ x^{2}\ \cdot \ x^{7}\]

\[14x^{9}\]

Use the product rule to simplify.

\[( - 9x^{3})( - 5x^{4})\]

The numerical factors give positive forty-five. The powers contribute three and four factors of x, for seven factors altogether.

\[45x^{7}\]

Use the product rule to simplify.

\[( - 3x^{8})(9x^{4})\]

A negative factor times a positive factor gives a negative result. Three times nine is twenty-seven, and eight x-factors together with four more give twelve x-factors.

\[- 27x^{12}\]

Use the product rule to simplify.

\[5^{5}\ \cdot \ 5^{7}\ \cdot \ 5^{6}\]

All three powers have the same base. The three strings contain five, seven, and six factors of 5. In all, there are eighteen factors of 5.

\[5^{18}\]

Simplify.

\[- 5b^{2}c( - 4cd)\]

The numerical factors are negative five and negative four, so their product is positive twenty. The b squared has no other b-factor to join with, so it remains b squared. The two visible c-factors are c to the first power and c to the first power, so together they make c squared. There is one d-factor.

\[( - 5)( - 4)\ \cdot \ b^{2}\ \cdot \ c\ \cdot \ c\ \cdot \ d\]

\[20b^{2}c^{2}d\]

Simplify.

\[(3xy^{2})(xyz)( - 2x^{2}y^{3})\]

Work with one kind of factor at a time. The numerical factors are 3, an implied 1, and -2, so the numerical result is -6. For x, the exponents are 1, 1, and 2, giving four x-factors. For y, the exponents are 2, 1, and 3, giving six y-factors. There is one z-factor.

\[3\ \cdot \ 1\ \cdot \ ( - 2)\]

\[x^{1 + 1 + 2}\ \cdot \ y^{2 + 1 + 3}\ \cdot \ z\]

\[- 6x^{4}y^{6}z\]

Simplify.

\[(3yz)( - 9x^{2}z)\]

The coefficients multiply to -27. There is one x-squared factor, one y-factor, and two z-factors. Because multiplication is commutative, the factors may be placed in the customary order: coefficient first, followed by variables in alphabetical order.

\[- 27yz^{2}x^{2}\]

\[- 27x^{2}yz^{2}\]

Combining factors in a longer product

Simplify.

\[- 8a^{3}c^{2}( - 2ab^{2})(abc)\]

The numerical factors -8, -2, and the implied 1 produce positive 16. Count each variable independently. The a-exponents are 3, 1, and 1, so there are five a-factors. The b-exponents are 0, 2, and 1, so there are three b-factors. The c-exponents are 2, 0, and 1, so there are three c-factors.

\[16a^{5}b^{3}c^{3}\]

Compare and contrast the following four expressions. Simplify each and explain why addition and multiplication use different rules.

  1. \(3x^4+3x^4\)
  2. \(3x^4\cdot3x^4\)
  3. \(3x^4+3x^5\)
  4. \(3x^4\cdot3x^5\)

These four expressions look similar, but the operation between the terms or factors changes what can be done.

1. The variable parts are identical and the terms are being added. Combine the numbers of copies and keep the variable part unchanged.

\[3x^{4}\ + \ 3x^{4}\]

\[6x^{4}\]

2. The two monomials are being multiplied. Multiply the coefficients, and then join the two groups of four x-factors.

\[3x^{4}\ \cdot \ 3x^{4}\]

\[9x^{8}\]

3. The terms are being added, but x to the fourth and x to the fifth are different quantities. They are unlike terms, so the expression cannot be combined.

\[3x^{4}\ + \ 3x^{5}\]

Can’t be simplified

4. The factors are being multiplied. Both powers have base x, so their factor strings can be joined even though the exponents are different. Multiply the coefficients and add the exponents.

\[3x^{4}\ \cdot \ 3x^{5}\]

\[9x^{9}\]

The central question is whether the expression is adding terms or multiplying factors. For addition, combine only terms with exactly the same variable part, and leave that variable part unchanged. For multiplication, multiply the numerical factors and combine powers that have the same base by adding their exponents. A variable with no written exponent has exponent one, and each different variable must be counted separately.

Simplify:

\[b^{3}\ \cdot \ b^{8}\ \cdot \ b^{7}\]

When powers with the same base are multiplied, their factors join into one longer string of repeated factors. Therefore we keep the base and add the exponents:

\[a^{m}\ \cdot \ a^{n}\ = \ a^{m + n}\]

This rule applies only when the bases are the same and the operation is multiplication. It does not say that we may combine unlike bases, and it does not apply across addition.

All three factors have base b, so add all three exponents:

\[b^{3}\ \cdot \ b^{8}\ \cdot \ b^{7}\ = \ b^{3 + 8 + 7}\ = \ b^{18}\]

Notice that b cubed, b to the eighth, and b to the seventh are like factors, not like terms. We are multiplying them, so the product rule applies.

Use the product rule to simplify:

\[\left( - 9x^{8} \right)\left( - 3x^{4} \right)\]

First multiply the numerical coefficients. A negative times a negative is positive. Then add the exponents on the common base x:

\[\left( - 9x^{8} \right)\left( - 3x^{4} \right)\ = \ 27x^{8 + 4}\ = \ 27x^{12}\]

Exponents are a compact way to describe repeated factors

An exponent tells us how many copies of a base are being multiplied. For example, b cubed means b multiplied by b multiplied by b. This repeated-factor meaning is the reason all of the exponent rules work.

\[b^{3}\ = \ b\ \cdot \ b\ \cdot \ b\]

The important question is not simply, "What exponents do I see?" The important question is, "What operation connects the expressions?" Multiplying powers with the same base leads to one rule. Raising a power to another power leads to a different rule. We should identify the structure before changing any exponents.

Simplify:

\[\left( 2^{3} \right)^{3}\]

A power raised to another power means that the inner power is being used repeatedly as a factor. That is why the exponents multiply:

\[\left( a^{m} \right)^{n}\ = \ a^{mn}\]

This is different from the product rule. With a product of two powers, we add exponents. With a power raised to a power, we multiply exponents.

The outside exponent 3 tells us to use three copies of 2 cubed. Altogether there are nine factors of 2:

\[\left( 2^{3} \right)^{3}\ = \ 2^{3 \cdot 3}\ = \ 2^{9}\]

When an exponent is outside parentheses containing a product, the power applies to every factor inside:

\[(ab)^{n}\ = \ a^{n}b^{n}\]

\[\left( \frac{a}{b} \right)^{n}\ = \ \frac{a^{n}}{b^{n}}\]

Simplify:

\[(2y)^{3}\]

The exponent 3 applies to both 2 and y. Cube the coefficient and cube the variable:

\[(2y)^{3}\ = \ 2^{3}y^{3}\ = \ 8y^{3}\]

Simplify. Assume the denominator is nonzero.

\[\left(\frac{a}{b}\right)^5\]

The same idea applies to a fraction. The outside exponent applies to the numerator and the denominator:

\[\left( \frac{a}{b} \right)^{5}\ = \ \frac{a^{5}}{b^{5}}\]

Simplify the following expression.

\[\left( 4a^{3}b^{8} \right)^{2}\]

Simplify, using positive exponents:

\[\left( 4a^{3}b^{8} \right)^{2}\]

Square the coefficient, and multiply each inner exponent by 2:

\[\left( 4a^{3}b^{8} \right)^{2}\ = \ 4^{2}a^{3 \cdot 2}b^{8 \cdot 2}\]

\[= \ 16a^{6}b^{16}\]

Simplify:

\[\left( x^{5} \right)^{5}\left( x^{3} \right)^{3}\]

Each parenthesized power is a power raised to a power, so multiply those exponents first. The two resulting powers then have the same base and are multiplied, so add their exponents:

\[\left( x^{5} \right)^{5}\left( x^{3} \right)^{3}\ = \ x^{25}x^{9}\]

\[= \ x^{25 + 9}\ = \ x^{34}\]

Simplify using positive exponents.

\[\frac{11^{- 12}}{11^{2}}\]

When powers with the same base are divided, matching factors cancel. We keep the base and subtract the denominator's exponent from the numerator's exponent:

\[\frac{a^{m}}{a^{n}}\ = \ a^{m - n}\]

The order matters: numerator exponent minus denominator exponent. If the result is negative, use the negative-exponent rule to write the final answer with positive exponents:

\[a^{- n}\ = \ \frac{1}{a^{n}}\ \ \ \ \ \ ;\ \ \ \ \ \ \frac{1}{a^{- n}}\ = \ a^{n}\]

Subtract the denominator exponent from the numerator exponent. Then rewrite the negative exponent as a reciprocal:

\[\frac{11^{- 12}}{11^{2}}\ = \ 11^{- 12 - 2}\ = \ 11^{- 14}\]

\[= \ \frac{1}{11^{14}}\]

Simplify using positive exponents.

\[\frac{3^{4}x^{9}}{3^{5}x^{4}}\]

Simplify, using positive exponents:

\[\frac{3^{4}x^{9}}{3^{5}x^{4}}\]

For the base 3, the exponent difference is 4 - 5 = -1, so one factor of 3 remains in the denominator. For x, the exponent difference is 9 - 4 = 5:

\[\frac{3^{4}x^{9}}{3^{5}x^{4}}\ = \ 3^{- 1}x^{5}\ = \ \frac{x^{5}}{3}\]

Simplify using positive exponents.

\[\frac{z^{4}}{z^{- 1}}\]

The quotient rule still says numerator exponent minus denominator exponent. Here the denominator exponent is -1. Subtracting a negative becomes addition:

\[\frac{z^{4}}{z^{- 1}}\ = \ z^{4 - ( - 1)}\ = \ z^{4 + 1}\ = \ z^{5}\]

Simplify using positive exponents. Evaluate numerical powers.

\[\frac{(2x)^{- 2}}{(5x)^{- 3}}\]

A negative exponent does not make a number negative. It tells us that the factor belongs on the opposite side of the fraction bar. Moving the factor changes the exponent from negative to positive. This is often the cleanest way to simplify an expression containing several negative exponents.

The negative exponent in the numerator moves 2x to the denominator. The negative exponent in the denominator moves 5x to the numerator:

\[\frac{(2x)^{- 2}}{(5x)^{- 3}}\ = \ \frac{(5x)^{3}}{(2x)^{2}}\]

\[= \ \frac{125x^{3}}{4x^{2}}\ = \ \frac{125x}{4}\]

Simplify using positive exponents.

\[\left( \frac{x^{- 3}y^{- 4}}{z^{2}} \right)^{- 4}\]

Because the outside exponent is negative, first take the reciprocal of the entire expression and make the outside exponent positive:

\[\left( \frac{x^{- 3}y^{- 4}}{z^{2}} \right)^{- 4}\ = \ \left( \frac{z^{2}}{x^{- 3}y^{- 4}} \right)^{4}\]

The negative powers of x and y are in the denominator, so they move to the numerator. Then distribute the outside power of 4:

\[\left( x^{3}y^{4}z^{2} \right)^{4}\ = \ x^{12}y^{16}z^{8}\]

Simplify using positive exponents.

\[\frac{\left( m^{6}n \right)^{- 4}}{m^{- 12}n^{7}}\]

Simplify, using only positive exponents:

\[\frac{\left( m^{6}n \right)^{- 4}}{m^{- 12}n^{7}}\]

Apply the outside exponent to both factors in the numerator. Then subtract exponents base by base:

\[\frac{m^{- 24}n^{- 4}}{m^{- 12}n^{7}}\ = \ m^{- 24 - ( - 12)}n^{- 4 - 7}\]

\[= \ m^{- 12}n^{- 11}\ = \ \frac{1}{m^{12}n^{11}}\]

  1. Identify the operation connecting the powers: multiplication, division, or an outside exponent.

  2. For multiplication with like bases, add exponents.

  3. For division with like bases, subtract the denominator exponent from the numerator exponent.

  4. For a power raised to a power, multiply exponents. Apply an outside power to every factor inside parentheses.

  5. Simplify numerical coefficients separately from variable factors.

  6. Rewrite every negative exponent by moving its factor across the fraction bar. Unless directions say otherwise, leave the final answer with positive exponents.

  • Do not add exponents when terms are being added. The product rule applies to multiplication, not addition.

  • Do not distribute an exponent across a sum. In general, the square of x + y is not x squared + y squared.

  • Do not interpret a negative exponent as a negative coefficient. A negative exponent indicates a reciprocal.

  • In a quotient, subtract in the correct order: numerator exponent minus denominator exponent.
