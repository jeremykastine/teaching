Topic 32: Graphing in Slope-Intercept Form

Assigned to: TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic graphs a line directly from its slope and y-intercept and rewrites equations in slope-intercept form when necessary.

Key definitions

Slope-intercept form: y = mx + b. Slope m: vertical change divided by horizontal change. y-intercept b: the y-coordinate where the line crosses the y-axis.

Reading slope-intercept form

Slope-intercept form is

\[y = mx + b,\]

where \(m\) is the slope and \(b\) is the \(y\)-intercept. The intercept is the point \((0,b)\). The slope \(m\) is the ratio

\[m = \frac{\text{vertical change}}{\text{horizontal change}} = \frac{\text{rise}}{\text{run}}.\]

Graph \(y=\frac{7}{4}x+2\) using its slope and y-intercept.

Start at \((0,2)\). A slope of \(7/4\) means move right \(4\) and up \(7\), giving the point \((4,9)\). Moving left \(4\) and down \(7\) gives another point \(( - 4, - 5)\).

Plot these points and draw the line through them, extending it in both directions.

Using slope

Keep vertical change in the numerator and horizontal change in the denominator. A negative slope puts a negative sign in one change, not both. The intercept is a point on the vertical axis. If needed, isolate y before reading the slope and intercept.

Graph \(y=-\frac{1}{2}x+4\) using its slope and y-intercept.

Start at \((0,4)\). Interpret \(- 1/2\) as moving right \(2\) and down \(1\), producing \((2,3)\), \((4,2)\), and \((6,1)\). A line with negative slope falls from left to right.

Plot these points and draw the line through them, extending it in both directions.

Graph \(3x+2y=6\) using slope-intercept form.

To graph \(3x + 2y = 6\) by slope and intercept, isolate \(y\).

\[2y = - 3x + 6\]

\[y = - \frac{3}{2}x + 3.\]

The \(y\)-intercept is \((0,3)\), and the slope is \(- 3/2\).

Plot \((0,3)\) and \((2,0)\), then draw the line through them.

Graph \(y=2x-3\) in a -10 to 10 window on both axes. Check a point.

Use slope-intercept form:

\[y=2x-3\]

Plot \((0,-3), (1,-1)\) and draw the line through them. Check the second point:

\[-1=2(1)-3\]

Graph \(y=-\frac34x+5\) in a -10 to 10 window on both axes. Check a point.

Use slope-intercept form:

\[y=-\frac34x+5\]

Plot \((0,5), (4,2)\) and draw the line through them. Check the second point:

\[2=-\frac34(4)+5\]

Graph \(x-8y=-8\) in a -10 to 10 window on both axes. Check a point.

Use slope-intercept form:

\[y=\frac18x+1\]

Plot \((0,1), (8,2)\) and draw the line through them. Check the second point:

\[8-8(2)=-8\]
