Topic 7: Order of Operations

Assigned to: TSM 098 and TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic applies the powers learned in Topic 5 to numerical expressions and substitution. Both classes study the shared section. TSM 099 students then continue to polynomials, variable denominators, and the quadratic formula.

Every division in these worked examples has an integer result. Treat each fraction bar as grouped division; fraction arithmetic comes later. Here an integer result may be positive, zero, or negative.

TSM 098 and TSM 099TSM 099 only

Key definitions

Order of operations: evaluate grouped expressions, powers, multiplication and division, then addition and subtraction. Substitution: replace a variable with its given value. Linear combination: a sum or difference of expressions multiplied by constants. Factored polynomial: a polynomial written as a product. Rational expression: a quotient of polynomials; inputs making its denominator zero are excluded.

TSM 098 and TSM 099: shared evaluation skills

Both classes should be able to evaluate numerical expressions, linear expressions with parentheses, expressions with fixed numerical denominators, linear combinations, and factored polynomials.

Grouping symbols, including fraction bars, identify expressions to evaluate as a unit. Within a group, powers precede multiplication and division, which precede addition and subtraction. Multiplication and division have equal precedence and are read left to right; addition and subtraction have equal precedence and are read left to right. Independent operations may be completed together when the rewrites preserve the value of the whole expression.

Evaluate. Explain which operation must be performed first.

\[2+3\cdot4\]

The 3 is a factor in a product, so it cannot yet be added to 2. Multiplication takes precedence over addition.

\[2+3\cdot4=2+12=14\]

Adding 2 and 3 first would change the grouping and produce a different expression.

Evaluate. Identify independent operations that may be completed in the same step.

\[2+3\cdot4+5\cdot2^2+6+7-18\div3\cdot2\]

Evaluate the power. The product \(3\cdot4\) is independent of it. In the final term, division and multiplication have equal precedence: read \(18\div3\cdot2\) from left to right, so divide 18 by 3 before multiplying by 2. These changes may be made together.

\[2+12+5\cdot4+6+7-6\cdot2\]

Finish the remaining products. The neighboring additions do not take precedence over them.

\[2+12+20+6+7-12\]

Only addition and subtraction remain. Read them from left to right. Adding 6 and 7 earlier would also have been valid because neither number belongs to a higher-precedence operation.

\[14+20+6+7-12\]

\[34+6+7-12\]

\[40+7-12\]

\[47-12=35\]

Evaluate. Explain how the parentheses and fraction bar group the operations.

\[\frac{5(3+6)+3(4-3)}{3(9-2)-3^2}\]

The fraction bar groups the whole numerator and the whole denominator. Treat it as division after evaluating those groups. Complete the parentheses and the independent power first.

\[\frac{5(9)+3(1)}{3(7)-9}\]

A number next to parentheses means multiplication. Evaluate those products before adding or subtracting.

\[\frac{45+3}{21-9}\]

\[\frac{48}{12}=4\]

The final division is exact; no fraction-operation rules are needed.

Substitution into course expressions

Replace every occurrence of a variable with the entire input, using parentheses to protect signs. A number beside a variable or parentheses means multiplication. For a factored polynomial, evaluate the factors directly; expanding is unnecessary.

Evaluate at \(x=-3\) and \(y=2\).

\[4x-3y+5\]

Replace each variable with its entire value, using parentheses for negative inputs. Juxtaposition means multiplication.

\[4(-3)-3(2)+5\]

\[-12-6+5\]

\[-18+5=-13\]

Subtracting 6 moves the negative total farther from zero; adding 5 then reduces its magnitude. This is the type of linear expression used in equations and systems.

Evaluate at \(x=-2\).

\[2(3x-4)-5(x+2)\]

Substitute first. Evaluate each group before multiplying by its outside coefficient; expansion is unnecessary.

\[2(3(-2)-4)-5((-2)+2)\]

\[2(-6-4)-5(0)\]

\[2(-10)-0=-20\]

The minus sign before the second product stays outside its parentheses.

Evaluate at \(x=4\) and \(y=-2\).

\[\frac{3x-2y+8}{4}\]

The denominator is a fixed number. Evaluate the entire numerator before dividing by 4. Subtracting \(2(-2)\) subtracts a negative product.

\[\frac{3(4)-2(-2)+8}{4}\]

\[\frac{12-(-4)+8}{4}\]

\[\frac{12+4+8}{4}\]

\[\frac{24}{4}=6\]

Evaluate at \(x=-2\) and \(y=1\).

\[2(3x-2y)-3(x+4y)\]

This is a linear combination: two linear expressions are multiplied by constants and then combined. Keep each group intact while substituting.

\[2(3(-2)-2(1))-3((-2)+4(1))\]

\[2(-6-2)-3(-2+4)\]

\[2(-8)-3(2)\]

\[-16-6=-22\]

This structure prepares for combinations of equations in a linear system. Here the task is only to evaluate the given expression, not to solve a system.

Evaluate at \(x=-2\), \(x=0\), and \(x=3\). Compare how the signs and a zero factor affect the results.

\[2(x-3)(x+1)\]

The polynomial is already factored. Substitute into each factor and multiply; do not expand it first.

For \(x=-2\):

\[2((-2)-3)((-2)+1)\]

\[2(-5)(-1)=10\]

For \(x=0\):

\[2((0)-3)((0)+1)\]

\[2(-3)(1)=-6\]

For \(x=3\):

\[2((3)-3)((3)+1)\]

\[2(0)(4)=0\]

At \(x=-2\), both variable factors are negative, so their product is positive. At \(x=0\), the factors have opposite signs. At \(x=3\), one factor is zero, which makes the entire product zero.

Expanded polynomials

When substituting a negative input into a power, the entire negative number is the base. An outside coefficient is a separate factor. The following polynomials have coefficients from −5 through 5 and use inputs −10, 0, and 10 to focus on signs and precedence with straightforward arithmetic.

Evaluate at \(x=-10\), \(x=0\), and \(x=10\). Compare the effect of the input sign on even and odd powers.

\[-3x^2+2x-4\]

Substitute the entire input into each power. Evaluate powers first, then multiply by coefficients, then combine the terms. The coefficients are not part of the bases being raised to powers.

For \(x=-10\):

\[-3(-10)^2+2(-10)-4\]

\[-3(100)+2(-10)-4\]

\[-300-20-4=-324\]

For \(x=0\):

\[-3(0)^2+2(0)-4\]

\[-3(0)+2(0)-4\]

\[0+0-4=-4\]

For \(x=10\):

\[-3(10)^2+2(10)-4\]

\[-3(100)+2(10)-4\]

\[-300+20-4=-284\]

Compare the inputs: even powers of \(-10\) and \(10\) agree, while odd powers have opposite signs. At zero, every variable term vanishes and only the constant remains.

Evaluate at \(x=-10\), \(x=0\), and \(x=10\). Compare the effect of the input sign on even and odd powers.

\[2x^3-4x^2-3x+5\]

Substitute the entire input into each power. Evaluate powers first, then multiply by coefficients, then combine the terms. The coefficients are not part of the bases being raised to powers.

For \(x=-10\):

\[2(-10)^3-4(-10)^2-3(-10)+5\]

\[2(-1000)-4(100)-3(-10)+5\]

\[-2000-400+30+5=-2365\]

For \(x=0\):

\[2(0)^3-4(0)^2-3(0)+5\]

\[2(0)-4(0)-3(0)+5\]

\[0+0+0+5=5\]

For \(x=10\):

\[2(10)^3-4(10)^2-3(10)+5\]

\[2(1000)-4(100)-3(10)+5\]

\[2000-400-30+5=1575\]

Compare the inputs: even powers of \(-10\) and \(10\) agree, while odd powers have opposite signs. At zero, every variable term vanishes and only the constant remains.

Rational expressions with variable denominators

Check the original denominator before evaluating. Evaluate the whole numerator and denominator separately, then divide. These inputs produce integer quotients, so symbolic fraction operations and cancellation are unnecessary.

Evaluate at \(x=-2\), \(x=0\), and \(x=5\). State the input excluded by the original denominator and compare the signs of the three values.

\[\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}\]

The original denominator must be nonzero, so \(x\ne2\). All three supplied inputs are allowed. Substitute into the numerator and denominator separately; there is no need to factor or cancel.

For \(x=-2\):

\[\frac{(-2)^2-4}{(-2)-2}\]

\[\frac{4-4}{-4}\]

\[\frac{0}{-4}=0\]

For \(x=0\):

\[\frac{(0)^2-4}{(0)-2}\]

\[\frac{0-4}{-2}\]

\[\frac{-4}{-2}=2\]

For \(x=5\):

\[\frac{(5)^2-4}{(5)-2}\]

\[\frac{25-4}{3}\]

\[\frac{21}{3}=7\]

A zero numerator gives zero when the denominator is nonzero. A zero denominator would make an expression undefined; it would never justify dividing.

Evaluate at \(x=-2\), \(x=0\), and \(x=2\). State the inputs excluded by the original denominator and compare the results at opposite inputs.

\[\frac{2x^2+10}{x^2-1}\]

The denominator is zero at \(x=-1\) and \(x=1\), so exclude those inputs. All three supplied inputs are allowed. Powers occur in both the numerator and denominator; evaluate them before the other operations.

For \(x=-2\):

\[\frac{2(-2)^2+10}{(-2)^2-1}\]

\[\frac{2(4)+10}{4-1}\]

\[\frac{18}{3}=6\]

For \(x=0\):

\[\frac{2(0)^2+10}{(0)^2-1}\]

\[\frac{2(0)+10}{0-1}\]

\[\frac{10}{-1}=-10\]

For \(x=2\):

\[\frac{2(2)^2+10}{(2)^2-1}\]

\[\frac{2(4)+10}{4-1}\]

\[\frac{18}{3}=6\]

The values at opposite inputs agree because the variable appears only squared. At zero, the denominator is negative; the resulting quotient is negative.

Evaluating the quadratic formula

The quadratic formula gives values from three coefficients, with \(a\ne0\). At this stage, practice evaluating the supplied formula rather than deriving it or learning an equation-solving method. The \(\pm\) symbol means to calculate once with addition and once with subtraction. The examples include positive and negative values of \(b\), perfect-square radicands, and integer results in both branches.

Evaluate both branches of the formula for \(a=1\), \(b=3\), and \(c=2\). Compare the two results.

\[\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\]

Replace each coefficient with its entire signed value. The leading negative sign means the opposite of \(b\); it does not change the sign inside the square \(b^2\).

\[\frac{-(3)\pm\sqrt{(3)^2-4(1)(2)}}{2(1)}\]

Evaluate powers and products: \((3)^2=9\), \(4(1)(2)=8\), and \(2(1)=2\). Keep the subtraction of the product, including its sign.

\[\frac{-3\pm\sqrt{9-(8)}}{2}\]

\[\frac{-3\pm\sqrt{1}}{2}\]

The radicand is a perfect square, so \(\sqrt{1}=1\). The \(\pm\) symbol asks for two separate calculations. Complete each numerator before dividing by the whole denominator.

\[\frac{-3\pm1}{2}\]

With the plus sign:

\[\frac{-3+1}{2}=\frac{-2}{2}=-1\]

With the minus sign:

\[\frac{-3-1}{2}=\frac{-4}{2}=-2\]

The two values are \(-1\) and \(-2\). Both are integers, so both are rational. The paired branches share the same denominator; only the choice of addition or subtraction changes.

Evaluate both branches of the formula for \(a=1\), \(b=-5\), and \(c=6\). Compare the two results.

\[\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\]

Replace each coefficient with its entire signed value. The leading negative sign means the opposite of \(b\); it does not change the sign inside the square \(b^2\).

\[\frac{-(-5)\pm\sqrt{(-5)^2-4(1)(6)}}{2(1)}\]

Evaluate powers and products: \((-5)^2=25\), \(4(1)(6)=24\), and \(2(1)=2\). Keep the subtraction of the product, including its sign.

\[\frac{5\pm\sqrt{25-(24)}}{2}\]

\[\frac{5\pm\sqrt{1}}{2}\]

The radicand is a perfect square, so \(\sqrt{1}=1\). The \(\pm\) symbol asks for two separate calculations. Complete each numerator before dividing by the whole denominator.

\[\frac{5\pm1}{2}\]

With the plus sign:

\[\frac{5+1}{2}=\frac{6}{2}=3\]

With the minus sign:

\[\frac{5-1}{2}=\frac{4}{2}=2\]

The two values are \(3\) and \(2\). Both are integers, so both are rational. The paired branches share the same denominator; only the choice of addition or subtraction changes.

Evaluate both branches of the formula for \(a=2\), \(b=2\), and \(c=-12\). Compare the two results.

\[\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\]

Replace each coefficient with its entire signed value. The leading negative sign means the opposite of \(b\); it does not change the sign inside the square \(b^2\).

\[\frac{-(2)\pm\sqrt{(2)^2-4(2)(-12)}}{2(2)}\]

Evaluate powers and products: \((2)^2=4\), \(4(2)(-12)=-96\), and \(2(2)=4\). Keep the subtraction of the product, including its sign.

\[\frac{-2\pm\sqrt{4-(-96)}}{4}\]

\[\frac{-2\pm\sqrt{100}}{4}\]

The radicand is a perfect square, so \(\sqrt{100}=10\). The \(\pm\) symbol asks for two separate calculations. Complete each numerator before dividing by the whole denominator.

\[\frac{-2\pm10}{4}\]

With the plus sign:

\[\frac{-2+10}{4}=\frac{8}{4}=2\]

With the minus sign:

\[\frac{-2-10}{4}=\frac{-12}{4}=-3\]

The two values are \(2\) and \(-3\). Both are integers, so both are rational. The paired branches share the same denominator; only the choice of addition or subtraction changes.

Evaluate both branches of the formula for \(a=-1\), \(b=-3\), and \(c=4\). Compare the two results.

\[\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\]

Replace each coefficient with its entire signed value. The leading negative sign means the opposite of \(b\); it does not change the sign inside the square \(b^2\).

\[\frac{-(-3)\pm\sqrt{(-3)^2-4(-1)(4)}}{2(-1)}\]

Evaluate powers and products: \((-3)^2=9\), \(4(-1)(4)=-16\), and \(2(-1)=-2\). Keep the subtraction of the product, including its sign.

\[\frac{3\pm\sqrt{9-(-16)}}{-2}\]

\[\frac{3\pm\sqrt{25}}{-2}\]

The radicand is a perfect square, so \(\sqrt{25}=5\). The \(\pm\) symbol asks for two separate calculations. Complete each numerator before dividing by the whole denominator.

\[\frac{3\pm5}{-2}\]

With the plus sign:

\[\frac{3+5}{-2}=\frac{8}{-2}=-4\]

With the minus sign:

\[\frac{3-5}{-2}=\frac{-2}{-2}=1\]

The two values are \(-4\) and \(1\). Both are integers, so both are rational. The paired branches share the same denominator; only the choice of addition or subtraction changes.
