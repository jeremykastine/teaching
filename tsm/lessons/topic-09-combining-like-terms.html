Topic 9: Combining Like Terms

Assigned to: TSM 098 and TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic simplifies sums and differences by combining terms that represent the same kind of quantity.

Key definitions

Term: a number, variable, or product separated by addition or subtraction. Coefficient: the numerical factor of a variable term. Like terms: terms with exactly the same variable factors and exponents.

Simplify by combining like terms.

\[13w\ + \ 17w\]

A number beside a variable tells how many copies of that variable are being added. For example, three times four means four copies of three.

\[3\ \times \ 4\ = \ 3\ + \ 3\ + \ 3\ + \ 3\]

In the same way, the expression can be shown as two adjacent strings of w-copies: one string with thirteen copies and one string with seventeen copies.

\[13w\ + \ 17w\ = \overset{13\ copies}{\overbrace{w + w + ... + w} +}\overset{17\ copies}{\overbrace{w + w + ... + w}}\ \]

Putting the two strings together gives one string containing thirty copies of w. Thirty copies of w are written as 30w.

\[30w\]

Combining like terms is a way of counting how many copies of the same quantity we have. The first examples make that meaning explicit by writing out the repeated copies. Later, once the idea is clear, we will count the copies without writing every one of them.

Simplify by combining like terms.

\[- 2m^{2}\ + \ 4m^{2}\]

The term -2m² means two negative copies of m². The term +4m² means four positive copies of m². Writing those copies out shows what is happening.

\[- m^{2}\ - \ m^{2}\ + \ m^{2}\ + \ m^{2}\ + \ m^{2}\ + \ m^{2}\]

Pair a negative m² with a positive m². Each such pair cancels to zero. Two pairs cancel, leaving two positive copies of m².

\[m^{2}\ + \ m^{2}\]

Two positive copies of m² are written as 2m².

\[2m^{2}\]

What makes terms like

Terms are like when they name exactly the same kind of quantity. Their variable letters must match, and the exponents on those variables must also match. The number of copies and the sign may be different. Those differences do not stop the terms from being like.

The terms 5r and -13r² are unlike. One term contains r, while the other contains r². The terms 9r and -19r are like because both are copies of r. The terms 3m, 6m, and -9m are all like because all three are copies of m. The terms 10a² and 10a⁷ are unlike because a² and a⁷ are different quantities.

The same test applies to the additional practice groups. In 4, 2x, and -21, the constants 4 and -21 are like, but 2x is a different kind of term. The terms 11z and -15z are like. The terms 12v⁸ and -8v⁸ are like. The terms z and y are unlike, and m and n are unlike, because the variable letters differ. The numbers 8, 11, and -5 are all constants, so they are all like terms.

Simplify the expression.

\[2t\ + \ 8\ - \ 5\]

The 8 and -5 are like terms because both are constants. Think of them as eight positive ones and five negative ones. Five positive-negative pairs cancel, leaving three positive ones. The term 2t is not a constant, so it remains separate.

\[2t\ + \ 3\]

Collect like terms.

\[2z\ - \ 16z\ - \ 6z\]

There are two positive copies of z. There are sixteen negative copies and six more negative copies, for twenty-two negative copies altogether. The two positive copies cancel with two of the negative copies. Twenty negative copies remain.

\[- 20z\]

Simplify by combining like terms.

\[- 9x^{7}\ + \ 4x^{7}\ - \ 2x^{7}\]

There are nine negative copies of x⁷ and two more negative copies, for eleven negative copies altogether. The four positive copies cancel with four of those negative copies. Seven negative copies remain.

\[- 7x^{7}\]

Simplify the expression.

\[9a\ - \ 5a\ + \ 4\]

The nine positive copies of a and five negative copies of a are like. Five pairs cancel, leaving four positive copies of a. The constant 4 is a different kind of term, so it remains in the expression.

\[4a\ + \ 4\]

Simplify the expression by combining like terms.

\[9p^{2}\ + \ 5p^{3}\ - \ 7p^{2}\ - \ 2p^{3}\]

This expression contains two kinds of terms: copies of p² and copies of p³. The p² terms may be combined with one another, and the p³ terms may be combined with one another, but a copy of p² cannot cancel or combine with a copy of p³.

\[(9p^{2}\ - \ 7p^{2})\ + \ (5p^{3}\ - \ 2p^{3})\]

Among the p² terms, seven positive-negative pairs cancel, leaving two positive copies of p². Among the p³ terms, two pairs cancel, leaving three positive copies of p³.

\[2p^{2}\ + \ 3p^{3}\]

Parentheses can change the grouping without changing an addition

Before adding polynomial groups, consider an ordinary sum. The same five numbers may be grouped in different ways. The parentheses tell us which addition to perform first, but they do not change the total.

\[2\ + \ 3\ + \ 4\ + \ 5\ + \ 6\]

\[(2\ + \ 3)\ + \ (4\ + \ 5\ + \ 6)\]

\[5\ + \ 15\]

\[20\]

A different grouping gives the same total.

\[2\ + \ (3\ + \ 4)\ + \ (5\ + \ 6)\]

\[2\ + \ 7\ + \ 11\]

\[20\]

The same idea applies when two polynomial expressions are joined by a plus sign. Because the groups are being added, the parentheses may be removed without changing any term or changing any sign. Once the parentheses are gone, terms that name the same kind of quantity can be placed together and counted.

Add the polynomials and simplify.

\[(4x^{2}\ - \ 5x\ - \ 22)\ + \ (2x^{2}\ + \ 7x)\]

The plus sign between the groups means that every term in the second group keeps its sign when the parentheses are removed.

\[4x^{2}\ - \ 5x\ - \ 22\ + \ 2x^{2}\ + \ 7x\]

Now place together the terms that name the same kind of quantity. The x² terms belong together, the x terms belong together, and the constant -22 has no other constant with which to combine.

\[(4x^{2}\ + \ 2x^{2})\ + \ ( - 5x\ + \ 7x)\ - \ 22\]

Four copies of x² and two more copies make six copies of x². Five negative copies of x cancel with five of the seven positive copies, leaving two positive copies of x. The -22 remains.

\[6x^{2}\ + \ 2x\ - \ 22\]

Collect like terms.

\[6x^{3}\ + \ 5x^{3}\ + \ 4\]

Six copies of x³ and five more copies make eleven copies of x³. The constant 4 is a different kind of term and remains separate.

\[11x^{3}\ + \ 4\]

Add.

\[(5x^{3}\ - \ 2x^{2}\ + \ 4x)\ + \ (2x^{3}\ + \ 5x^{2}\ - \ 11x)\]

Because the groups are being added, remove the parentheses without changing any signs.

\[5x^{3}\ - \ 2x^{2}\ + \ 4x\ + \ 2x^{3}\ + \ 5x^{2}\ - \ 11x\]

Place together the x³ terms, the x² terms, and the x terms.

\[(5x^{3}\ + \ 2x^{3})\ + \ ( - 2x^{2}\ + \ 5x^{2})\ + \ (4x\ - \ 11x)\]

The x³ terms make seven positive copies. Two negative x² copies cancel with two of the five positive copies, leaving three positive x² copies. Four positive x copies cancel with four of the eleven negative copies, leaving seven negative x copies.

\[7x^{3}\ + \ 3x^{2}\ - \ 7x\]

The central question in every problem is: what kind of quantity does each term represent, and how many positive or negative copies of that quantity are present? Only terms representing the same quantity can be combined. Positive and negative copies cancel in pairs, and any copies left over determine the simplified term.
