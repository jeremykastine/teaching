Topic 3: Signed Numbers, Including Dividing by Zero

Assigned to: TSM 098 and TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic interprets and performs addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division with positive numbers, negative numbers, and zero.

Key definitions

Signed number: a positive number, negative number, or zero. Opposites: numbers the same distance from 0 with different signs. Undefined: an expression that has no numerical value; division by 0 is undefined.

Addition and subtraction: supports or opposes?

First ask: What is the sign of the first term? A number with no sign written before it is positive; a number with a negative sign is negative. Zero is neither positive nor negative.

Next ask: Does the second term support or oppose the first? Read the second number together with the operation and any negative sign attached to it. Adding a positive number has a positive effect; adding a negative number has a negative effect. Subtracting a positive number has a negative effect; subtracting a negative number has a positive effect.

If the second term supports the first, combine their sizes and keep their common sign. If it opposes the first, ask: Which one wins, and by how much? Subtract the smaller size from the larger size and keep the sign of the winner. Equal opposing sizes cancel to zero.

Read each expression as written; you do not need to rewrite addition as subtraction or subtraction as addition. A zero contribution changes nothing. If the first term is zero, the result is the second term's effect.

Multiplication and division

Multiplication may be written with a multiplication sign, a centered dot, or adjacent factors. Parentheses can make a whole expression one factor. Division may be written with a division symbol or a fraction bar.

For multiplication and division of nonzero numbers, decide the sign separately from the arithmetic. Pair the negative signs: each pair has a positive effect. If one negative sign remains unpaired, the answer is negative; if none remain, it is positive. This also works with more than two factors.

This pairing rule applies to multiplication and division. For addition and subtraction, decide whether the second term supports or opposes the first; two separate negative terms support each other and keep their negative sign.

Evaluate the expression.

\[10\ \cdot \ 8\]

Both factors are positive, so the product is positive. Multiply their sizes.

\[10 \cdot 8 = 80\]

Evaluate the expression.

\[6\ \cdot \ ( - 9)\]

There is one negative sign, and it has no partner. That single negative carries forward into the answer. The product has a size of fifty-four.

\[- 54\]

This can also be understood as six copies of negative nine. Repeating a negative amount six times produces a negative total.

Evaluate the expression.

\[( - 6)\ \cdot \ ( - 2)\]

The two negative signs form a pair, so together they have a positive effect. After dealing with that pair, multiply the sizes of the factors.

\[6\ \cdot \ 2\]

The product is positive twelve.

\[12\]

Evaluate the expression.

\[( - 2)\ \cdot \ ( - 3)\ \cdot \ ( - 4)\]

The same rule continues when there are three negative factors.

\[( - 2)\ \cdot \ ( - 3)\ \cdot \ ( - 4)\]

Pair two of the negative signs. That pair produces a positive factor, but one negative sign remains without a partner. The unpaired negative carries forward.

\[6\ \cdot \ ( - 4)\]

The product therefore remains negative.

\[- 24\]

Evaluate the expression.

\[( - 2)\ \cdot \ ( - 3)\ \cdot \ ( - 4)\ \cdot \ ( - 5)\]

The four negative signs can be arranged into two pairs. Each pair has a positive effect, so no negative sign is left to carry forward.

\[6\ \cdot \ 20\]

The product is positive one hundred twenty.

\[120\]

Evaluate the expression.

\[18\ \div \ 2\]

There are no negative signs to carry. Eighteen divided by two is nine.

\[9\]

Division can be checked with multiplication. In this case, \(9\ \cdot \ 2\ = \ 18\), so the quotient returns the original dividend.

Evaluate the expression.

\[15\ \div \ ( - 5)\]

There is one negative sign, and it has no partner. It carries into the answer. The quotient has a size of three, so the result is negative three.

\[- 3\]

The multiplication check is \(( - 3)\ \cdot \ ( - 5)\ = \ 15\). The two negatives in the check form a pair, producing the positive dividend.

Evaluate the expression.

\[( - 7)\ \div \ ( - 1)\]

The two negative signs form a pair, so they have a positive effect. Seven divided by one is seven.

\[7\]

The check \(7\ \cdot \ ( - 1)\ = \ - 7\) returns the original dividend.

Evaluate the expression.

\[0\ \div \ ( - 53)\]

Zero may be divided by any nonzero number.

\[0\ \div \ ( - 53)\]

The answer is zero. The single negative does not turn zero into a negative number; zero has no positive or negative size to carry. The quotient is still zero.

\[0\]

The multiplication check is \(0\ \cdot \ ( - 53)\ = \ 0\).

Determine whether the expression is defined. If it is, evaluate it.

\[( - 7)\ \div \ 0\]

Division by zero is a different situation.

\[( - 7)\ \div \ 0\]

A quotient would have to be a number that becomes negative seven when multiplied by zero. But every expression of the form \(?\ \cdot \ 0\) has a value of zero. No number can make the multiplication check return negative seven.

There is no number that passes the multiplication check, so the expression is undefined.

Finding two integers

The same pair must satisfy both the requested sum and product. Use the product to decide whether the signs match or oppose, then search factor pairs. Use the sum to choose the signs and sizes, and check both conditions.

Find two integers whose sum is \(7\) and whose product is \(12\).

A positive product requires two nonzero integers with the same sign. Since the sum is positive, both integers must be positive.

The positive factor pairs of 12 are 1 and 12; 2 and 6; 3 and 4.

The sizes 3 and 4 add to 7. Choose \(3\) and \(4\).

Check both conditions using the same pair (\(3 + 4 = 7\); \(3 \cdot 4 = 12\)).

Find two integers whose sum is \(-7\) and whose product is \(12\).

A positive product requires two nonzero integers with the same sign. Since the sum is negative, both integers must be negative.

The positive factor pairs of 12 are 1 and 12; 2 and 6; 3 and 4.

The sizes 3 and 4 add to 7. Choose \(-3\) and \(-4\).

Check both conditions using the same pair (\((-3) + (-4) = -7\); \((-3) \cdot (-4) = 12\)).
