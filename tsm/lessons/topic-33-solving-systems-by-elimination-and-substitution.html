Topic 33: Solving Systems by Elimination and Substitution

Assigned to: TSM 098 and TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic solves systems algebraically by substitution or elimination and recognizes systems with one, no, or infinitely many solutions.

Key definitions

Substitution: replace a variable with an equivalent expression. Elimination: add or subtract equations so one variable cancels. Infinitely many solutions: both equations describe the same line. No solution: the equations describe distinct parallel lines.

Elimination and substitution

Elimination combines equations to remove one variable. Multiply one or both entire equations when needed to obtain opposite coefficients. Substitution replaces an isolated variable with an equal expression from the other equation. Check the final ordered pair in both original equations.

Solve the system by elimination. Give the solution as an ordered pair and check it in both equations.

\[\begin{cases}2x+3y=13\\4x-3y=5\end{cases}\]

The y-coefficients are opposites. Add the equations to obtain \(6x=18\), while retaining the first equation.

\[\begin{cases}2x+3y=13\\6x=18\end{cases}\]

Divide the second equation by 6, then substitute its value of x into the first.

\[\begin{cases}6+3y=13\\x=3\end{cases}\]

Subtract 6 and divide by 3 in the first equation.

\[\begin{cases}x=3\\y=\frac73\end{cases}\]

The solution is \((3,\frac73)\). Check both original equations (\(2(3)+3(\frac73)=13\) and \(4(3)-3(\frac73)=5\)).

Solve by substitution and check in both equations: \(y=2x+1\) and \(3x+y=16\).

Replace y in the second equation with \(2x+1\), retaining the equation that defines y.

\[\begin{cases}y=2x+1\\3x+(2x+1)=16\end{cases}\]

Combine like terms and subtract 1.

\[\begin{cases}y=2x+1\\5x=15\end{cases}\]

Divide by 5, then use the resulting x-value to find y.

\[\begin{cases}x=3\\y=7\end{cases}\]

The solution is \((3,7)\). Both original equations check (\(7=2(3)+1\) and \(3(3)+7=16\)).

Special systems

A false statement after elimination or substitution means there is no solution. An identity means the two equations describe the same line and there are infinitely many solutions.

Solve and check the system: \(x+y=9\) and \(x-y=1\).

Add the equations to eliminate y, and retain the first equation.

\[\begin{cases}x+y=9\\2x=10\end{cases}\]

Divide the second equation by 2 and substitute into the first.

\[\begin{cases}x=5\\5+y=9\end{cases}\]

\[\begin{cases}x=5\\y=4\end{cases}\]

The solution is \((5,4)\). Check both equations (\(5+4=9\) and \(5-4=1\)).

Solve and check the system: \(y=3x-4\) and \(2x+y=11\).

Substitute the expression for y into the second equation while retaining the first.

\[\begin{cases}y=3x-4\\2x+3x-4=11\end{cases}\]

\[\begin{cases}y=3x-4\\5x=15\end{cases}\]

Divide by 5 and substitute the x-value into the first equation.

\[\begin{cases}x=3\\y=5\end{cases}\]

The solution is \((3,5)\). Check both equations (\(5=3(3)-4\) and \(2(3)+5=11\)).

Solve and check the system: \(2x+3y=8\) and \(5x-2y=1\).

Multiply the first equation by 2 and the second by 3 to obtain opposite y-coefficients.

\[\begin{cases}4x+6y=16\\15x-6y=3\end{cases}\]

Add the equations and retain the first.

\[\begin{cases}4x+6y=16\\19x=19\end{cases}\]

Divide the second equation by 19, then substitute into the first.

\[\begin{cases}4+6y=16\\x=1\end{cases}\]

\[\begin{cases}x=1\\y=2\end{cases}\]

The solution is \((1,2)\). Check both original equations (\(2(1)+3(2)=8\) and \(5(1)-2(2)=1\)).
