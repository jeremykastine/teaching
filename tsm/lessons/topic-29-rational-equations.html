Topic 29: Rational Equations

Assigned to: TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic solves equations containing fractions with variable denominators. Original denominator restrictions determine which answers are allowable.

Key definitions

Rational equation: an equation containing rational expressions. Restriction: a value that makes an original denominator zero. Extraneous solution: a candidate from transformed work that does not solve the original equation.

Solve

\[\frac{x^2-1}{x-1}=4\]

State restrictions before simplifying: \(x\ne1\), because the original denominator cannot be zero.

Factor the numerator, then cancel the common nonzero factor.

\[\frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x-1}=4\]

\[x+1=4\]

\[x=3\]

Simplifying removed the denominator, so there is no denominator left to move. The original restriction still applies.

Check: \(x=3\) is allowable, and \(\frac{3^2-1}{3-1}=\frac{8}{2}=4\).

Solve

\[\frac{1}{x-1}+\frac{2}{x+1}=1\]

The original denominators require \(x\ne1,-1\). Combine the left side using a common denominator, then simplify its numerator.

\[\frac{(x+1)+2(x-1)}{(x-1)(x+1)}=1\]

\[\frac{x+1+2x-2}{(x-1)(x+1)}=1\]

\[\frac{3x-1}{(x-1)(x+1)}=1\]

Only the left side has a denominator. Move the entire denominator to multiply the right side.

\[3x-1=1\cdot(x-1)(x+1)\]

Expand the product, move all terms to one side, and factor.

\[3x-1=x^2-1\]

\[0=x^2-3x\]

\[0=x(x-3)\]

\[x=0\quad\text{or}\quad x=3\]

Both candidates are allowable. Check in the original equation: when \(x=0\), the left side is \(-1+2=1\); when \(x=3\), it is \(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2}{4}=1\).

Solve

\[\frac{2}{x-1}+\frac{1}{x+1}=\frac{7}{x^2-1}\]

The restrictions are \(x\ne1,-1\). Factor the denominator on the right: \(x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)\). Combine and simplify the left side.

\[\frac{2(x+1)+(x-1)}{(x-1)(x+1)}=\frac{7}{(x-1)(x+1)}\]

\[\frac{3x+1}{(x-1)(x+1)}=\frac{7}{(x-1)(x+1)}\]

Each side is now one fraction. Move both denominators across in the same step, keeping each denominator as a whole factor.

\[(3x+1)(x-1)(x+1)=7(x-1)(x+1)\]

Because the restrictions make \((x-1)(x+1)\) nonzero, divide both sides by that common factor.

\[3x+1=7\]

\[3x=6\]

\[x=2\]

Check: 2 is allowable, and both sides of the original equation equal \(7/3\).

Move denominators only after each side has been combined into one fraction. In a sum of fractions, one denominator does not apply to every term on its side.

Solve

\[\frac{x}{x-2}=\frac{6}{x(x-2)}\]

The restrictions are \(x\ne0,2\). Each side is already one simplified fraction. Move both denominators across in one step.

\[x\cdot x(x-2)=6(x-2)\]

The common factor \(x-2\) is nonzero for every allowable value, so divide both sides by it.

\[x^2=6\]

\[x=\pm\sqrt{6}\]

Both candidates are allowable. To check either value in the original equation, use \(x^2=6\) to simplify the right side.

\[\frac{6}{x(x-2)}=\frac{x^2}{x(x-2)}=\frac{x}{x-2}\]

The two sides agree for both \(x=\sqrt{6}\) and \(x=-\sqrt{6}\).

Solve

\[\frac{x+1}{x-3}=\frac{4}{x-3}\]

The original denominators require \(x\ne3\). Each side is already one simplified fraction. Move both denominators across.

\[(x+1)(x-3)=4(x-3)\]

Since \(x-3\) is nonzero for allowable values, divide both sides by it.

\[x+1=4\]

\[x=3\]

This candidate violates the original restriction and makes both original denominators zero. Reject it. The original equation has no solution.

Always retain restrictions from the original equation, even when factors disappear during simplification. Check every candidate in the original equation.
