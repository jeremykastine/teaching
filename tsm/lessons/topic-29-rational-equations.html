Topic 29: Rational Equations

Assigned to: TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic solves equations containing fractions with variable denominators. Original denominator restrictions determine which answers are allowable.

Key definitions

A rational equation contains rational expressions. A restricted value makes an original denominator zero. An extraneous solution is a candidate that solves the resulting equation but fails in the original one.

Restrictions come first

Factor the original denominators and record every value that makes one of them zero. To find an LCD, include each distinct factor with the highest power that occurs in the denominators you are combining.

Combine and simplify each side as in Topic 28. When the denominators match, cancel like denominators and set the numerators equal. When they differ, multiply both sides by the LCD so the necessary factors multiply each numerator. The original restrictions ensure that this multiplier is nonzero.

The resulting equation may be linear or quadratic. Keep the original restrictions throughout and check each candidate in the original equation or reject it when substitution would give a zero denominator.

Solve

\[\frac{x^2-1}{x-1}=4\]

State restrictions before simplifying: \(x\ne1\), because the original denominator cannot be zero.

Factor the numerator, then cancel the common nonzero factor.

\[\frac{(x-1)(x+1)}{x-1}=4\]

\[x+1=4\]

\[x=3\]

The original restriction still applies after cancellation.

Check: \(x=3\) is allowable, and \(\frac{3^2-1}{3-1}=\frac{8}{2}=4\).

Solve

\[\frac{1}{x-1}+\frac{2}{x+1}=1\]

The original denominators require \(x\ne1,-1\). Combine the left side using a common denominator, then simplify its numerator.

\[\frac{(x+1)+2(x-1)}{(x-1)(x+1)}=1\]

\[\frac{x+1+2x-2}{(x-1)(x+1)}=1\]

\[\frac{3x-1}{(x-1)(x+1)}=1\]

Move the division by \((x-1)(x+1)\) to the other side as a multiplication.

\[3x-1=1\cdot(x-1)(x+1)\]

Expand the product, move all terms to one side, and factor.

\[3x-1=x^2-1\]

\[0=x^2-3x\]

\[0=x(x-3)\]

\[x=0\quad\text{or}\quad x=3\]

Both candidates are allowable. Check in the original equation: when \(x=0\), the left side is \(-1+2=1\); when \(x=3\), it is \(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{2}{4}=1\).

Solve

\[\frac{2}{x-1}+\frac{1}{x+1}=\frac{7}{x^2-1}\]

The restrictions are \(x\ne1,-1\). Factor the denominator on the right: \(x^2-1=(x-1)(x+1)\). Combine and simplify the left side.

\[\frac{2(x+1)+(x-1)}{(x-1)(x+1)}=\frac{7}{(x-1)(x+1)}\]

\[\frac{3x+1}{(x-1)(x+1)}=\frac{7}{(x-1)(x+1)}\]

Cancel like denominators by multiplying both sides by \((x-1)(x+1)\), which is nonzero for every allowable value of \(x\).

\[3x+1=7\]

\[3x=6\]

\[x=2\]

Check: 2 is allowable, and both sides of the original equation equal \(7/3\).

Solve

\[\frac{x}{x-2}=\frac{6}{x(x-2)}\]

The restrictions are \(x\ne0,2\). The LCD is \(x(x-2)\). Multiply both sides by this LCD. The factor \(x-2\) cancels on the left, leaving the extra factor \(x\), while the entire denominator cancels on the right.

\[x^2=6\]

\[x=\pm\sqrt{6}\]

Both candidates are allowable. To check either value in the original equation, use \(x^2=6\) to simplify the right side (\(\frac{6}{x(x-2)}=\frac{x^2}{x(x-2)}=\frac{x}{x-2}\)).

The two sides agree for both \(x=\sqrt{6}\) and \(x=-\sqrt{6}\).

Factoring to find the LCD

Look for a greatest common factor first. Then check for a difference of squares or a factorable trinomial. Two denominators can have factors in common even when they look different before factoring.

Solve

\[\frac{x+5}{3x-6}+\frac1{x-2}=\frac4{x-2}\]

Factor out the GCF in \(3x-6=3(x-2)\). The original denominators require \(x\ne2\). The LCD on the left is \(3(x-2)\), so multiply the second numerator and denominator by 3.

\[\frac{(x+5)+3}{3(x-2)}=\frac4{x-2}\]

\[\frac{x+8}{3(x-2)}=\frac4{x-2}\]

Multiply both sides by \(3(x-2)\). The entire denominator cancels on the left, and a factor of 3 remains on the right.

\[x+8=3(4)\]

\[x=4\]

At the allowable value \(x=4\), the original left side is \(\frac96+\frac12=2\) and the right side is \(\frac42=2\).

Solve

\[\frac2{x-3}+\frac1{x+2}=\frac{x+9}{x^2-x-6}\]

The numbers \(-3\) and \(2\) multiply to \(-6\) and add to \(-1\), so \(x^2-x-6=(x-3)(x+2)\). Exclude \(x=3\) and \(x=-2\). The factored trinomial is also the LCD of the left side.

\[\frac{2(x+2)+(x-3)}{(x-3)(x+2)}=\frac{x+9}{(x-3)(x+2)}\]

\[\frac{3x+1}{(x-3)(x+2)}=\frac{x+9}{(x-3)(x+2)}\]

Cancel like denominators and solve the linear equation.

\[3x+1=x+9\]

\[2x=8\]

\[x=4\]

At the allowable value \(x=4\), the original left side is \(2+\frac16=\frac{13}{6}\) and the right side is \(\frac{4+9}{16-4-6}=\frac{13}{6}\).

Solve

\[\frac3{2x-1}-\frac1{x+3}=\frac{3x+6}{2x^2+5x-3}\]

To factor the trinomial, split \(5x\) as \(6x-x\). Grouping gives \(2x(x+3)-(x+3)=(2x-1)(x+3)\). The original restrictions are \(x\ne\frac12,-3\).

Use \((2x-1)(x+3)\) to combine the left side.

\[\frac{3(x+3)-(2x-1)}{(2x-1)(x+3)}=\frac{3x+6}{(2x-1)(x+3)}\]

\[\frac{x+10}{(2x-1)(x+3)}=\frac{3x+6}{(2x-1)(x+3)}\]

Cancel like denominators, then collect like terms.

\[x+10=3x+6\]

\[4=2x\]

\[x=2\]

At the allowable value \(x=2\), the original left side is \(\frac33-\frac15=\frac45\) and the right side is \(\frac{12}{8+10-3}=\frac45\).

Solve

\[\frac3{2x^2-18}+\frac1{x-3}=\frac5{2(x+3)}\]

Factor out 2, then factor the difference of squares to get \(2x^2-18=2(x-3)(x+3)\). Exclude \(x=3\) and \(x=-3\).

The second fraction on the left needs the factor \(2(x+3)\) to match the first denominator.

\[\frac{3+2(x+3)}{2(x-3)(x+3)}=\frac5{2(x+3)}\]

\[\frac{2x+9}{2(x-3)(x+3)}=\frac5{2(x+3)}\]

Clear the denominators by multiplying both sides by \(2(x-3)(x+3)\). The extra factor on the right is \(x-3\).

\[2x+9=5(x-3)\]

\[2x+9=5x-15\]

\[24=3x\]

\[x=8\]

At the allowable value \(x=8\), the original left side is \(\frac3{110}+\frac15=\frac{25}{110}=\frac5{22}\) and the right side is \(\frac5{2(8+3)}=\frac5{22}\).

Quadratic equations and excluded candidates

When the denominators clear to leave a quadratic equation, solve it and check each candidate separately. A candidate that makes any original denominator zero is extraneous.

Solve

\[\frac2{x-2}+\frac3{x^2-4x+4}=1\]

The trinomial \(x^2-4x+4=(x-2)^2\) is a perfect square. The original denominators require \(x\ne2\). The LCD on the left includes two copies of \(x-2\).

\[\frac{2(x-2)+3}{(x-2)^2}=1\]

Move the division by \((x-2)^2\) to the other side as a multiplication, then expand and collect terms.

\[2(x-2)+3=(x-2)^2\]

\[2x-1=x^2-4x+4\]

\[0=x^2-6x+5\]

\[0=(x-1)(x-5)\]

\[x=1\quad\text{or}\quad x=5\]

Both are allowable. In the original equation, the left side at \(x=1\) is \(-2+3=1\), and at \(x=5\) it is \(\frac23+\frac39=1\). The solutions are \(1\) and \(5\).

Solve

\[\frac{x}{x-4}=\frac{24}{x^2-2x-8}\]

Factor \(x^2-2x-8=(x-4)(x+2)\). Exclude \(x=4\) and \(x=-2\).

\[\frac{x}{x-4}=\frac{24}{(x-4)(x+2)}\]

Clear the denominators by multiplying by their LCD, \((x-4)(x+2)\). A factor of \(x+2\) remains on the left.

\[x(x+2)=24\]

\[x^2+2x-24=0\]

\[(x+6)(x-4)=0\]

The quadratic gives candidates \(x=-6\) and \(x=4\). Reject \(4\) because it makes both original denominators zero. At \(x=-6\), the original sides are \(\frac{-6}{-10}=\frac35\) and \(\frac{24}{36+12-8}=\frac35\). The only solution is \(x=-6\).

Solve

\[\frac2{x-1}+\frac2{x+2}=\frac6{x^2+x-2}+1\]

Factor \(x^2+x-2=(x-1)(x+2)\). The original restrictions are \(x\ne1,-2\). Combine both sides using \((x-1)(x+2)\), writing the whole term \(1\) with that same denominator.

\[\frac{2(x+2)+2(x-1)}{(x-1)(x+2)}=\frac{6+(x-1)(x+2)}{(x-1)(x+2)}\]

Cancel like denominators and simplify the numerators.

\[4x+2=6+(x-1)(x+2)\]

\[4x+2=x^2+x+4\]

\[0=x^2-3x+2\]

\[0=(x-1)(x-2)\]

The quadratic gives candidates \(x=1\) and \(x=2\). Reject \(1\) because it makes original denominators zero. At \(x=2\), the original left side is \(2+\frac24=\frac52\) and the right side is \(\frac64+1=\frac52\). The only solution is \(x=2\).

Solve

\[\frac{x+1}{x-3}=\frac{4}{x-3}\]

The original denominators require \(x\ne3\). Cancel like denominators by multiplying both sides by \(x-3\), which is nonzero for allowable values.

\[x+1=4\]

\[x=3\]

This candidate violates the original restriction and makes both original denominators zero. Reject it. The original equation has no solution.
