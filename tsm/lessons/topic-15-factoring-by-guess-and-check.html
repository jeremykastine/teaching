Topic 15: Factoring by Guess and Check

Assigned to: TSM 098 and TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic factors trinomials by choosing binomial factors whose product recreates the original trinomial, especially when the leading coefficient is 1.

Key definitions

Trinomial: a polynomial with three terms. Leading coefficient: the coefficient of the highest-degree term. Factor pair: two numbers whose product is a specified number. Prime polynomial: a polynomial that does not factor over the integers.

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[t^{2}\ + \ 9t\ + \ 8\]

We need two integers with a sum of 9 and a product of 8. The numbers 8 and 1 work:

\[8\ + \ 1\ = \ 9\]

\[8(1)\ = \ 8\]

Now replace 9t with 8t + t. We have not changed the value of the expression; we have only rewritten its middle term.

\[t^{2}\ + \ 9t\ + \ 8\]

\[= \ t^{2}\ + \ 8t\ + \ t\ + \ 8\]

\[= \ t(t\ + \ 8)\ + \ 1(t\ + \ 8)\]

\[= \ (t\ + \ 8)(t\ + \ 1)\]

The two groups both contained t + 8, so that common binomial became one factor. The remaining factors, t and 1, formed the other binomial.

Factoring reverses multiplication

When we multiply two binomials, the distributive property produces a polynomial. Factoring asks us to reverse that process: we begin with a polynomial and rewrite it as a product. An expression is not completely factored until it is written as multiplication.

In this topic, the trinomials have a leading coefficient of 1. Their general form is:

\[x^{2}\ + \ bx\ + \ c\]

We are looking for two integers whose sum is the coefficient of the middle term and whose product is the constant term. If those integers are m and n, then:

\[m\ + \ n\ = \ b\]

\[mn\ = \ c\]

Once we find m and n, we split the middle term into two terms. That creates four terms, so we can use factoring by grouping.

\[x^{2}\ + \ bx\ + \ c\]

\[= \ x^{2}\ + \ mx\ + \ nx\ + \ c\]

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[s^{2}\ + \ 8s\ + \ 15\]

We need two integers whose sum is 8 and whose product is 15. The pair 3 and 5 satisfies both conditions:

\[3\ + \ 5\ = \ 8\]

\[3(5)\ = \ 15\]

Split the middle term and factor by grouping:

\[s^{2}\ + \ 8s\ + \ 15\]

\[= \ s^{2}\ + \ 3s\ + \ 5s\ + \ 15\]

\[= \ s(s\ + \ 3)\ + \ 5(s\ + \ 3)\]

\[= \ (s\ + \ 3)(s\ + \ 5)\]

The order of the two numbers does not affect the final answer. Splitting 8s as 5s + 3s would lead to the same two factors in the opposite order.

The shortcut when the leading coefficient is 1

The first two examples showed why the sum-and-product method works: the two numbers split the middle term, and factoring by grouping then produces two binomial factors. Once that pattern is familiar, we usually do not need to write out the grouping steps every time.

When the leading coefficient is 1, the first term of each factor must be x. If m and n are the two integers whose sum is b and whose product is c, those same two numbers become the constant terms in the factors:

\[x^{2}\ + \ bx\ + \ c\ = \ (x\ + \ m)(x\ + \ n)\]

In other words, after we find the correct two numbers, we can place them directly into the two parentheses. Factoring by grouping explains and justifies the shortcut; the template lets us move directly to the answer. We still pay close attention to the signs and check by multiplying when needed.

Use the signs to narrow the search

The signs of the two numbers are controlled by their sum and product. If the product is negative, one number must be positive and the other negative. If the product is positive, the two numbers must have the same sign. Their sum tells us whether both are positive or both are negative.

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[r^{2}\ + \ 2r\ - \ 63\]

The product must be -63, so the numbers have opposite signs. Their sum must be positive 2, so the positive number must have the larger absolute value. The factor pair 9 and 7 has a difference of 2. We therefore use 9 and -7:

\[9\ + \ ( - 7)\ = \ 2\]

\[9( - 7)\ = \ - 63\]

Because the leading coefficient is 1, we can now place 9 and -7 directly into the two binomial factors:

\[r^{2}\ + \ 2r\ - \ 63\ = \ (r\ + \ 9)(r\ - \ 7)\]

A quick multiplication check shows that the middle terms combine correctly:

\[(r\ + \ 9)(r\ - \ 7)\]

\[= \ r^{2}\ - \ 7r\ + \ 9r\ - \ 63\]

\[= \ r^{2}\ + \ 2r\ - \ 63\]

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[v^{2}\ - \ 13v\ + \ 40\]

The product is positive 40, so the two numbers have the same sign. Their sum is negative 13, so both numbers must be negative. The numbers -5 and -8 work:

\[( - 5)\ + \ ( - 8)\ = \ - 13\]

\[( - 5)( - 8)\ = \ 40\]

Again, the leading coefficient is 1, so we can use the shortcut and write the factors immediately:

\[v^{2}\ - \ 13v\ + \ 40\ = \ (v\ - \ 5)(v\ - \ 8)\]

We can check by multiplying the binomials. FOIL is simply a quick way to organize the distributive property:

\[(v\ - \ 5)(v\ - \ 8)\]

\[= \ v^{2}\ - \ 8v\ - \ 5v\ + \ 40\]

\[= \ v^{2}\ - \ 13v\ + \ 40\]

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[a^{2}\ + \ 7a\ + \ 60\]

We need two integers with a sum of 7 and a product of 60. If we begin with integer pairs that add to 7, the possibilities are 1 and 6, 2 and 5, or 3 and 4. Their products are:

\[1(6)\ = \ 6\]

\[2(5)\ = \ 10\]

\[3(4)\ = \ 12\]

None of these products is 60. Looking from the other direction gives the same conclusion: no factor pair of 60 has a sum of 7. Therefore this trinomial is not factorable using integers. We leave it as written.

\[a^{2}\ + \ 7a\ + \ 60\]

Factor completely over the integers, or state that it is not factorable.

\[t^{2}\ + \ 4tg\ - \ 21g^{2}\]

The same idea works when the last term contains another variable. Factor:

\[t^{2}\ + \ 4tg\ - \ 21g^{2}\]

The first terms of the factors must multiply to t squared, so they will be t and t. The last terms must multiply to -21g squared. We still search for two integer coefficients whose sum is 4 and whose product is -21. The numbers 7 and -3 work:

\[7\ + \ ( - 3)\ = \ 4\]

\[7( - 3)\ = \ - 21\]

Attach a factor of g to each of those numbers. The same shortcut now places 7g and -3g directly into the factors:

\[t^{2}\ + \ 4tg\ - \ 21g^{2}\ = \ (t\ + \ 7g)(t\ - \ 3g)\]

  1. Write the trinomial in descending order and confirm that its leading coefficient is 1.

  2. Identify the middle coefficient and the constant term.

  3. Find two integers whose sum is the middle coefficient and whose product is the constant term.

  4. Place those integers directly into the two binomial factors. The full grouping method may still be used whenever you want to show why the shortcut works.

  5. Check by multiplying. If no integer pair works, the trinomial does not factor over the integers.
