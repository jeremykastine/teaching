Topic 28: Linear Equations with Fractions

Assigned to: TSM 098 and TSM 099

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to show or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

This topic solves linear equations containing numerical fractions. Combining and simplifying each side makes the equation easier to solve.

Key definitions

A least common denominator (LCD) is the least positive common multiple (LCM) of the denominators. It lets us add or subtract fractions by writing them with the same denominator. A multi-term numerator contains a sum or difference.

Combine and simplify first

Combine the fractions on each side using that side's LCD. Once each side is a single fraction or a whole expression, use one of these moves.

  • If both sides have the same denominator, cancel like denominators. Multiply both sides by the shared denominator and set the numerators equal.
  • If the denominators differ, move the divisions to the other side as multiplications. Multiplying both sides by the product of the denominators makes each denominator cancel on its own side and multiply the entire numerator on the other side.
  • If only one side has a denominator, move that one division to the other side as a multiplication. This amounts to multiplying both sides by that denominator.

When different denominators share a factor, you can also multiply both sides by their LCD to keep the multipliers smaller.

Solve

\[\frac{x}{3}+\frac{x}{6}=5\]

Combine and simplify each side first. On the left, use a common denominator to add the fractions.

\[\frac{2x}{6}+\frac{x}{6}=5\]

\[\frac{3x}{6}=5\]

\[\frac{x}{2}=5\]

Move the division by 2 to the other side as a multiplication. Multiplying both sides by 2 cancels the division on the left and multiplies 5 on the right.

\[x=2(5)\]

\[x=10\]

Check in the original equation (\(\frac{10}{3}+\frac{10}{6}=\frac{20}{6}+\frac{10}{6}=5\)).

Solve

\[\frac{5}{4}x+\frac{1}{8}x=\frac{3}{8}+x\]

Combine each side into one fraction. Rewrite the left terms with denominator 8 and write the whole term on the right as a fraction with denominator 8.

\[\frac{10x}{8}+\frac{x}{8}=\frac{3}{8}+\frac{8x}{8}\]

\[\frac{11x}{8}=\frac{8x+3}{8}\]

Cancel like denominators by multiplying both sides by 8, then solve.

\[11x=8x+3\]

\[3x=3\]

\[x=1\]

Check: both sides of the original equation equal \(11/8\) when \(x=1\).

Solve

\[\frac{x+7}{5}-\frac{19}{15}=\frac{x-3}{6}\]

Combine the fractions on the left, then distribute and combine like terms in the numerator.

\[\frac{3(x+7)-19}{15}=\frac{x-3}{6}\]

\[\frac{3x+21-19}{15}=\frac{x-3}{6}\]

\[\frac{3x+2}{15}=\frac{x-3}{6}\]

Clear the denominators by multiplying both sides by 30, the LCD of 15 and 6. On the left, \(30\div15=2\). On the right, \(30\div6=5\). Keep each numerator together in parentheses.

\[2(3x+2)=5(x-3)\]

\[6x+4=5x-15\]

\[x=-19\]

Check in the original equation (\(\frac{-19+7}{5}-\frac{19}{15}=\frac{-36-19}{15}=-\frac{11}{3}\); \(\frac{-19-3}{6}=-\frac{11}{3}\)).

Solve

\[\frac{x+2}{8}=-\frac{x+3}{5}\]

Move the divisions to the other side as multiplications. Multiplying both sides by \(8\cdot5=40\) cancels the 8 on the left and the 5 on the right. The 5 multiplies the entire left numerator, and the 8 multiplies the entire right numerator. The negative sign still applies to the right side.

\[5(x+2)=-8(x+3)\]

Distribute and solve.

\[5x+10=-8x-24\]

\[13x=-34\]

\[x=-\frac{34}{13}\]

Check: both sides of the original equation equal \(-1/13\).

Finding less obvious LCDs

When the LCD is hard to see, list multiples of the largest denominator. Stop at the first multiple divisible by every denominator on that side.

Solve

\[\frac{5x+2}{18}-\frac{x-4}{24}=1\]

Of the first three multiples of 24 (24, 48, and 72), only 72 is divisible by 18. Multiply the first numerator by 4 and the second by 3 to write both fractions with denominator 72.

\[\frac{4(5x+2)-3(x-4)}{72}=1\]

The subtraction applies to the entire second numerator, giving \(-3x+12\).

\[\frac{17x+20}{72}=1\]

Multiply both sides by 72, then solve.

\[17x+20=72\]

\[x=\frac{52}{17}\]

At \(x=\frac{52}{17}\), the original left side is \(\frac{49}{51}-\left(-\frac{2}{51}\right)=1\), as required.

Solve

\[\frac{x+5}{24}+\frac{x-3}{36}-\frac{x+5}{54}=\frac{1}{12}\]

Of the first four multiples of 54 (54, 108, 162, and 216), only 216 is divisible by both 24 and 36. The multipliers on the left are 9, 6, and 4.

\[\frac{9(x+5)+6(x-3)-4(x+5)}{216}=\frac{1}{12}\]

On the left, \(9(x+5)-4(x+5)=5(x+5)\). On the right, \(12\) divides \(216\), so \(\frac{1}{12}=\frac{18}{216}\).

\[\frac{11x+7}{216}=\frac{18}{216}\]

Cancel like denominators and solve.

\[11x+7=18\]

\[x=1\]

At \(x=1\), the original left side is \(\frac14-\frac1{18}-\frac19=\frac1{12}\), as required.

Regroup fractions before combining

With four fractions, look for two pairs with easy LCDs. Use addition or subtraction on both sides to put one pair on each side. Combine each pair separately, then deal with the two remaining denominators.

Solve by regrouping the fractions so each side has an easy LCD.

\[\frac{x+2}{12}+\frac{x}{5}=\frac{x+4}{18}+\frac{x+12}{35}\]

Pair 12 with 18 (LCD 36) and 5 with 35 (LCD 35). Subtract \(\frac{x+4}{18}\) and \(\frac{x}{5}\) from both sides.

\[\frac{x+2}{12}-\frac{x+4}{18}=\frac{x+12}{35}-\frac{x}{5}\]

Combine each side using its own LCD.

\[\frac{3(x+2)-2(x+4)}{36}=\frac{(x+12)-7x}{35}\]

\[\frac{x-2}{36}=\frac{12-6x}{35}\]

Move the divisions to the other side as multiplications. Multiplying both sides by \(36\cdot35\) leaves 35 multiplying the entire left numerator and 36 multiplying the entire right numerator.

\[35(x-2)=36(12-6x)\]

\[35x-70=432-216x\]

\[251x=502\]

\[x=2\]

At \(x=2\), the original left side is \(\frac4{12}+\frac25\) and the right side is \(\frac6{18}+\frac{14}{35}\). Both equal \(\frac13+\frac25=\frac{11}{15}\).

Solve by regrouping the fractions so each side has an easy LCD.

\[\frac{2x-2}{8}-\frac{x+1}{10}=\frac{x+6}{30}-\frac{x-2}{24}\]

Pair 8 with 24 (LCD 24) and 10 with 30 (LCD 30). Add \(\frac{x-2}{24}\) and \(\frac{x+1}{10}\) to both sides.

\[\frac{2x-2}{8}+\frac{x-2}{24}=\frac{x+6}{30}+\frac{x+1}{10}\]

Combine and simplify each side.

\[\frac{3(2x-2)+(x-2)}{24}=\frac{(x+6)+3(x+1)}{30}\]

\[\frac{7x-8}{24}=\frac{4x+9}{30}\]

Clear the denominators using 120, their LCD. This leaves a multiplier of 5 on the left and 4 on the right.

\[5(7x-8)=4(4x+9)\]

\[35x-40=16x+36\]

\[19x=76\]

\[x=4\]

At \(x=4\), the original left side is \(\frac68-\frac5{10}=\frac14\) and the right side is \(\frac{10}{30}-\frac2{24}=\frac14\).

When the variable terms cancel

If the variable terms cancel, read the remaining statement. A false statement means there is no solution. A true statement means every real number is a solution because the original numerical denominators are nonzero for every value of \(x\).

Solve

\[\frac{2x+3}{14}-\frac{x-1}{21}=\frac{4x+10}{42}\]

The LCD on the left is 42. Subtract the whole second numerator when combining.

\[\frac{3(2x+3)-2(x-1)}{42}=\frac{4x+10}{42}\]

\[\frac{4x+11}{42}=\frac{4x+10}{42}\]

Cancel like denominators, then subtract \(4x\) from both sides.

\[4x+11=4x+10\]

\[11=10\]

The statement is false for every \(x\), so there is no solution.

Solve

\[\frac{x-2}{9}+\frac{x+4}{6}=\frac{5x+8}{18}\]

The LCD on the left is 18. Combine and simplify the left numerator.

\[\frac{2(x-2)+3(x+4)}{18}=\frac{5x+8}{18}\]

\[\frac{5x+8}{18}=\frac{5x+8}{18}\]

Cancel like denominators and subtract \(5x\) from both sides.

\[5x+8=5x+8\]

\[8=8\]

The statement is true for every \(x\), so the solution set is all real numbers.
