Lecture and Assessment Plan

Master lecture notes

Updated November 10, 2026

Twenty essential lectures, each designed for one hour, provide the methods students need for their course assessments. Review activities reinforce the material, and extension activities broaden it as time permits. Every assessed problem should be reasonably solvable using methods taught in the essential lectures.

Lecture 1 establishes signed-number operations and multiplication facts. Subsequent lectures teach arithmetic when it becomes relevant to the algebra. Numerical fractions and rational expressions share Lectures 8–10. Their explicit assessment differs by course.

Four unit tests in each course

  • TSM 098 — Test 1 Expressions, Test 2 Numerical Fractions, Test 3 Linear and Absolute Value Equations, Test 4 Linear Systems by Substitution.
  • TSM 099 — Test 1 Expressions and Factoring, Test 2 Fractions and Rational Expressions, Test 3 Equations, Test 4 Graphing and Writing Linear Equations.

Blank assessment cells indicate no explicit testing. A test number alone indicates full topic coverage. Additional text identifies partial coverage. Test 2 in TSM 099 includes numerical fractions and predominantly rational expressions.

Lecture 1Signed-number operations and multiplication facts Compute signed sums, differences, products, and quotients. Emphasize recognition of multiplication facts in both directions, such as seeing 56 as 7 × 8. Connect products, factors, signs, and factor pairs to the calculations students will meet in algebra.
Lecture 2Combining like terms and like factors Add and subtract like terms, distinguish terms from factors, and combine repeated variable factors. Introduce positive-integer powers as repeated multiplication and evaluate expressions using the relevant order of operations. Examples include 3x + 5x − 2x and 2x2 · 3x3. Test 1
Lecture 3The distributive property Distribute a numerical or monomial factor, distribute a minus sign, and combine like terms within larger expressions. Use examples such as 3(2x − 5) − 2(x + 4) and 2x(3x − 4). Revisit signed multiplication, parentheses, and order of operations as needed. Test 1
Lecture 4Multiplying polynomials Multiply each term by each term, apply the rules for like factors, and combine like terms. Include binomial products for both courses and larger products involving trinomials for TSM 098. Use signed coefficients and single-variable polynomials. Test 1Test 1 · Binomial products only
Lecture 5GCF factoring and cubic factoring by grouping List divisors to find a numerical GCF, identify the shared variable factors, and factor out the complete GCF. Introduce useful divisibility facts here. Then factor four-term cubic polynomials by grouping, such as x3 + 2x2 + 3x + 6. Check by multiplying back. Test 1 · GCF factoring onlyTest 1
Lecture 6Monic trinomials and differences of squares Factor x2 + bx + c by finding and checking signed factor pairs. Recognize and factor a difference of squares. Review products, sums, signs, and perfect squares as they become relevant. Include recognition of expressions that do not factor over the integers. Test 1 · Monic trinomials onlyTest 1
Lecture 7Nonmonic trinomials by guess and check Organize possible factors of the leading coefficient and constant term in a table, choose signs, and check the middle coefficient by multiplication. Include examples such as 6x2 + 11x + 3, varied signs, and a case that does not factor over the integers. Use manageable factor searches. Test 1
Lecture 8Simplifying fractions and rational expressions Reduce numerical fractions by common factors, in one step or in stages. Factor polynomial numerators and denominators, then cancel common factors, including opposite factors. Introduce division by zero and restrictions from the original denominators. Distinguish cancellation of factors from cancellation within sums. Test 2 · Numerical fractions onlyTest 2
Lecture 9Multiplying and dividing fractions and rational expressions Multiply fractions, rewrite division using a reciprocal, factor when necessary, and simplify by cancellation. Include signed numerical fractions, unit fractions with numerator 1, and rational expressions with different factoring patterns. Explain the nonzero conditions on denominators and divisors. Test 2 · Numerical fractions onlyTest 2
Lecture 10Adding and subtracting fractions and rational expressions Start with common denominators, then find numerical LCMs by listing multiples and algebraic LCDs from factored denominators. Make equivalent fractions, combine numerators, distribute subtraction across every term, and simplify. Include numerical and rational examples that cancel at the end and others that do not. Test 2 · Numerical fractions onlyTest 2
Lecture 11Linear equations without fractions Solve from simple coefficient equations through equations with terms on both sides and parentheses. Distribute, combine like terms, and use equality-preserving operations. Include one solution, no solution, and all real numbers. Explain what happens when the remaining variable coefficient is zero. Test 3
Lecture 12Absolute value equations Interpret absolute value as distance from zero. Isolate an absolute value and solve both linear cases, such as |2x − 3| = 5. Include zero and negative right-hand sides. Establish √(u2) = |u| for real u as preparation for taking square roots of quadratic equations. Test 3Test 3
Lecture 13Quadratic equations by factoring Move terms to one side, factor the quadratic, and use the zero-product property to find all solutions. Connect the existing factoring methods to equation solving and check the resulting values. Test 3
Lecture 14Quadratic equations by square roots and the quadratic formula Solve forms such as (x − 3)2 = 16 by taking principal square roots to obtain an absolute value equation. Put most of the hour into identifying coefficients and applying the quadratic formula. Teach square-root evaluation, simplification by perfect-square factors, signed substitution, and fraction reduction within these problems. Address negative radicands and the imaginary unit where complex answers are requested. Test 3
Lecture 15Linear equations with numerical fractions Combine and simplify each side first using common denominators as needed. Once a denominator divides a whole side, move it to multiply the entire opposite side. Include denominators on one side and on both sides, and retain parentheses around complete numerators. This lecture immediately precedes rational equations. Test 3Test 3
Lecture 16Rational equations State restrictions from the original denominators, combine and simplify each side, and move remaining whole-side denominators to multiply the opposite side. Solve the resulting linear or quadratic equation and check candidates in the original equation. Include extraneous solutions caused by excluded values. Test 3
Lecture 17Graphing linear equations Interpret ordered pairs as solutions, plot points, and graph from a table or intercepts. Introduce slope as rise over run and graph equations in slope-intercept form. Include horizontal and vertical lines in instruction. Review signed coordinates, substitution, and fractional rise/run as needed. Test 4
Lecture 18Solving systems by graphing Graph both equations, interpret their intersection as a simultaneous solution, and check the ordered pair in both equations. Include one intersection, parallel lines, and coincident lines to connect the graph to one, zero, or infinitely many solutions. Test 4
Lecture 19Solving linear systems by substitution Isolate a variable when necessary, substitute the complete expression into the other equation, solve, and substitute back. Include systems with a variable already isolated, systems needing rearrangement, and inconsistent or dependent systems. Use the preceding graphs to interpret the algebraic results. Test 4
Lecture 20Writing equations of lines Find slope from a graph or from two points, then use a point to determine the equation. Include writing an equation from a displayed line and from two supplied points. Revisit differences of signed coordinates, fraction reduction, and solving for the intercept. Test 4

Instructional decisions

  • Arithmetic enters where it supports the algebra. Teach useful divisibility facts and lists of divisors with GCF factoring, lists of multiples with common denominators, and division-by-zero restrictions with fractions and equation solving.
  • Find numerical GCFs and LCMs by lists. Prime factorization is outside the essential lecture sequence.
  • Use grouping for four-term cubic polynomials. Trinomials use guess and check throughout, including nonmonic trinomials.
  • Use absolute value equations to explain taking square roots of a square. The second quadratic lecture emphasizes the quadratic formula, alongside the square-root method.
  • Word problems belong to extension activities and carry no explicit unit-test coverage in this plan.
  • Reserve room for cancellations and optional activities. A section with 25 meetings has five meetings beyond the 20 essential lectures. A section with 20 longer meetings can pair lectures when needed, adjusting review and testing time on those days.
