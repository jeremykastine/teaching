Lecture and Assessment Plan

Master lecture notes

Updated November 25, 2026

Twenty essential lectures, each designed for one hour, provide the methods students need for their course assessments. Review activities reinforce the material, and extension activities broaden it as time permits. Every assessed problem should be reasonably solvable using methods taught in the essential lectures.

The named units are Expressions and Factoring; Fractions and Rational Expressions; Equations; and Graphs and Linear Systems. Signed-number operations and multiplication facts establish the arithmetic foundation. Later topics teach arithmetic when it becomes relevant to the algebra. Numerical fractions and rational expressions share a unit; their explicit assessment differs by course.

Four unit tests in each course

  • TSM 098 — Test 1 Expressions, Test 2 Numerical Fractions, Test 3 Linear and Absolute Value Equations, Test 4 Linear Systems by Substitution.
  • TSM 099 — Test 1 Expressions and Factoring, Test 2 Fractions and Rational Expressions, Test 3 Equations, Test 4 Graphing and Writing Linear Equations.

Blank assessment cells indicate no explicit testing. A test number alone indicates full topic coverage. Additional text identifies partial coverage. Test 2 in TSM 099 includes numerical fractions and predominantly rational expressions.

Use real numbers throughout the lectures and tests, including the final. Students should be able to explain when an equation has no real solution. Imaginary numbers are a possible unassessed supplemental activity.

Expressions and Factoring
Signed-number operations and multiplication facts Compute signed sums, differences, products, and quotients. Emphasize recognition of multiplication facts in both directions, such as seeing 56 as 7 × 8. Connect products, factors, signs, and factor pairs to the calculations students will meet in algebra.
Combining like terms and like factors Add and subtract like terms, distinguish terms from factors, and combine repeated variable factors. Fractions and Rational Expressions
Simplifying fractions and rational expressions Reduce numerical fractions by common factors, in one step or in stages. Factor polynomial numerators and denominators, then cancel common factors, including opposite factors. Introduce division by zero and restrictions from the original denominators. Distinguish cancellation of factors from cancellation within sums. Test 2 · Numerical fractions onlyTest 2
Multiplying and dividing fractions and rational expressions Multiply fractions, rewrite division using a reciprocal, factor when necessary, and simplify by cancellation. Include signed numerical fractions, unit fractions with numerator 1, and rational expressions with different factoring patterns. Explain the nonzero conditions on denominators and divisors. Make equivalent fractions, combine numerators, distribute subtraction across every term, and simplify. Include numerical and rational examples that cancel at the end and others that do not. Equations
Linear equations without fractions Solve from simple coefficient equations through equations with terms on both sides and parentheses. Distribute, combine like terms, and use equality-preserving operations. Include one solution, no solution, and all real numbers. Explain what happens when the remaining variable coefficient is zero. Test 3
Absolute value and absolute value equations Introduce absolute value as distance from zero and evaluate it for positive numbers, negative numbers, and zero. Isolate the absolute value in equations such as |2x − 3| = 5. Use the sign of the right-hand side to decide whether there are two cases, one case, or no real solution. Establish √(u2) = |u| for real u in preparation for Quadratic equations by square roots and the quadratic formula. Test 3Test 3
Quadratic equations by factoring Move terms to one side, factor the quadratic, and use the zero-product property to find all solutions. Connect the existing factoring methods to equation solving and check the resulting values. Test 3
Quadratic equations by square roots and the quadratic formula Apply the absolute value approach from Absolute value and absolute value equations. For example, take principal square roots in (x − 3)2 = 16 to obtain |x − 3| = 4. Put most of the hour into identifying coefficients and applying the quadratic formula. Teach square-root evaluation, simplification by perfect-square factors, signed substitution, and fraction reduction within these problems. Explain why a square equal to a negative number or a negative discriminant gives no real solution. Test 3
Linear equations with numerical fractions Combine and simplify each side first using common denominators as needed. Once a denominator divides a whole side, move it to multiply the entire opposite side. Include denominators on one side and on both sides, and retain parentheses around complete numerators. This lecture immediately precedes rational equations. Test 3Test 3
Rational equations State restrictions from the original denominators, combine and simplify each side, and move remaining whole-side denominators to multiply the opposite side. Solve the resulting linear or quadratic equation and check candidates in the original equation. Include extraneous solutions caused by excluded values. Test 3
Graphs and Linear Systems
Graphing linear equations Interpret ordered pairs as solutions, plot points, and graph from a table or intercepts. Introduce slope as rise over run and graph equations in slope-intercept form. Include horizontal and vertical lines in instruction. Review signed coordinates, substitution, and fractional rise/run as needed. Test 4
Solving systems by graphing Graph both equations, interpret their intersection as a simultaneous solution, and check the ordered pair in both equations. Include one intersection, parallel lines, and coincident lines to connect the graph to one, zero, or infinitely many solutions. Test 4
Solving linear systems by substitution Isolate a variable when necessary, substitute the complete expression into the other equation, solve, and substitute back. Include systems with a variable already isolated, systems needing rearrangement, and inconsistent or dependent systems. Use the preceding graphs to interpret the algebraic results. Test 4
Writing equations of lines Find slope from a graph or from two points, then use a point to determine the equation. Include writing an equation from a displayed line and from two supplied points. Revisit differences of signed coordinates, fraction reduction, and solving for the intercept. Test 4

Instructional decisions

  • Arithmetic enters where it supports the algebra. Teach useful divisibility facts and lists of divisors with GCF factoring, lists of multiples with common denominators, and division-by-zero restrictions with fractions and equation solving.
  • Find numerical GCFs and LCMs by lists. Prime factorization is outside the essential lecture sequence.
  • Use grouping for four-term cubic polynomials. Trinomials use guess and check throughout, including nonmonic trinomials.
  • Use absolute value equations to explain taking square roots of a square. The quadratic formula lesson emphasizes that formula alongside the square-root method.
  • Word problems belong to extension activities and carry no explicit unit-test coverage in this plan.
  • Reserve room for cancellations and optional activities. A section with 25 meetings has five meetings beyond the 20 essential lectures. A section with 20 longer meetings can pair lectures when needed, adjusting review and testing time on those days.
