Lecture 18: Solving systems by graphing

Assessment coverage TSM 099 test 4

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

A system solution satisfies both equations at the same time. On a graph it is a point belonging to both lines.

Key definitions

Intersecting lines have one common point. Distinct parallel lines have none. Coincident lines are two equations for the same line.

Graph \(y=x+1\) and \(y=-x+5\). Find the intersection and verify it.

Plot the first using \((0,1),(2,3)\) and the second using \((0,5),(2,3)\). The intersection is \((2,3)\). Both \(2+1\) and \(-2+5\) equal \(3\).

Graph \(x+y=4\) and \(y=2x-2\). Identify and check the solution.

The first line has intercepts \((4,0),(0,4)\). The second starts at \((0,-2)\) and rises two for each step right. The intersection is \((2,2)\). Check \(2+2=4\) and \(2(2)-2=2\).

Graph \(y=-x+2\) and \(y=-x-1\). State the number of solutions.

The lines have slope \(-1\) and different vertical intercepts. Their graphs are parallel, so there is no solution.

Graph \(2x+y=6\) and \(4x+2y=12\). Describe all solutions.

Dividing the second equation by \(2\) gives the first. Both graphs pass through \((0,6)\) and \((3,0)\) and coincide. Every point on \(y=6-2x\) solves the system.

Graph \(x=-2\) and \(y=3\). Verify their intersection.

The vertical line has \(x=-2\) everywhere and the horizontal line has \(y=3\) everywhere. Their unique common point is \((-2,3)\).

Find the intersection by graphing \(y=\frac12x-1\) and \(y=-x+2\). Check it in both equations.

The first line goes through \((0,-1)\) and \((2,0)\). The second goes through \((0,2)\) and \((2,0)\). The intersection is \((2,0)\), and substitution gives \(0\) from each right side.
