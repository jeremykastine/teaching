Lecture 13: Quadratic equations by factoring

Assessment coverage TSM 099 test 3

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Use factoring to turn a quadratic equation into a product equal to zero. Each factor supplies a possible solution.

Key definitions

The zero-product property says that a product is zero exactly when at least one of its factors is zero.

Solve \(x^2-7x+12=0\).

The factor pair \(-3,-4\) gives \((x-3)(x-4)=0\). Set each factor equal to zero to obtain \(x=3,4\).

Solve \(2x^2=10x\).

Subtract \(10x\), then factor out \(2x\).

\[2x(x-5)=0\]

The factors give \(x=0\) or \(x=5\). Both make the original sides equal.

Solve \(9x^2-16=0\).

Factor the difference of squares. From \((3x-4)(3x+4)=0\), obtain \(x=4/3\) or \(x=-4/3\).

Solve \(6x^2+11x=-3\).

Move \(-3\) to the left by adding \(3\). The trinomial factors as \((2x+3)(3x+1)\). The two solutions are \(-3/2\) and \(-1/3\).

Solve \(x^2+25=10x\).

Move \(10x\) left. The remaining expression is \((x-5)^2\). Both identical factors give the single distinct solution \(x=5\).

Solve and check \(3x^2-4x=4\).

After subtracting \(4\), factor as \((3x+2)(x-2)=0\). The candidates are \(-2/3\) and \(2\). Substituting gives \(4/3+8/3=4\) and \(12-8=4\).
