Lecture 14: Quadratic equations by square roots and the quadratic formula

Assessment coverage TSM 099 test 3

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

When a square is isolated, take principal square roots and solve the resulting absolute value equation. For a general quadratic, first put it in standard form and identify its signed coefficients.

Key definitions

For \(ax^2+bx+c=0\) with \(a\ne0\), the quadratic formula is \(x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}\). The radicand \(b^2-4ac\) is the discriminant. The principal square root of a nonnegative real number is nonnegative.

Taking square roots

Solve \((x-3)^2=16\).

Take principal square roots of both sides to get \(|x-3|=4\). Then \(x-3=4\) or \(x-3=-4\), giving \(7\) and \(-1\).

Solve \(2(x+1)^2=18\).

Divide by \(2\) first. Taking square roots gives \(|x+1|=3\). The solutions are \(2\) and \(-4\).

The quadratic formula

One perfect-square factor can come out of a radical. Substitute negative coefficients with parentheses, and simplify the entire numerator before reducing.

Use the formula to solve \(3x^2-x-2=0\).

Use \(a=3,b=-1,c=-2\). The discriminant is \(1+24=25\).

\[x=\frac{1\pm\sqrt{25}}6=\frac{1\pm5}{6}\]

The solutions are \(1\) and \(-2/3\).

Use the formula to solve \(2x^2+4x-1=0\) exactly.

Here \(a=2,b=4,c=-1\), so the discriminant is \(16+8=24\). The square factor \(4\) leaves \(\sqrt{24}=2\sqrt6\).

\[x=\frac{-4\pm2\sqrt6}{4}=\frac{-2\pm\sqrt6}{2}\]

Use the formula to solve \(2x^2+8x= -8\).

Add \(8\) before identifying \(c\). The discriminant is \(64-64=0\). The only distinct solution is \(-8/4=-2\).

Use the formula to solve \(x^2-5x=2\) exactly.

Use \(a=1,b=-5,c=-2\). The discriminant is \(25+8=33\), with no square factor larger than \(1\).

\[x=\frac{5\pm\sqrt{33}}2\]

Solve \(x^2+4x+8=0\) over the real numbers. Give the answers in terms of \(i\) if complex answers are requested.

The discriminant is \(16-32=-16\), so there are no real solutions. Using \(i^2=-1\) and \(\sqrt{-16}=4i\), the formula gives \((-4\pm4i)/2=-2\pm2i\).

Use the formula to solve \(4x^2-12x-7=0\) and reduce both answers.

The discriminant is \(144+112=256\), so \(x=(12\pm16)/8\). The two fractions reduce to \(7/2\) and \(-1/2\).
