Lecture 20: Writing equations of lines

Assessment coverage TSM 099 test 4

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Find the slope first, then use a point to locate the particular line with that slope.

Key definitions

For two points with different \(x\)-coordinates, \(m=\frac{y_2-y_1}{x_2-x_1}\). The slope-intercept form is \(y=mx+b\), where \((0,b)\) is the vertical intercept.

Find the slope of the line through \((-2,5)\) and \((4,-1)\).

Use the same point order for both differences.

\[m=\frac{-1-5}{4-(-2)}=\frac{-6}{6}=-1\]

Write the equation of the displayed line in slope-intercept form.

From the marked points, move right \(3\) and down \(2\). Thus \(m=-2/3\). The vertical intercept is \(2\).

\[y=-\frac23x+2\]

Write the equation through \((-3,4)\) and \((3,2)\).

The slope is \((2-4)/(3-(-3))=-1/3\). Substitute \((3,2)\) to get \(2=-1+b\), hence \(b=3\). The equation is \(y=-x/3+3\).

Write an equation of the line with slope \(3/2\) through \((4,-1)\).

Substitution into \(y=mx+b\) gives \(-1=(3/2)(4)+b\). Thus \(b=-7\) and \(y=3x/2-7\).

Write the equations through \((-2,1),(4,1)\) and through \((5,-2),(5,4)\).

In the first pair, \(y\) never changes, giving \(y=1\). In the second pair, \(x\) never changes, giving \(x=5\). The slopes are \(0\) and undefined, respectively.

Write and check the equation through \((-4,-3)\) and \((2,6)\).

The slope is \((6+3)/(2+4)=3/2\). Use \((2,6)\) to get \(6=3+b\), so \(b=3\). The equation \(y=3x/2+3\) gives \(-3\) at \(x=-4\) and \(6\) at \(x=2\).
