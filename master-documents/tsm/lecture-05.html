Lecture 5: GCF factoring and cubic factoring by grouping

Assessment coverage TSM 098 test 1 · GCF factoring only · TSM 099 test 1

Overview

Reverse distribution by identifying a shared factor. A four-term cubic may have two groups that reveal the same binomial factor.

Key definitions

A divisor divides an integer with remainder zero. The greatest common factor (GCF) of terms includes the greatest positive common divisor of their coefficients and the smallest power of each shared variable.

List divisors and factor out a GCF

An even last digit gives divisibility by \(2\). A last digit of \(0\) or \(5\) gives divisibility by \(5\), and a last digit of \(0\) gives divisibility by \(10\). For \(3\) and \(9\), check whether the digit sum is a multiple of the proposed divisor. List positive divisors in pairs, stopping when the pairs begin to repeat.

Find the GCF of \(90\) and \(126\) by listing divisors.

The divisors of \(90\) are \(1,2,3,5,6,9,10,15,18,30,45,90\). Those of \(126\) are \(1,2,3,6,7,9,14,18,21,42,63,126\). The largest common divisor is \(18\).

Factor out the complete GCF of \(18x^4-24x^3+30x^2\).

The GCF of \(18,24,30\) is \(6\). All three terms contain at least two factors of \(x\). Divide each term by \(6x^2\) to find the terms inside the parentheses.

\[18x^4-24x^3+30x^2=6x^2(3x^2-4x+5)\]

Factor \(-15x^3+25x^2\). Use a negative factor so the remaining leading term is positive.

The common factor has magnitude \(5x^2\). Dividing by \(-5x^2\) gives \(3x\) and \(-5\).

\[-15x^3+25x^2=-5x^2(3x-5)\]

Grouping four-term cubics

Factor each pair and check whether a binomial is common to the two results. Choose a negative GCF when that makes the binomials match.

Factor \(x^3+2x^2+3x+6\) by grouping.

The first pair has factor \(x^2\). The second pair has factor \(3\). Both leave \(x+2\).

\[x^3+2x^2+3x+6=x^2(x+2)+3(x+2)=(x+2)(x^2+3)\]

Factor \(2x^3-6x^2-5x+15\) by grouping and check by multiplication.

Factor \(-5\) from the second pair to leave \(x-3\) in both groups.

\[2x^3-6x^2-5x+15=2x^2(x-3)-5(x-3)=(x-3)(2x^2-5)\]

Multiplying back gives \(2x^3-6x^2-5x+15\).

Factor \(3x^3+2x-6x^2-4\) by grouping.

Put the powers in descending order so the first pair contains \(x^2\) and the second pair has factor \(2\).

\[3x^3+2x-6x^2-4=3x^2(x-2)+2(x-2)=(x-2)(3x^2+2)\]
