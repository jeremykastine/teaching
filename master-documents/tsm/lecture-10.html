Lecture 10: Adding and subtracting fractions and rational expressions

Assessment coverage TSM 098 test 2 · Numerical fractions only · TSM 099 test 2

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Add or subtract the numerators once the denominators match. When subtracting, the minus sign applies to the entire second numerator.

Key definitions

The least common multiple (LCM) is the smallest positive common multiple of whole-number denominators. A least common denominator (LCD) takes the largest required power of each denominator factor.

Numerical fractions

Add \(\frac5{18}+\frac7{18}\) and simplify.

There are \(12\) eighteenths. Divide numerator and denominator by \(6\).

\[\frac5{18}+\frac7{18}=\frac{12}{18}=\frac23\]

Find the LCM of \(8\) and \(12\) by listing multiples, then calculate \(-\frac58+\frac7{12}\).

The multiples of \(8\) begin \(8,16,24\). Those of \(12\) begin \(12,24\). With denominator \(24\), the numerators become \(-15\) and \(14\).

\[-\frac58+\frac7{12}=-\frac{15}{24}+\frac{14}{24}=-\frac1{24}\]

Subtract \(\frac5{14}-\frac9{21}\).

The first common multiple is \(42\). Multiply the first numerator by \(3\) and the second by \(2\).

\[\frac5{14}-\frac9{21}=\frac{15-18}{42}=-\frac1{14}\]

Rational expressions

Add \(\frac{x^2+1}{x-2}+\frac{x-7}{x-2}\) and simplify.

Combine the numerators. The result factors as \((x+3)(x-2)\).

\[\frac{x^2+1+x-7}{x-2}=\frac{(x+3)(x-2)}{x-2}=x+3,\qquad x\ne2\]

Subtract \(\frac{x^2+2x-5}{x-2}-\frac{x-2}{x-2}\) and decide whether a factor cancels.

Subtracting \(x-2\) adds \(-x+2\). The numerator becomes \(x^2+x-3\). Its value at \(2\) is \(3\), so \(x-2\) is not a factor.

\[\frac{x^2+2x-5}{x-2}-\frac{x-2}{x-2}=\frac{x^2+x-3}{x-2},\qquad x\ne2\]

Simplify \(\frac{x}{x^2-4}-\frac1{x+2}\).

The LCD is \((x-2)(x+2)\). The second numerator becomes \(x-2\). Subtracting it leaves \(2\).

\[\frac{x}{x^2-4}-\frac1{x+2}=\frac{x-(x-2)}{(x-2)(x+2)}=\frac2{x^2-4},\qquad x\ne\pm2\]

Simplify \(\frac1{x-1}-\frac{2x-3}{(x-1)^2}\).

The LCD keeps the squared factor. The numerator is \((x-1)-(2x-3)=2-x\), which is \(1\) at the excluded input \(1\), so nothing cancels.

\[\frac1{x-1}-\frac{2x-3}{(x-1)^2}=\frac{2-x}{(x-1)^2},\qquad x\ne1\]

Simplify \(\frac{x}{x^2-9}+\frac{3}{x^2-9}+\frac{1}{x-3}\).

Exclude \(x=\pm3\). The denominator \(x^2-9\) already contains the factor \(x-3\). Multiply the last numerator by \(x+3\) so all three denominators match.

\[\frac{x+3+(x+3)}{(x-3)(x+3)}=\frac{2(x+3)}{(x-3)(x+3)}=\frac2{x-3},\qquad x\ne\pm3\]

Simplify \(\frac{x}{2x^2-5x-3}-\frac1{1-2x}\).

Factor the first denominator as \((2x+1)(x-3)\) and write \(1-2x=-(2x-1)\). These denominators have no common factor. The original exclusions are \(-1/2,3,1/2\).

\[\frac{x(2x-1)+(2x+1)(x-3)}{(2x+1)(x-3)(2x-1)}=\frac{4x^2-6x-3}{(2x+1)(x-3)(2x-1)}\]

The numerator is nonzero at all three excluded inputs, so no factor cancels.
