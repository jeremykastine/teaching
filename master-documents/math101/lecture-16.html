Lecture 16: Logarithmic Functions and Equations

Assessment coverage Test 3

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Every original logarithm needs a positive argument. Keep those conditions in view while solving and check each candidate.

Key definitions

For \(b>0\), \(b\ne1\), \(\log_b u=v\) means \(b^v=u\). For positive arguments, a sum of logs becomes the log of a product, a difference becomes the log of a quotient, and a coefficient can become an exponent. Natural logarithms have base \(e\).

Solve \(\log_3(x-2)=2\).

The domain condition is \(x>2\). In exponential form \(x-2=3^2=9\), so \(x=11\). The original argument is positive and \(\log_3 9=2\).

Solve \(\log_2 x+\log_2(x-2)=3\).

Both arguments are positive only for \(x>2\). Combine the logs to get \(\log_2(x(x-2))=3\), then \(x(x-2)=8\). Factoring \(x^2-2x-8=(x-4)(x+2)\) gives candidates \(4,-2\). Only \(4\) is allowed, and \(\log_2 4+\log_2 2=3\).

Solve \(\log_3(x+5)-\log_3(x-1)=1\).

The domain requires \(x>1\). The quotient rule gives \((x+5)/(x-1)=3\). Then \(x+5=3x-3\) and \(x=4\). The original arguments are \(9\) and \(3\), giving \(2-1=1\).

Solve \(2\log_5 x=\log_5(3x+4)\).

Both arguments must be positive, so \(x>0\). Move the coefficient to an exponent and equate arguments, giving \(x^2=3x+4\). The factors \((x-4)(x+1)\) give \(4\) and \(-1\). Only \(4\) works.

Solve \(\ln(2x+1)=-2\) exactly.

The domain is \(x>-1/2\). Exponential form gives \(2x+1=e^{-2}\), so \(x=(e^{-2}-1)/2\). Its argument is \(e^{-2}>0\).

Solve \(\log_2(x+2)=\log_2(1-x)\) and verify the domain.

Both arguments are positive on \((-2,1)\). Equal log outputs of the same base have equal arguments. Then \(x+2=1-x\) and \(x=-1/2\). Both arguments become \(3/2\).
