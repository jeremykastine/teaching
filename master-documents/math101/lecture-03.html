Lecture 3: Transformations of y = |x| and y = x2

Assessment coverage Test 1

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

The absolute value graph and the quadratic graph each have a vertex. Track the vertex and points one unit to its left and right to compare the shapes.

Key definitions

For \(y=a|x-h|+k\) or \(y=a(x-h)^2+k\) with \(a\ne0\), \((h,k)\) is the vertex and \(x=h\) is the axis of symmetry. The sign of \(a\) gives the opening direction. Its magnitude sets the vertical scale.

Sketch \(y=|x-2|+1\) and give the vertex, axis, domain, and range.

Shift the V right \(2\) and up \(1\). The vertex is \((2,1)\), the axis is \(x=2\), and the nearby points \((1,2),(3,2)\) lie on its arms. The domain is all real numbers and the range is \([1,\infty)\).

Sketch \(y=-2(x+1)^2+3\) and give the vertex, axis, domain, and range.

The vertex is \((-1,3)\) and the axis is \(x=-1\). The original points \((\pm1,1)\) move to \((-2,1),(0,1)\). The parabola opens down and is vertically stretched by \(2\). The domain is all real numbers and the range is \((-\infty,3]\).

Compare \(y=\frac12|x+1|-2\) and \(y=\frac12(x+1)^2-2\). Mark their common vertex and compare heights two units from the vertex.

Both have vertex \((-1,-2)\) and axis \(x=-1\). Two units from the axis the V has height \((1/2)(2)-2=-1\), and the parabola has height \((1/2)(4)-2=0\). Each has range \([-2,\infty)\).

Write an equation for the parent V reflected across the x-axis and then shifted right \(3\) and up \(2\). Give its range.

Reflection negates each height, then the translations move the vertex to \((3,2)\). The equation is \(y=-|x-3|+2\) and the range is \((-\infty,2]\).

The graph of \(y=x^2\) is stretched horizontally by \(2\) and shifted down \(3\). Write its new equation and track \((2,4)\).

Replace \(x\) with \(x/2\) and subtract \(3\). The equation is \(y=(x/2)^2-3\). The point becomes \((4,1)\).

Algebra practice

Expand \((2x-3)(x+4)\) and recover the original factors by checking the two cross terms.

The four products are \(2x^2,8x,-3x,-12\). Thus the expansion is \(2x^2+5x-12\). Leading factors \(2x,x\) with constants \(-3,4\) give cross terms \(8x\) and \(-3x\), which sum to \(5x\).
