Lecture 14: Exponential and Logarithmic Graphs

Assessment coverage Test 3

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Make a short table of exact exponential values, then swap the coordinates to graph the corresponding logarithm.

Key definitions

For \(y=ab^x\) with \(a>0\) and \(b>0\), \(b\ne1\), the output at \(x=0\) is \(a\). Each unit increase in \(x\) multiplies the output by \(b\). The equation \(y=\log_b x\) asks what power of \(b\) equals \(x\).

Sketch \(y=2^x\) using inputs \(-2,-1,0,1,2\). Give the domain, range, and asymptote.

The outputs are \(1/4,1/2,1,2,4\). Each step doubles the output. The domain is all real numbers, the range is \((0,\infty)\), and the left tail approaches \(y=0\) from above.

Sketch \(y=3(1/2)^x\) using inputs \(-1,0,1,2\). Identify the initial value and decay factor.

The outputs are \(6,3,3/2,3/4\). The initial value is \(3\) and the output halves with each unit step right. The right tail approaches \(y=0\). The domain is all real numbers and the range is positive.

Sketch \(y=\log_2 x\) by reflecting \(y=2^x\) across \(y=x\). Give the domain, range, and asymptote.

Swap coordinates to get \((1/4,-2),(1/2,-1),(1,0),(2,1),(4,2)\). The exponential’s range becomes the logarithm’s domain \((0,\infty)\), and its domain becomes the logarithm’s range, all real numbers. The asymptote reflects to \(x=0\).

Sketch \(y=\log_{1/3}x\) by reflecting points from \(y=(1/3)^x\). Give its direction and asymptote.

On the exponential, inputs \(-1,0,1\) give \(3,1,1/3\). Swapping coordinates gives \((3,-1),(1,0),(1/3,1)\). The logarithm decreases and tends to \(+\infty\) as \(x\) approaches \(0\) from the right.

Compare \(y=2^x\) and \(y=4\cdot2^x\). Give the two initial values and the common multiplier.

Both multiply their output by \(2\) when \(x\) increases by \(1\). At \(x=0\), their outputs are \(1\) and \(4\). The graph of the second has four times the height of the first at every input. Both approach \(y=0\) on the left.

State whether each of \(y=3^x\), \(y=(1/3)^x\), \(y=\log_3 x\), and \(y=\log_{1/3}x\) increases or decreases.

The exponential with base \(3\) increases and the exponential with base \(1/3\) decreases. Reflecting across \(y=x\) preserves the direction of a one-to-one monotone graph. Thus \(\log_3 x\) increases and \(\log_{1/3}x\) decreases.
