Lecture 7: Quadratic Forms, Vertices, and Extrema

Assessment coverage Test 2

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Rewriting a quadratic in vertex form makes its lowest or highest point visible. Each algebraic form describes the same graph.

Key definitions

Standard form is \(ax^2+bx+c\). Vertex form is \(a(x-h)^2+k\), with vertex \((h,k)\). To complete a square in a monic expression, take half the linear coefficient and square it. Add and subtract that same number to preserve the expression.

Expand \(y=2(x-3)^2-5\) and give the minimum and where it occurs.

Expand the square as \(x^2-6x+9\), then multiply by \(2\) and subtract \(5\).

\[y=2(x-3)^2-5=2x^2-12x+13\]

The minimum is \(-5\) at \(x=3\), where the square is zero.

Rewrite \(y=x^2+8x+11\) in vertex form and give the vertex and range.

Half of \(8\) is \(4\), so add and subtract \(16\).

\[y=x^2+8x+16-16+11=(x+4)^2-5\]

The vertex is \((-4,-5)\) and the range is \([-5,\infty)\).

Rewrite \(y=2x^2-12x+7\) in vertex form.

Take \(2\) out of the two terms containing \(x\). Half of \(-6\) is \(-3\), and its square is \(9\).

\[y=2(x^2-6x+9-9)+7=2(x-3)^2-11\]

Find the maximum value of \(y=-3x^2-12x+1\) and the input that gives it.

Take out \(-3\) from the variable terms and complete the square inside. The adjustment \(-4\) inside contributes \(12\) outside.

\[y=-3(x^2+4x+4-4)+1=-3(x+2)^2+13\]

The highest value is \(13\) at \(x=-2\).

Complete the square for \(y=x^2-3x+1\). Give the exact vertex.

Half of \(-3\) is \(-3/2\). Add and subtract \(9/4\).

\[y=\left(x-\frac32\right)^2-\frac94+1=\left(x-\frac32\right)^2-\frac54\]

The vertex is \((3/2,-5/4)\).

Use vertex form to sketch \(y=-x^2+4x+1\) and identify its increasing and decreasing intervals.

Complete the square to get \(y=-(x-2)^2+5\). The graph rises up to \((2,5)\) and falls after it. It is increasing on \((-\infty,2)\) and decreasing on \((2,\infty)\).
