Lecture 5: Transformations of y = x3 and y = ∛x

Assessment coverage Test 1

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Track the point that starts at the origin. A few exact points on either side show how the cubic and cube-root curves are shaped.

Key definitions

A real cube root reverses cubing. Swapping coordinates reflects a graph across \(y=x\). Both parent curves accept and produce every real number.

Sketch \(y=x^3\) and \(y=\sqrt[3]{x}\) using five corresponding points and explain how to get the second graph from the first.

On the cubic use \((-2,-8),(-1,-1),(0,0),(1,1),(2,8)\). Swap coordinates to get \((-8,-2),(-1,-1),(0,0),(1,1),(8,2)\) on the cube-root graph. The graphs reflect across \(y=x\).

Sketch \(y=-(x-1)^3+2\) and track the original points with x-coordinates \(-1,0,1\).

Move right \(1\), negate the original heights, and add \(2\). The three points become \((0,3),(1,2),(2,1)\). The graph decreases, and its domain and range are both all real numbers.

Sketch \(y=2\sqrt[3]{x+2}-1\) using three points.

The origin goes to \((-2,-1)\). The points \((-1,-1),(1,1)\) become \((-3,-3),(-1,1)\). The graph increases and extends without a domain or range endpoint.

Sketch \(y=(x/2)^3-1\) and give the images of \((0,0)\) and \((1,1)\).

Double each x-coordinate and subtract \(1\) from each y-coordinate. The images are \((0,-1)\) and \((2,0)\). The graph is the horizontally stretched cubic shifted down.

Describe how the graph of \(y=\sqrt[3]{-x}\) differs from \(y=\sqrt[3]{x}\). Compare it with \(y=-\sqrt[3]{x}\).

Replacing \(x\) by \(-x\) reflects the curve across the y-axis. Since opposite inputs have opposite cube roots, this produces the same decreasing graph as reflecting the output across the x-axis.

Algebra practice

Factor \(x^2-x-12\) and explain how the signs determine the factors.

The constant is negative, so the constants in the factors have opposite signs. The pair \(-4,3\) multiplies to \(-12\) and adds to \(-1\). The factors are \((x-4)(x+3)\).
