Lecture 18: Combining and Composing Functions

Assessment coverage Test 4

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Combining functions means either doing arithmetic on two outputs or feeding one output into the other rule. Determine which inputs can make it through the entire calculation.

Key definitions

For arithmetic combinations, start with inputs allowed by both functions, and exclude zeros of a divisor for quotients. For \((f\circ g)(x)=f(g(x))\), the input must be allowed by \(g\) and its output must be allowed by \(f\).

Let \(f(x)=2x-1\) and \(g(x)=x^2+3\). Find \((f+g)(x)\) and \((fg)(x)\).

Add or multiply outputs at the same input. The sum is \(x^2+2x+2\). The product is \((2x-1)(x^2+3)=2x^3-x^2+6x-3\). Both domains are all real numbers.

Let \(f(x)=x+2\) and \(g(x)=x^2-4\). Find and simplify \((f/g)(x)\) with its domain.

The denominator vanishes at \(-2\) and \(2\). Factor and cancel on the original domain.

\[(f/g)(x)=\frac{x+2}{(x-2)(x+2)}=\frac1{x-2},\qquad x\ne-2,2\]

Let \(f(x)=2x-1\) and \(g(x)=x^2+3\). Find \(f(g(x))\) and \(g(f(x))\).

Substituting the complete inner expression gives \(f(g(x))=2(x^2+3)-1=2x^2+5\). Reversing the order gives \(g(f(x))=(2x-1)^2+3=4x^2-4x+4\). Both are defined on all real inputs.

Let \(f(t)=\sqrt t\) and \(g(x)=5-2x\). Find \(f(g(x))\) and its domain.

The outer square root receives \(5-2x\). It requires \(5-2x\ge0\), so \(f(g(x))=\sqrt{5-2x}\) has domain \((-\infty,5/2]\).

Let \(f(t)=1/(t-2)\) and \(g(x)=\sqrt{x+1}\). Find \(f(g(x))\) and its domain.

The inner rule requires \(x\ge-1\). The outer rule excludes \(\sqrt{x+1}=2\), which occurs at \(x=3\). The composition is \(1/(\sqrt{x+1}-2)\) and its domain is \([-1,3)\cup(3,\infty)\).

Use the table to find \(f(g(2))\) and determine whether \(g(f(2))\) is defined by the table.

\[\begin{array}{c|rrr}x&0&1&2\\\hline f(x)&2&1&3\\g(x)&1&0&0\end{array}\]

The inner output \(g(2)=0\) leads to \(f(0)=2\). In the other order, \(f(2)=3\), but \(3\) is outside the displayed domain of \(g\). Thus \(g(f(2))\) is undefined for the functions given by the table.
