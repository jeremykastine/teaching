Lecture 4: Transformations of y = √x

Assessment coverage Test 1

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Track the endpoint of the square-root graph. Then use points with square inputs to see which way the curve extends.

Key definitions

The principal square root is nonnegative. The parent graph \(y=\sqrt{x}\) starts at \((0,0)\) and extends right and up. Negating the input reflects across the y-axis. Negating the output reflects across the x-axis.

Sketch \(y=\sqrt{x}\) using \(x=0,1,4,9\). Give its domain and range.

Plot \((0,0),(1,1),(4,2),(9,3)\) and draw the smooth curve through them. Both domain and range are \([0,\infty)\).

Sketch and compare \(y=\sqrt{x-2}\) and \(y=\sqrt{2-x}\). Give the domain and range of each.

Both start at \((2,0)\). The first extends right through \((3,1),(6,2)\) and has domain \([2,\infty)\). The second extends left through \((1,1),(-2,2)\) and has domain \((-\infty,2]\). Both ranges are \([0,\infty)\).

Sketch \(y=-\sqrt{x+1}+2\). Give the endpoint, domain, and range.

Shift the starting point to \((-1,2)\) and reflect the heights so the curve extends right and down. The points with horizontal offsets \(1\) and \(4\) are \((0,1)\) and \((3,0)\). The domain is \([-1,\infty)\) and the range is \((-\infty,2]\).

Sketch \(y=2\sqrt{3-x}-1\) and list three points, including the endpoint.

The endpoint is \((3,-1)\). The parent points \((1,1),(4,2)\) move to \((2,1),(-1,3)\). It extends left and up, with domain \((-\infty,3]\) and range \([-1,\infty)\).

Write an equation for the parent square-root graph shifted left \(2\) and up \(4\) and stretched vertically by \(3\).

Multiply the parent output by \(3\), then add \(4\). The left shift replaces \(x\) by \(x+2\). The equation is \(y=3\sqrt{x+2}+4\).

Algebra practice

Factor \(12x^3-18x^2\) completely and verify by multiplying.

Both coefficients are divisible by \(6\) and both terms contain \(x^2\). The factorization is \(6x^2(2x-3)\). Distributing gives the original two terms.
