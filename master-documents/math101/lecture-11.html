Lecture 11: Polynomial End Behavior and Turning Points

Assessment coverage Test 2

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Use the leading term for the tails and a sign chart for the parts between intercepts. The sketch can show where a turning point must occur even without its exact coordinates.

Key definitions

End behavior describes what happens as \(x\) goes far left or far right. A local maximum or minimum is a highest or lowest value compared with nearby points.

Give the end behavior of \(y=-2x^5+6x^2+1\).

The leading term is \(-2x^5\). The odd power keeps the sign of \(x\), and the negative coefficient reverses it. The left end rises toward \(+\infty\) and the right falls toward \(-\infty\).

Give the end behavior of \(y=3x^4-x^3-20\) and \(y=-3x^4+x^3+20\).

The leading terms are \(3x^4\) and \(-3x^4\). The fourth power is positive at large inputs of either sign. Both ends of the first rise. Both ends of the second fall.

Sketch \(y=(x+2)x(x-3)\) using signs on the four intervals. Where must there be a local high point or low point?

From left to right the signs are negative, positive, negative, positive. The leading term \(x^3\) gives a falling left tail and rising right tail. Between \(-2\) and \(0\) the graph goes above zero then returns to zero, so it reaches a local maximum in that interval. Between \(0\) and \(3\) it goes below zero then returns, so it reaches a local minimum there.

Sketch \(y=-(x+1)^2(x-2)^2\). Use the signs and zeros to explain where local extrema must occur.

The leading term is \(-x^4\), so both tails fall. The graph is negative except at \(-1\) and \(2\). Both zeros are local maxima at height zero. The graph lies below zero between them and must reach a local minimum there.

Could a degree-four polynomial with positive leading coefficient have a graph whose left tail rises and whose right tail falls? Explain.

Both tails rise because the even power is positive far in either direction and the coefficient is positive. The described tails would require odd degree with negative leading coefficient.

Give a polynomial of smallest degree whose tails both rise, whose graph touches at \(x=-2\), and whose graph crosses at \(x=1\) and \(x=3\).

The multiplicities must be at least \(2,1,1\), respectively. Taking those exponents gives \(y=(x+2)^2(x-1)(x-3)\). Its leading term \(x^4\) gives two rising tails. The signs are positive to the left of \(1\) except at the touch, negative between \(1\) and \(3\), and positive to the right of \(3\).
