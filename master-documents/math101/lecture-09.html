Lecture 9: Polynomial Division

Assessment coverage Test 2

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Divide by repeatedly rewriting the dividend as a convenient multiple of the divisor plus what remains. Choose each multiple to remove the leading term of the current remainder.

Key definitions

For dividend \(P\), divisor \(D\), quotient \(Q\), and remainder \(R\), the identity is \(P=DQ+R\). Stop when the remainder is zero or its degree is smaller than the divisor’s degree.

Rewrite \(157\) as a multiple of \(12\) plus a remainder less than \(12\).

Start with the easy multiple \(120\), leaving \(37\). Recognize \(36\) as another multiple of \(12\).

\[157=12(10)+37=12(10)+12(3)+1=12(13)+1\]

The quotient is \(13\) and the remainder is \(1\).

Divide \(x^2+5x+6\) by \(x+2\) and check.

To account for \(x^2\), start with \((x+2)x\). Subtracting it leaves \(3x+6\), which is exactly \((x+2)3\).

\[x^2+5x+6=(x+2)x+(3x+6)=(x+2)(x+3)\]

Multiplying back gives the dividend. The remainder is zero.

Divide \(2x^3-x+4\) by \(x-1\).

Treat the missing \(x^2\) term as \(0x^2\). Account for the leading terms in turn with \(2x^2\), \(2x\), and \(1\) times the divisor.

\[\begin{aligned}2x^3-x+4&=(x-1)2x^2+(2x^2-x+4)\\&=(x-1)(2x^2+2x)+(x+4)\\&=(x-1)(2x^2+2x+1)+5.\end{aligned}\]

The remainder is \(5\). Multiplying the quotient by \(x-1\) gives \(2x^3-x-1\), which checks after adding \(5\).

Divide \(x^4+2x^3+3x+1\) by \(x^2+1\).

Use \(x^2\) first and \(2x\) next. After those two rewrites the remainder is \(-x^2+x+1\), so use \(-1\) to remove its leading term.

\[\begin{aligned}x^4+2x^3+3x+1&=(x^2+1)x^2+(2x^3-x^2+3x+1)\\&=(x^2+1)(x^2+2x)+(-x^2+x+1)\\&=(x^2+1)(x^2+2x-1)+(x+2).\end{aligned}\]

The quotient is \(x^2+2x-1\) and the remainder is \(x+2\). The latter has degree \(1\), so the process stops.

Divide \(3x^2+4x-1\) by \(x+1\). Check the quotient and remainder by multiplying and adding.

Start with \(3x(x+1)\), leaving \(x-1\). Use \((x+1)\) once more, leaving \(-2\).

\[3x^2+4x-1=(x+1)(3x+1)-2\]

Expansion gives \(3x^2+4x+1-2=3x^2+4x-1\).

Determine whether \(x-2\) divides \(x^3-3x^2+4\) exactly and give the quotient if it does.

The first multiple is \(x^2(x-2)\), leaving \(-x^2+4\). Recognize that remainder as \(-(x-2)(x+2)\).

\[x^3-3x^2+4=(x-2)x^2-(x-2)(x+2)=(x-2)(x^2-x-2)\]

The remainder is zero, and the quotient is \(x^2-x-2\).
