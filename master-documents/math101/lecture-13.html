Lecture 13: Rational Functions — End Behavior and Asymptotes

Assessment coverage Test 3

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Compare the numerator and denominator degrees to predict the tails. Then use a quotient and remainder to find a slant asymptote when the degree difference is one.

Key definitions

For a rational function, a horizontal asymptote is a finite height approached for large positive or negative inputs. When the numerator degree is smaller, that height is zero. When the degrees match, it is the ratio of leading coefficients. When the numerator degree is one larger, the linear quotient gives a slant asymptote.

Find the horizontal asymptote of \(y=\frac{4x-3}{2x^2+1}\). Say whether each tail is above or below it.

Divide top and bottom by \(x^2\). The numerator tends to \(0\) and the denominator to \(2\), so \(y=0\). The denominator is positive. The left tail is negative and the right tail positive.

Find the horizontal asymptote of \(y=\frac{6x^2+1}{-3x^2+x-2}\).

Divide by \(x^2\) and keep the terms that do not approach zero. The ratio approaches \(6/(-3)=-2\), so the asymptote is \(y=-2\).

Find the slant asymptote of \(y=\frac{x^2+1}{x-1}\). State the vertical asymptote and locate the tails relative to the slant line.

Rewrite the dividend as \((x-1)(x+1)+2\).

\[y=x+1+\frac2{x-1},\qquad x\ne1\]

The remainder term tends to zero, so the slant asymptote is \(y=x+1\). The vertical asymptote is \(x=1\). The graph lies below the slant line to the left of \(1\) and above it to the right.

Sketch \(y=\frac{2x+2}{x-1}\) using asymptotes and both intercepts.

Write \(y=2+4/(x-1)\). The asymptotes are \(x=1,y=2\). The intercepts are \((-1,0)\) and \((0,-2)\). The left branch lies below \(y=2\) and tends to \(-\infty\) at \(1\) from the left. The right branch stays above \(2\) and tends to \(+\infty\) from the right.

Determine whether \(y=\frac{x^3+1}{x+2}\) has a horizontal or slant asymptote.

The numerator degree is \(3\) and the denominator degree is \(1\). The quotient starts with \(x^2\) and has degree \(2\). The remainder term tends to zero, so the end behavior follows a quadratic. There is no horizontal or linear slant asymptote.

Analyze and sketch \(y=\frac{x^2-4}{x^2-x-2}\), including the hole, intercepts, and asymptotes.

Exclude \(-1,2\). The reduced expression is \((x+2)/(x+1)=1+1/(x+1)\). The hole is \((2,4/3)\) and the intercepts are \((-2,0)\) and \((0,2)\). The asymptotes are \(x=-1,y=1\). The left branch lies below \(1\) and the right branch above \(1\).
