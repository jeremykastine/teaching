Lecture 15: Exponential Functions and Equations

Assessment coverage Test 3

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Isolate the power first. Match bases when convenient, or use a logarithm to name the exponent exactly.

Key definitions

For \(a>0\), \(b>0\), \(b\ne1\), the graph of \(y=ab^x\) is one-to-one. A horizontal line at a positive height meets it once. A nonpositive height is outside its range.

For \(y=4\cdot3^x\), give the initial value and the multiplier. Find the input where the height is \(36\).

At \(0\) the height is \(4\), and one step right triples it. The equation \(4\cdot3^x=36\) gives \(3^x=9=3^2\), so the graph reaches height \(36\) at \(x=2\).

Solve \(5(1/2)^x=40\) exactly.

Divide by \(5\) to get \((1/2)^x=8=(1/2)^{-3}\). Thus \(x=-3\). A decay graph reaches this larger height to the left of its initial point.

Solve \(2^{3x-1}=16\).

Write \(16=2^4\). Equate exponents to obtain \(3x-1=4\), hence \(x=5/3\).

Solve \(3\cdot2^x=21\) exactly.

Divide by \(3\) to get \(2^x=7\). The exponent is \(x=\log_2 7\), equivalently \(\ln7/\ln2\). Since \(2^2<7<2^3\), the solution lies between \(2\) and \(3\).

Solve \(7(3/4)^{2x}=2\) exactly.

Divide by \(7\) and take the natural logarithm of both positive sides.

\[2x\ln(3/4)=\ln(2/7)\]

Divide by \(2\ln(3/4)\) to get \(x=\ln(2/7)/(2\ln(3/4))\). Both logarithms in the ratio are negative, so the input is positive.

Solve \(4\cdot3^x=-2\) over the reals. Explain using the graph.

Since \(3^x\) is positive for every real input, the left side is positive. The horizontal line \(y=-2\) has no intersection with the graph.
