Lecture 12: Rational Functions — Zeros, Holes, and Vertical Asymptotes

Assessment coverage Test 3

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Factor and record the original restrictions. The simplified expression shows what happens near each excluded input, while the original domain tells which points belong to the graph.

Key definitions

A hole is a missing point at an excluded input where the simplified expression has a finite value. A vertical asymptote occurs where the magnitude grows without bound on at least one side. A zero is an allowed input whose output is zero.

Find the domain, zero, hole, and vertical asymptote of \(y=\frac{(x-2)(x+1)}{(x-2)(x-4)}\).

Exclude \(2\) and \(4\) from the original denominator. For allowed inputs, \(y=(x+1)/(x-4)\). The zero is \(-1\). The missing height at \(2\) is \(-3/2\), giving a hole at \((2,-3/2)\). The remaining denominator gives a vertical asymptote at \(x=4\).

Factor \(y=\frac{x^2-9}{x^2-x-6}\) and find the zeros, holes, and vertical asymptotes.

The original denominator is \((x-3)(x+2)\), so exclude \(3,-2\). The numerator is \((x-3)(x+3)\). Canceling gives \((x+3)/(x+2)\). The zero is \(-3\), the hole is \((3,6/5)\), and the vertical asymptote is \(x=-2\).

Analyze \(y=\frac{x-1}{(x-1)^2}\). Decide whether the graph has a hole or vertical asymptote at \(1\) and give both one-sided behaviors.

For \(x\ne1\), the expression becomes \(1/(x-1)\). A denominator factor remains, so \(x=1\) is an asymptote. The output tends to \(-\infty\) on the left and \(+\infty\) on the right. There are no zeros.

Find the hole and any zeros of \(y=\frac{(x+2)^2}{x+2}\).

For \(x\ne-2\) the equation gives \(y=x+2\). The missing point is \((-2,0)\). The only input that would make the simplified expression zero is excluded, so the original has no zero.

Find the one-sided behavior at \(x=2\) for \(y=-\frac{3}{(x-2)^2}\).

The denominator is positive and approaches zero from both sides. The numerator stays negative, so both sides tend to \(-\infty\).

Find the zeros and vertical asymptotes of \(y=\frac{x(x-3)}{(x+1)(x-2)}\). Determine the sign on each side of \(x=2\) near the asymptote.

The zeros are \(0\) and \(3\), and the asymptotes are \(x=-1,2\). Near \(2\) the numerator is negative and \(x+1\) is positive. Just left of \(2\), \(x-2\) is negative and the quotient tends to \(+\infty\). Just right of \(2\), it tends to \(-\infty\).
