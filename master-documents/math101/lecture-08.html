Lecture 8: Quadratic Equations and x-Intercepts

Assessment coverage Test 2

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Set the output to zero to find the x-intercepts. Use the form of the equation to choose a solving method.

Key definitions

The real solutions of \(ax^2+bx+c=0\) are the x-coordinates of the x-intercepts. The discriminant \(b^2-4ac\) determines whether the quadratic formula gives two, one, or zero distinct real solutions.

Find the x-intercepts of \(y=2x^2-7x+3\) by factoring.

Set \(y=0\). The factorization \((2x-1)(x-3)\) gives \(x=1/2\) or \(x=3\) by the zero-product property. The intercepts are \((1/2,0)\) and \((3,0)\).

Find the x-intercepts of \(y=3(x+2)^2-27\) by taking square roots.

Set the output to zero, giving \((x+2)^2=9\). Taking principal square roots gives \(|x+2|=3\), so \(x=1\) or \(x=-5\). The intercepts are \((1,0)\) and \((-5,0)\).

Find the exact x-intercepts of \(y=2x^2+4x-3\) by the quadratic formula.

Here \(a=2,b=4,c=-3\). The discriminant is \(16+24=40\) and \(\sqrt{40}=2\sqrt{10}\).

Use the discriminant to predict how \(y=4x^2-12x+9\) meets the x-axis.

The discriminant is \(144-144=0\). The graph touches at the one intercept \((3/2,0)\), since the expression is \((2x-3)^2\).

Determine whether \(y=x^2+2x+5\) has a real x-intercept. Explain from the discriminant and the vertex.

The discriminant is \(4-20=-16\), so there is no real intercept. Vertex form is \((x+1)^2+4\). Its minimum height is \(4\).

Find the x-intercepts of \(y=x^2-6x+5\) and compare them with the vertex.

Factor as \((x-1)(x-5)\), giving intercepts \((1,0)\) and \((5,0)\). Their midpoint has \(x=3\), the axis of symmetry. Rewriting as \((x-3)^2-4\) gives vertex \((3,-4)\).
