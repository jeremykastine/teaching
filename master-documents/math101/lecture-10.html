Lecture 10: Polynomial Zeros and Multiplicity

Assessment coverage Test 2

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

A real zero is an input where the output is zero. Factored form shows where the graph meets the axis and what it does nearby.

Key definitions

If \(r\) is a zero of a polynomial, \(x-r\) divides it exactly. The multiplicity of \(r\) is the number of copies of that factor. An odd multiplicity changes the sign across the zero. An even multiplicity keeps the same sign.

Find the real zeros and multiplicities of \(y=2(x+1)^2(x-3)\). State whether the graph crosses or touches at each.

The roots are \(-1\) with multiplicity \(2\) and \(3\) with multiplicity \(1\). The square factor keeps its sign on the two sides of \(-1\), so the graph touches there. The linear factor changes sign at \(3\), so the graph crosses there.

Find the real zeros of \(y=x^4-5x^2+4\) and their multiplicities.

Factor using \(x^2\) as the repeated expression.

\[x^4-5x^2+4=(x^2-1)(x^2-4)=(x-1)(x+1)(x-2)(x+2)\]

The zeros \(-2,-1,1,2\) are all simple, so the graph crosses at each.

Given the zero \(2\) of \(y=x^3-4x^2+x+6\), find the other zeros.

The multiple \(x^2(x-2)\) leaves \(-2x^2+x+6\). The next multiple \(-2x(x-2)\) leaves \(-3x+6=-3(x-2)\). The quotient is \(x^2-2x-3=(x-3)(x+1)\). The other zeros are \(3\) and \(-1\).

Find the real zeros and multiplicities of \(y=x^3+4x\).

Factoring gives \(x(x^2+4)\). The second factor is positive for all real \(x\). The only real zero is \(0\), with multiplicity \(1\).

Write a lowest-degree polynomial that crosses at \(x=-2\), touches at \(x=1\), and passes through \((0,4)\).

Use one factor \(x+2\) and two copies of \(x-1\). Write \(y=a(x+2)(x-1)^2\). At \(x=0\), the output is \(2a\), so \(a=2\).

Compare the intercepts and local behavior of \(y=(x-1)^2\) and \(y=(x-1)^3\).

Both have intercept \((1,0)\). For the square, the heights are positive to both sides, so it touches and turns. For the cube, the heights change from negative to positive, so it crosses even though the curve flattens near the intercept.
