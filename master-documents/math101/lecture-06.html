Lecture 6: Transformations of y = 1/x and y = 1/x2

Assessment coverage Test 1

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Track the two asymptotes as the reciprocal graphs move. Then track a point on each side to decide where the branches lie.

Key definitions

An asymptote describes how the graph approaches a line. The parent graphs \(y=1/x\) and \(y=1/x^2\) have asymptotes \(x=0\) and \(y=0\). For the first, the two branches have opposite signs. For the second, both are positive.

Compare the domains, ranges, and branch locations of \(y=1/x\) and \(y=1/x^2\).

Both domains exclude \(0\). The first has branches in quadrants I and III, so its range excludes just \(0\). The second is positive on both sides, so its range is \((0,\infty)\).

Sketch \(y=\frac{2}{x-1}+3\). Give the asymptotes, domain, and range.

Translate the vertical asymptote to \(x=1\) and the horizontal asymptote to \(y=3\). The parent points \((1,1),(-1,-1)\) become \((2,5),(0,1)\). The domain excludes \(1\) and the range excludes \(3\).

Sketch \(y=1-\frac1{(x+2)^2}\). Give the asymptotes, domain, and range.

Shift left \(2\), reflect the heights, and shift up \(1\). Both branches lie below \(y=1\), and the vertical asymptote is \(x=-2\). The domain is \((-\infty,-2)\cup(-2,\infty)\) and the range is \((-\infty,1)\).

For \(y=-\frac3{x+1}-2\), identify the asymptotes and state which branch is above the horizontal asymptote.

The asymptotes are \(x=-1\) and \(y=-2\). The point \((-1,-1)\) on the parent maps to \((-2,1)\), so the left branch is above \(y=-2\). The right branch is below.

Write an equation obtained from \(y=1/x^2\) with vertical asymptote \(x=3\), horizontal asymptote \(y=-2\), and both branches above \(y=-2\).

A right shift of \(3\) and a downward shift of \(2\) give \(y=1/(x-3)^2-2\). Keeping the positive scale keeps both branches above \(-2\).

Algebra practice

Factor \(9x^2-25\) and check the two middle products.

The squares are \((3x)^2\) and \(5^2\), giving \((3x-5)(3x+5)\). The cross terms \(15x\) and \(-15x\) cancel.
