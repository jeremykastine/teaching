Lecture 20: Transformations of a General Function

Assessment coverage Test 4

Double-click or double-tap the space beneath a problem to reveal or hide its solution. With a keyboard, focus that space and press Enter or Space.

Overview

Track points on a graph even when the formula for the original function is unknown. First decide whether the change acts on the input or the output.

Key definitions

If \((u,v)\) belongs to \(y=f(x)\), it becomes \((u+h,v+k)\) for \(f(x-h)+k\), \((u,av)\) for \(af(x)\), and \((u/b,v)\) for \(f(bx)\) when \(b\ne0\). For inside changes, solve for which new input feeds the old input to \(f\).

The only inputs of \(f\) are shown in the table. Give the table, domain, and range of \(y=f(x-3)+2\).

\[\begin{array}{c|rrr}x&-2&0&4\\\hline f(x)&3&-1&2\end{array}\]

Add \(3\) to each input and \(2\) to each output. The new pairs are \((1,5),(3,1),(7,4)\). The domain is \(\{1,3,7\}\) and the range is \(\{1,4,5\}\).

The domain of \(f\) is \([-2,5]\) and the range is \([-3,4]\). Give the domain and range of \(y=-2f(x)\).

The inputs stay the same. The outputs are multiplied by \(-2\), reversing the order of the bounds. The domain is \([-2,5]\) and the range is \([-8,6]\).

A graph of \(f\) has domain \([-2,6)\), range \([0,5]\), and contains \((4,3)\). Find the corresponding point, domain, and range for \(y=f(2x)\).

Halve the horizontal coordinates. The point becomes \((2,3)\) and the domain becomes \([-1,3)\). The range stays \([0,5]\).

The graph of \(f\) below consists of two line segments. Sketch \(y=f(-x)+1\) and give its domain and range.

Reflect the x-coordinates across the y-axis and move the heights up \(1\). The points become \((3,1),(1,4),(-2,0)\). Join them in increasing x-order. The domain is \([-2,3]\) and range is \([0,4]\).

The original graph includes \((4,-2)\) and has domain \([0,6]\) and range \([-2,4]\). Find the corresponding point, domain, and range for \(y=3f(2(x-1))-4\).

For the point, solve \(2(x-1)=4\) to get \(x=3\). The new height is \(3(-2)-4=-10\). The input bounds map by \(x=u/2+1\) to \([1,4]\). The outputs map by \(y=3v-4\) to \([-10,8]\).

A graph includes the point \((2,5)\). Give its image under \(y=f(x)+3\), \(y=f(x+3)\), and \(y=f(-x)\).

The first adds to the output, giving \((2,8)\). The second feeds \(2\) to the old rule when \(x=-1\), giving \((-1,5)\). The last feeds \(2\) to the old rule when \(x=-2\), giving \((-2,5)\).
